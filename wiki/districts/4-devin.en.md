# Devín (Bratislava IV)

Devín is a village at the confluence of the Morava and the Danube beneath the rock of the castle of the same name — the westernmost and most "historical" borough of okres Bratislava IV. Above the houses rises [[Hrad-Devin|Devín Castle (hrad Devín)]] — the ruins of a fortress standing on the site of a Great Moravian stronghold, one of Slovakia's national symbols; today it is a site of the [[Muzeum-mesta-Bratislavy|Bratislava City Museum]]. The border ran along the Morava here: after the Munich Agreement of 1938 Devín went to the German Reich, and in the socialist era the village lived in the dead zone of the [[iron-curtain|Iron Curtain]] — Devín saw both tragedies and legendary escapes to the Austrian bank.

The short "Reich" chapter is recalled by the [[Amfiteater-Devin|amphitheatre below the castle]] — a giant open-air "people's theatre" of the Third Reich, which after the war served all-Slavic rallies and open-air opera. Today Devín is a day-trip place: the castle, the river confluence, cycle paths along the former border strip and the vineyard slopes of Devínska Kobyla.

## Coat of Arms

![Coat of arms of Devín](/assets/erb-4-devin.png)

In a blue field, a walking silver winged ox with a golden halo above a scroll inscribed "S. LVCAS" — the symbol of Luke the Evangelist, patron of the Devín parish.
