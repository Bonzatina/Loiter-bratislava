# Čunovo (Bratislava V)

Čunovo (Hungarian Dunacsún) is Bratislava's southernmost borough, a village on the right bank of the Danube by the Hungarian border (okres Bratislava V). Like its neighbours [[5-rusovce|Rusovce]] and Jarovce, in the 16th century the village took in Croatian settlers fleeing the Ottomans, remained part of Hungary until 1947, when the "Bratislava bridgehead" went to Czechoslovakia under the Paris Peace Treaty, and became part of Bratislava in 1972. Today Čunovo is known above all for water: nearby are the Čunovo dam of the Gabčíkovo waterworks with the Hrušov reservoir, the Divoká voda slalom channel and the Danubiana gallery of modern art on the Danube levee.

The historic heart of the village is the [[Kastiel-Cunovo|Szapáry manor (Szapáryho kaštieľ)]], a Baroque-Classicist estate of the 18th century that the Bratislava Region has turned into the Čunovo Ecocentre.
