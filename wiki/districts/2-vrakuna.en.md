# Vrakuňa (Bratislava II)

Vrakuňa (hist. Verekne, Hung. Pozsony-Vereknye, Ger. Preßburg-Fragendorf) is Bratislava's eastern borough (Bratislava II okres) on both banks of the Little Danube, on the road to Žitný ostrov. The name goes back to a proto-Slavic root meaning "enclosure, wattle fence by the river" — the same base as the Hungarian toponyms Várkony. The village, founded in 1279, was first recorded in 1290 — named after its owner Lörinc Verekenyi; from the end of the 14th century it gradually passed into the city's ownership.

## History and Character

The year 1683 broke the village's history: the army of Sultan Galga — eight thousand horsemen riding through Vrakuňa to Imre Thököly's camp — destroyed both the village and the bridge; the inhabitants hid in the woods of the Danube islands, and only by 1768 did the population return to its former size. In the mid-19th century there were 69 houses and 451 residents, mostly Hungarian farmers; by 1940 the village had grown to 1,232 people. In 1948 Verekne was officially renamed Vrakuňa, on 1 January 1972 the village became part of Bratislava, and since 1990 it has been one of the 17 boroughs: 10.3 km² and more than 18,000 residents.

Today's Vrakuňa is a panel housing estate (Bučinová, Jedľová, Kríková, Poľnohospodárska, Stavbárska streets…) around the pedestrian zone on Poľnohospodárska; there is no industry on its territory, but residents feel the neighbourhood of the Slovnaft refinery, the airport and the city's sewage works. A footbridge over the Little Danube leads to the Vrakuňa forest park, from where a cycle path runs towards the city centre. The era's best-known building is the [[Pentagon]] on Stavbárska — the builders' dormitory of the neighbouring Medzi jarkami estate that became the city's most notorious address.
