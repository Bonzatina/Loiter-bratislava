# Rača (Bratislava III)

Rača is the city's north-eastern borough at the foot of the Little Carpathians (Bratislava III okres), an ancient winegrowing village whose slopes are still covered with vineyards: Rača's Frankovka modrá is one of the great names of Little Carpathian wine, made famous by Maria Theresa herself: in 1767, recovering from a grave illness (she had even received the last rites), the empress credited her recovery to a barrel of Frankovka from the Rača priest Radochányi — the "miraculous" wine never left her cellars afterwards, a donation deed for the red wine sealed its fame, and the priest received in thanks a gold-embroidered vestment which the [[Racianska-pamatna-izba|Rača Memorial Room]] displays to this day. The old core with its winegrowers' houses lives alongside panel estates; a world of its own is the Rendez railway colony (Východné) on the eastern edge, by the Bratislava-East marshalling yard, where the [[Zeleznicne-muzeum-SR|Railway Museum of Slovakia]] works in the historic locomotive depot.
