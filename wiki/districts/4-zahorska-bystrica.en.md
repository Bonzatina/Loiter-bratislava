# Záhorská Bystrica (Bratislava IV)

Záhorská Bystrica (German Bisternitz, Hungarian Pozsonybeszterce) is Bratislava's northernmost borough (okres Bratislava IV): beyond the ridge of the Little Carpathians, already on the Záhorie side, at about 175 metres above sea level. The village is first mentioned on 26 July 1314. Its cadastral area is huge by city standards — 32.3 km², mostly forest; the population is around eight thousand.

The borough has a double character: the surviving peasant houses of the old village stand next to new villas, and the Markíza television company sits on the edge. The parish landmark is the Roman Catholic Church of Saints Peter and Paul (1834) with its baroque rectory; chapels are scattered around the area — St John of Nepomuk (1896), Our Lady of Lourdes (1913), Saints Cosmas and Damian (1839) and St Wendelin. What mostly brings city people here, though, is the Little Carpathian forest: walking and cycling routes start from here, and deep in the woods, halfway to Marianka, stands [[Maly-Slavin|Malý Slavín]].

## Coat of Arms

![Coat of arms of Záhorská Bystrica](/assets/erb-4-zahorska-bystrica.png)

In a red field, Saints Peter and Paul in white robes with golden haloes: Peter with a golden key and a book, Paul with a golden sword and a book. They are the patrons of the local parish — the church of 1834 is dedicated to them.
