# Vajnory (Bratislava III)

Vajnory (German Weinern) is a small borough at the foot of the Little Carpathians — 13.5 km² and a little over five thousand inhabitants, administratively in okres Bratislava III. It sits at 130–133 metres, where the Danubian lowland meets the hills: the north-western part of its cadastre falls within the Little Carpathians protected landscape area, the Šúr national nature reserve of the NATURA 2000 network lies right by its boundary, and the [[Vajnorske-jazero|Vajnory lakes]] to the south-east. The people of Vajnory take justified pride in their history and in the particular beauty of the folk art still kept and developed here.

People lived here as early as the La Tène period, some 2,300 years ago; during motorway construction archaeologists uncovered a Slavic-Avar burial ground with ten skeleton graves and six Slavic urns of ancient form. The village arose as a subject settlement of [[Bratislavsky-hrad|Bratislava Castle]]: its people served the town and supplied their good wine to the royal court. The first written notice comes only in 1237, when the village bore its original Slavic name Prača (Pračany). From 1307, when the monastery of Heiligenkreuz in Austria owned it, the German name Weinern came into use — straight from the chief occupation of its people, viticulture and winemaking; it survives to this day in its Slovakised form. In the 16th century the town bought the village back — so after four centuries Vajnory returned to its founder. It became an independent municipality with the abolition of serfdom in 1851, and a borough of Bratislava only after the Second World War, in 1946.

The centre of Vajnory was declared a heritage zone in 1992. Besides the [[Kostol-Sedembolestnej-Vajnory|church]] and the [[Vajnorsky-ludovy-dom|folk house]] there are chapels of St John of Nepomuk, St Urban and St Wendelin, and a statue of St Florian. A native of Vajnory was Titus Zeman (1915–1969), a Roman Catholic Salesian priest sentenced to 25 years in prison and beatified on 30 September 2017.

## Coat of Arms

![Coat of arms of Vajnory](/assets/erb-3-vajnory.png)

In a red field a silver vintner's knife and a silver bunch of grapes, with two golden rosettes flanking the knife. The subject is direct: the village took the name Weinern from the grape, and the vineyards on its slopes are part of the [[little-carpathian-wine|Little Carpathian region]], where Vajnory counts as a top-category wine community.
