# The Waterworks Museum (Vodárenské múzeum)

The Waterworks Museum (Vodárenské múzeum) of the Bratislava Water Company (BVS) is a specialised technical museum (since 2007) in the protected building of the city's first pumping station on Devínska cesta: the place where, in early February 1886 (on the 1st or the 4th, accounts differ), Bratislava's first waterworks — the third on the territory of Slovakia — went into service. The three-hectare grounds with the Waterworks Garden, one of the city's loveliest, are a favourite of families with children.

![The first pumping station building — now the Waterworks Museum](/assets/vodarenske-muzeum.jpg)

The source was chosen in 1881–1882 by the Dresden building councillor Bernhard Salbach, builder of the waterworks of Budapest, Linz, Vienna and Prague: the island of Sihoť in the Karlova Ves arm of the Danube. The first well — a dug well 250 centimetres across — was excavated on 25 August 1884; its water ran through a 1.7-kilometre pipeline under the Danube arm to the main pumping station in Karlova Ves and was driven by steam pumps across the city to the main reservoir by the castle — a pressure-gravity system, a technological summit of its day. The first main, about 37 kilometres long, served some 17,500 residents; the first well itself worked until the mid-1960s (its watertight steel hatch is raised to the level of the Danube's "hundred-year water", almost five metres above the surrounding terrain), while the island of Sihoť remains an irreplaceable source to this day: some of its structures are a national cultural monument, and the island's northern part holds an outdoor exposition with the first well, the pumping station of 1912 and a walk-through tunnel carrying the pipes under the Karlova Ves arm (excursions to the island itself, however, are suspended until further notice).

The museum's exhibition is split between the history and the present of Bratislava's water management — from wooden pipes to today's 3,200 kilometres of network: four large models of the historic structures, virtual reconstructions, LED panels with short films, original fittings of 1912 in the basement, a "waterman's room" and a projection hall, and among the exhibits even a fragment of water piping from the gardens of Versailles. Since 2019 there has also been an outdoor display of waterworks machinery with a natural pond, a wooden pier, drinking fountains and a small amphitheatre.

## Practical Information

In summer the museum is open on weekdays from 9:00 to 14:00, entry free (no guided tours); the grounds with the garden are open daily from 6:00 to 21:00. In summer the Waterworks Garden hosts the free classes of the "Karlova Ves cultural and sporting summer" — from zumba and yoga to tai chi.
