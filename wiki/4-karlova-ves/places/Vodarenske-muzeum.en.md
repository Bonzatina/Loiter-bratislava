# The Waterworks Museum (Vodárenské múzeum)

The Waterworks Museum (Vodárenské múzeum) of the Bratislava Water Company (BVS) occupies the protected building of the city's first pumping station on Devínska cesta — the place where, on 4 February 1886, the history of Bratislava's water supply began. The first municipal water main, about 37 kilometres long, served some 17,500 residents: the water came from a well on the island of Sihoť, passed under a Danube branch to the main pumping station in Karlova Ves, and continued along the Danube embankment to Rybné námestie.

![The first pumping station building — now the Waterworks Museum](/assets/vodarenske-muzeum.jpg)

For its time the system was a technological summit — steam pumps, an electric engine room, modern piping; part of the original equipment survives, and some of it still works. The exhibition tells a century and a half of the city's water management — from wooden pipes to today's 3,200 kilometres of network. Most remarkable of all: the Sihoť source is irreplaceable even now — the island still waters Bratislava.
