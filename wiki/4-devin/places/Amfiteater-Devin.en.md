# The Amphitheatre below Devín Castle (Amfiteáter Devín)

The amphitheatre below Devín Castle is the city's most uncomfortable heritage: a giant open-air theatre begun by the Third Reich. After the Munich Agreement of 1938 Devín went to Germany, and on the steep southern slope beneath the castle ruins a Freilichtbühne was laid out — a venue for mass propaganda spectacles with the most "authentic" natural scenery, of the kind the Reich liked to build across the country. Terraces for more than a hundred thousand spectators were cut into the concave slope, mercilessly destroying the remains of the medieval fortress walls (blown up back in 1809); by the water's edge rose a three-storey building whose reinforced-concrete roof serves as the stage. The basic structure was finished at the very end of the war, and the building was completed in 1948 to a design by Václav Houdek.

![All-Slavic Day at the amphitheatre, 5 July 1945](/assets/amfiteater-devin.jpg)

The amphitheatre never got to serve Nazi propaganda — on 5 July 1945 it was filled by the first All-Slavic Day, and the terraces built for "the superiority of the German nation" hosted a celebration of Slavic solidarity instead. Later, operas sounded from the roof-stage — Krútňava, Svätopluk; the building held a restaurant and dressing rooms. After 1989 a sad chapter began: the city museum leased the building rent-free for 99 years to the Stockholm firm Seafly, the contract was declared unlawful, and the courts dragged on from 2008 up to the Supreme Court; only in December 2023 did the city councillors approve a settlement giving a chance to return the decaying structure to the city and breathe life into it. For now the peeling "railway station on the Danube bank", as the historian Holčík quipped, remains perhaps the only surviving Third Reich structure in Slovakia.
