# Gate of Freedom (Brána slobody)

The Gate of Freedom (Brána slobody) is a memorial to the victims of the communist regime at the confluence of the Morava and the Danube, right below [[Hrad-Devin|Devín Castle]], where the strip of the [[iron-curtain|Iron Curtain]] ran along the river. The monument by sculptor Peter Mészároš was unveiled on 17 November 2005, the Day of the Struggle for Freedom and Democracy: a concrete gate — a "triumphal arch" — with a steel grille bent outwards, the barrier overcome on the way to freedom. The leaf of the gate is "shot through" — traces of the shots fired into the backs of those leaving — and on the inner side, beneath the bent-out grille, are carved the names of some four hundred women and men who died attempting to cross the Iron Curtain in 1945–1989.

![Gate of Freedom below Devín (Brána slobody)](/assets/brana-slobody.jpg)

The memorial is cast from the highest-grade concrete, the kind used for nuclear power stations and dams, and weighs 95 tons — floods have more than once submerged it up to the grille. It is dedicated also to those forced to leave their homeland and those who fought for a free country; the gate has been visited by many world guests — from a Japanese princess and Queen Elizabeth II to US Secretary of State Mike Pompeo.

On 16 June 2016 a bronze bust of Anton Srholec was added nearby — a Catholic priest and philanthropist whose fate reads like a history of the Curtain itself: in 1951 the seminarian Srholec and his companions tried to swim the flooding Morava, received 12 years for "illegal border crossing" and worked ten of them in the uranium mines of Jáchymov; amnestied in 1960 and secretly ordained in Rome in 1970, he spent long years under the regime as a manual labourer, and in retirement devoted himself to the homeless. The author shaped the memorial as a concrete block that seems to have flown through the Gate of Freedom, bending aside the grille that barred the way to freedom — Srholec was present at the birth of the Gate itself.
