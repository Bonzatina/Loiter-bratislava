# Horse Railway Station (Stanica konskej železnice)

The station of the first horse-drawn railway (Stanica konskej železnice) on the corner of Krížna and Legionárska streets (Krížna 33) is an unassuming-looking but unique building of 1836–1840: the terminus of the first railway of the Kingdom of Hungary, the horse-drawn line from Bratislava to Svätý Jur. Station buildings of that early era have barely survived anywhere in the world, and contemporaries had good reason to call it the "Pressburg rarity" (Prešporská rarita); architecturally it is designed as a head station modelled on palace architecture. It is the southwesternmost outpost of Nové Mesto — a building so important to the borough that it appears on its [[3-nove-mesto|coat of arms]] — and a national cultural monument.

![Horse Railway Station (Stanica konskej železnice)](/assets/stanica-konskej-zeleznice.jpg)

The railway itself began in 1836, when seventeen counts, landowners and merchants founded the First Hungarian Bratislava–Trnava Railway Company. The first section to Svätý Jur opened in 1840, the first train reached Pezinok on 30 June 1841 (the trip from Bratislava took about 1 hour 40 minutes), and Trnava on 1 June 1846, in two and a half hours; by the end of that year the line stretched to Sereď. The carriages were drawn by horses, changed roughly every 12 kilometres at relay stations; the line carried both passengers and freight, remained the only one of its kind in the whole Kingdom of Hungary, and gave the city's economy a visible push — not for nothing did the [[Pivovar-Stein|Stein Brewery]] settle right next to its station in 1872. That same year horse traction was abolished: the line was rebuilt, and on 1 May 1873 the first steam-hauled train ran along it.

With new stations built elsewhere the building's importance declined, yet it survived even the socialist-era reconstruction of the crossing — boreholes for demolition charges had already been drilled into its walls when the Hydrostav company bought and repaired it at the last moment, housing its club there for years. After 1989 the house passed to the Nové Mesto borough and serves as a wedding ceremony hall; a 2018 public tender for its future use attracted not a single bid. In 2025 the borough announced a partial reconstruction costing around €350,000 — roof, windows, waterproofing, wiring — with its building office to move in; the Slovak Railways (ŽSR) are also interested, contemplating moving their headquarters into the former railway hospital next door. Meanwhile a new secondary city centre is taking shape around Trnavské mýto: the Istropolis construction, plans for the Filiálka station and the renewal of the [[Trznica|Market Hall]] across the road.
