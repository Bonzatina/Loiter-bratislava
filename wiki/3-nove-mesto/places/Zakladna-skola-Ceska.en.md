# The Schools on Česká and Budyšínska (Školy na Českej a Budyšínskej)

The school complex on Česká street was the first modern functionalist answer to a school building in Slovakia. Air, light and space instead of historical ornament: here, for the first time in the country, interwar architecture answered not "what should a school look like" but "how does one learn in it".

![The Schools on Česká and Budyšínska (Školy na Českej a Budyšínskej)](/assets/zakladna-skola-ceska.jpg)

The schools were needed by the new quarter on Tehelné pole; the idea appeared in 1924, the government council approved an accelerated construction on 14 November 1925, and the 1926 competition was won by the young Prague architects **Pavel Smetana** (b. 1903) and **Karel Seifert** (1900–1927), ahead of Jozef Marek and [[Balan-Alois|Alois Balán]] with Jiří Grossmann, among others. Seifert did not live to see the work finished — he died in a car accident in 1927. The foundation stone was laid on 24 June 1928, construction was completed on 28 October 1929, classes began on 5 December, and the ceremonial opening fell on 9 March 1930, the school furniture having arrived late.

Boys and girls were taught separately, so the complex was made of two almost identical T-plan buildings — the boys' on Česká and the girls' on parallel Budyšínska — together with a residence for both headmasters. The architects worked with simple geometric forms, flat roofs, loggias, marquees and large surfaces of glass concrete; a characteristic wide canopy covers the entrance steps. The cross-bar of the T is offset by half a storey and holds the gymnasium and the special rooms — drawing and handicrafts. Originally the buildings had three storeys; a fourth was added after the Second World War, and with it the roof terraces with their summer classrooms disappeared. Round windows, loggias and glass concrete are a direct echo of Bauhaus aesthetics.

From 25 January 1929 the schools carried the name "Masaryk Jubilee Schools" (Jubilejné školy Masarykove), and on 12 October 1930 the eighty-year-old president T. G. Masaryk came in person. Before the front arrived, the German military administration took the building as a field hospital and teaching moved elsewhere; from 1976 the house on Budyšínska served as the district national committee of Bratislava III. The school on Česká never lost its purpose: its pupils included the ice-hockey player Vladimír Dzurilla, the figure skater Ondrej Nepela, the musician Miroslav Žbirka, president Ivan Gašparovič and the actor Richard Stanke.

- Address: Česká 10 (boys' school) and Budyšínska (girls')
- Access: a working school, no public entry; both buildings can be seen from the street
