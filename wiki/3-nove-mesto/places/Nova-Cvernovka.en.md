# Nová Cvernovka

Nová Cvernovka at Račianska 78–80 is Slovakia's largest cultural and creative centre, and the second life of the community evicted in 2016 from the old [[Cvernovka|Cvernovka]] on Svätoplukova. The artists, designers, architects and musicians did not scatter: the Cvernovka Foundation came to terms with the Bratislava Self-Governing Region, and the whole community moved into the empty building of a former secondary chemistry school on the northern edge of Nové Mesto.

![Nová Cvernovka](/assets/nova-cvernovka.jpg)

The building is itself a monument of the post-war years. It went up at the turn of the 1940s and 1950s as the administrative block of the neighbouring Dynamitka — the chemical works once founded by Alfred Nobel's company and later renamed after Juraj Dimitrov; the design is attributed to Vladimír Karfík, a pupil of Le Corbusier and Frank Lloyd Wright who spent years at Baťa's Zlín and taught in Bratislava from 1946. It was conceived as a spacious block on an inverted L-shaped plan with an emphasised entrance. From 1951 a secondary chemistry school occupied it, and the house carried that name for decades.

Today it holds some 132 studios and ateliers, multifunctional halls, a public library and a café; outside there is a terrace with a stage, a public park, a community garden and a playground. The programme runs year-round across music, visual art, literature, film and theatre, and the yard and park are freely open — which makes Nová Cvernovka one of the few places in the city you can simply walk into without an occasion.
