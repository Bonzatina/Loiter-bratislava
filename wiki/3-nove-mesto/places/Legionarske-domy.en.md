# Legionnaires' Houses (Legionárske domy)

The Legionnaires' Houses (Legionárske domy) at Ursínyho 1 and neighbouring plots are a cooperative residential quarter built in the 1920s for veterans of the Czechoslovak Legions to a design by [[Jurkovic-Dusan|Dušan Jurkovič]] and Jan Pacl. It exemplifies the publicly supported housing of Czechoslovakia's first independent years — and is almost the only coherent ensemble of **Rondocubism** in Bratislava, the republic's "national style" transplanted here from Prague.

![Legionnaires' Houses (Legionárske domy), archival photo](/assets/legionarske-domy.jpg)

Jurkovič drew the design in 1923 and construction ran in 1923–1924; the quarter's name came from the legionnaires — soldiers of the Austro-Hungarian army taken into Russian captivity in the First World War who crossed into the forming Czechoslovak units (a memorial plaque on the first house recorded the buildings' purpose). Of Ursínyho street's 340 metres, the Legiodomy occupy 290 — nine tenement houses of five to seven storeys, grouped into four half-enclosed blocks and stepping gently down as the terrain rises, plus nine family villas at the street's end. Every one- and two-room flat had its own toilet and bathroom — generosity by 1920s standards. The composition is classical: a pronounced plinth, an articulated body, a tall peaked roof; the Rondocubist vocabulary reads in the geometric panel decor, dark window surrounds, brick claddings and Jurkovič's beloved folk motifs. The quarter is still residential — and remains the city's "colourful gem": its look has barely changed in a century, and the owners deliberately avoid insulating the facades so as not to destroy the architecture's identity.
