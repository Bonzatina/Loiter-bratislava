# Red Bridge (Červený most)

The Red Bridge (Červený most) is a railway viaduct over the Mlynská dolina valley at the entrance to the forest park — the gateway to [[Zelezna-studnicka|Železná studnička]] and a monument to the birth of Slovak railways: **on 20 August 1848 the first steam train from Vienna arrived in Bratislava across this very bridge**, plugging the city into Europe's expanding railway network.

![Red Bridge (Červený most)](/assets/cerveny-most.jpg)

The first bridge was built in 1845–1848 as part of the Hungarian Central Railway from Gänserndorf via Marchegg to Bratislava: chief engineer Jozef Bayer, contractor the Italian firm of Felice Tallachini. It was the largest railway bridge of the oldest steam line on Slovak territory — 215 metres long and 17 high, nine semicircular arches of bright red brick (hence the name): the first, on the Lamač side, spanned the road to Železná studnička, the other eight strode across the valley of the Vydrica stream. In 1881 the bridge was widened to double track, and in 1935 it was thoroughly reinforced: the 90-centimetre brick vaults were clad in a 30-centimetre reinforced-concrete shell with waterproofing — losing nothing of their look and extending the bridge's life "by another 80 years".

Fate decided otherwise: on 4 April 1945 the retreating German army blew up the viaduct — only ruins remained, and the end abutment on the Bratislava side (protected since 29 April 1986 as a technical monument). Immediately after the liberation, residents with the Red Army's help erected a wooden single-track provisional bridge in seven days, and in 1946–1948 the present bridge rose — designed by engineer [[Tesar-Arpad|Arpád Tesár]], future co-author of the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]]: reinforced-concrete piers in stone cladding and a 1,122-ton steel structure made by the bridge shop of the Škoda works in Plzeň. In memory of its predecessor, the steel was painted that same red. By the bridge lies the Bratislava-Železná studienka halt of the Marchegg line — the very route of 1848.
