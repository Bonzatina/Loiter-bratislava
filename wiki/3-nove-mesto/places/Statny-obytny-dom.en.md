# The State Residential House on Vajnorská (Štátny obytný dom)

The large corner house at the crossing of Vajnorská and Trnavská streets by Trnavské mýto was built in 1924 to a design by Czech architect Vojtěch Šebor (1890–1968), who worked in Bratislava for a time. The state residential house carries the characteristic colouring of Rondocubism — a style that came to the city from Bohemia, called "Czech Art Deco" by some and "misguided" by its critics. By the entrance on the Trnavské mýto side, the construction year "1924" with the letter L can still be read on the facade.

![The house at the corner of Vajnorská and Trnavská, burned out in 1945 (photo by Ladislav Roller)](/assets/statny-obytny-dom.jpg)

At the end of the Second World War the house was burned by German troops: in Ladislav Roller's photograph of 28 May 1945 nothing remains but a burnt-out shell — like so many Bratislava buildings of those weeks. The century-old building was restored, and today it is one of Nové Mesto's notable addresses. An amusing constant: almost eighty years ago a pharmacy operated on the ground floor, right on the corner — and a pharmacy stands in the same spot today.
