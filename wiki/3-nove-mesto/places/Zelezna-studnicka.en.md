# Železná studnička

Železná studnička ("the Iron Well") is the best-known and best-loved spot of Bratislava's forest park in the Little Carpathians, the townspeople's old recreation ground on the forest road toward Kamzík. Its spa biography began back in the 19th century: in 1828 the Ferdinand Baths (Ferdinandove kúpele) opened here — the bathhouse that made the place famous, but later fell into decay and was demolished in 1970.

![The Swan Pavilion at Železná studnička](/assets/zelezna-studnicka.jpg)

Half a century on, the place is being revived: the family firm of entrepreneur Alojz Hlina set out to "restore Železná studnička's shine and glory". On the site of the baths a garden restaurant is rising, its footprint and architecture echoing the legendary spa house (one storey with an attic, with a lecture room and a terrace), and next to it a replica of the historic Swan Pavilion (Labutí pavilón) on its original spot: by autumn 2022 the pavilion had received its roof and little tower. Inside there will be a libresso and a bookshop with reading rooms. Before construction could start, a huge plane tree that had grown on the pavilion's site — 14 tonnes with its roots — had to be moved by a 100-tonne crane; the tree took hold a few dozen metres away, and, as the entrepreneur joked, a legend was born "that this place is magical, and whatever is agreed here will survive". The project also includes the restoration of the rotunda and the boundary stone — both cultural monuments — plus rest areas, a playground and enclosures with animals.

These woods have their own legend too — of the Klepáč mill, the only one on the Vydrica stream surrounded by water on both sides (the stream itself and the millrace feeding the wheel; when and why its walls tilted, nobody knows). The miller Klepáč, so the tale goes, had a beautiful daughter, Anička, who fell in love with the young hunter Janko; her parents, aiming higher, forbade the meetings, but the lovers met in secret until the miller noticed tracks in the snow. When on Christmas Eve the hunter came to ask for her hand, it was not the miller but the miller's wife who cursed the pair — and ever since, two strange animals roam the woods by the mill: a fox with the face of a beautiful girl and a hare in a hunter's hat. The enchanted lovers, they say, can be seen every winter — especially on the night of 24–25 December.
