# Rusovce Mansion (Rusovský kaštieľ)

The Rusovce mansion (Rusovský kaštieľ) is a neo-Gothic castle in the English "Windsor" style — Bratislava's most romantic country house. The estate received its present look, with crenellated parapets, oriels and corner turrets, in 1841–1844 under Count Emánuel Zichy-Ferraris, who rebuilt the old baroque mansion following the fashion for English Gothic. Around it lies a large landscape park merging into the Danube floodplain forests.

![Rusovce Mansion (Rusovský kaštieľ)](/assets/rusovsky-kastiel.jpg)

The brightest page in the castle's biography is its forty years under [[Stefania-Belgicka|Princess Stephanie of Belgium]], widow of Crown Prince Rudolf of Habsburg. In 1906 she and her husband, Count Elemér Lónyay, bought the estate and turned it into a model farm with greenhouses, a rose garden and electricity; the couple lived here until the spring of 1945, when they fled the approaching front. After the war the mansion housed the SĽUK folk-art ensemble, then stood empty for decades awaiting reconstruction — the building belongs to the state, and its restoration has become a long-running national saga; the park is open for walks.
