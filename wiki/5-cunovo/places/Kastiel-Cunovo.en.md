# Čunovo Manor — Ecocentre (Kaštieľ Čunovo)

The manor in Čunovo, known as the Szapáry manor (Szapáryho kaštieľ), is a Baroque-Classicist estate of the 18th century, the architectural gem of Bratislava's southernmost village. It was built by Peter Szapáry, great-grandson of Zuzana Armpruster (married Egresdy), who was raised to the rank of countess in 1722; besides Čunovo the Szapáry family owned a manor below Bratislava Castle, a castle and two manors in Považská Bystrica and an estate in Trebišov. The national cultural monument decayed over time — and in 2021 the Bratislava Self-Governing Region launched a comprehensive reconstruction in three parts: renewal of the manor itself, of the granary (sýpka) and revitalisation of the adjoining garden.

![Čunovo Manor and its granary under reconstruction, the Hrušov reservoir beyond the woods (Kaštieľ Čunovo)](/assets/kastiel-cunovo.jpg)

The works were thorough: the rotten roof truss was replaced with a steel structure topped with timber, the roofing was renewed and the historic chimneys restored; the attic received administrative and lodging quarters, the cleared cellars — creative workshops; the garden gained fencing, parking, paths, a biotope pond, a gazebo and information panels. The works, finished by summer 2023, were paid mostly by the European Regional Development Fund through the Interreg cross-border programmes ("Ecoregion SKAT" and "Ecoregion SKHU") — and so the estate became the Čunovo Ecocentre (Ekocentrum Čunovo), an environmental education centre for the whole region reaching into the Austrian and Hungarian borderlands: workshops, school programmes, excursions, volunteer days, summer camps and conferences.

Today the interior houses an interactive exhibition on the nature of the Danube and its wetlands (admission per price list), while the garden — a pond, medicinal herb beds, a sensory path and a bird hide — opens up during free events. A telling example is the "Summer háVEĎovačka" (Letná háVEĎovačka, from háveď — "vermin, creepy-crawlies"): a day of live snakes, spiders, scorpions, turtles, ticks and parasites, where scientists from universities and the Slovak Academy of Sciences teach how to tell a grass snake from a viper, how to remove a tick properly, and surprise visitors with the fact that Christmas mistletoe is a parasite too. Opening hours in the July–August holidays: Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 15:00–20:00.
