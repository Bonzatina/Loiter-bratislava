# Church of Our Lady of Sorrows (Kostol Sedembolestnej Panny Márie)

The church in [[3-vajnory|Vajnory]] is the village's oldest building and the core of the heritage zone declared in 1992. It was built in 1270–1279, and has changed its dedication twice: first to the Virgin Mary, then to the Hungarian king St Ladislaus, and since 1968 to the Virgin again — Our Lady of Sorrows.

![Church of Our Lady of Sorrows (Kostol Sedembolestnej Panny Márie)](/assets/kostol-vajnory.jpg)

The church has a single nave and a polygonally closed sanctuary under a Gothic ribbed vault with medieval bosses — this is the surviving 13th-century core. In 1771 the church was enlarged by a new nave, roofed with seven bays of the so-called Prussian vault. The tower with its pyramidal roof keeps loopholes in the smooth panels of its shaft — a trace of the time when a parish church in a border village was also a fortification.

The most unexpected thing inside is the sanctuary: it was painted with wall compositions by the women of Vajnory, the same mistresses of folk ornament whose work the [[Vajnorsky-ludovy-dom|Vajnory Folk House]] displays. A 13th-century Gothic vault and the painting of 20th-century village artists ended up in one room, and that is exactly what sets Vajnory apart from Bratislava's other villages.
