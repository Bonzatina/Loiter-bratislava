# Vajnory Folk House (Vajnorský ľudový dom)

The Vajnory Folk House at Roľnícka 118 is a national cultural monument of the early 19th century and a specimen of the traditional farmyard building of row villages. Inside it shows Vajnory life whole, from the open hearth to the painted walls.

![Vajnory Folk House (Vajnorský ľudový dom)](/assets/vajnorsky-ludovy-dom.jpg)

The layout was dictated by how the plots were cut: the vertical division of building parcels forced houses to run from the street towards the threshing floors and the vineyards, which produced long yards, or indeed long houses. They were built chiefly of unfired mud brick combined with stone and fired brick; the walls came out 60–70 centimetres thick, the ceiling is carried by a thick load-bearing beam, and the floors are wooden. The living part was divided into three spaces — a kitchen with an open hearth, which also heated the front room, and a chamber, often rebuilt as a back room. The farm buildings — stable, byre and barn — were separated from the dwelling by the press house, where grapes were not only pressed but the finished wine stored. The house also has an architectural feature typical of the area: a reinforced entrance to house and cellars, the so-called výpustok.

The interior painting is a value of its own. It was always placed above the open hearth, and in a different colour scheme in the front room as well. The commonest motif of the Vajnory ornament is the little bird, which the local women painters could draw in an unbelievable number of forms. This is no museum antiquity: the same painting still serves as a source of inspiration for later generations, and the women of Vajnory painted the sanctuary of the [[Kostol-Sedembolestnej-Vajnory|local church]].
