# Dog Colony (Psia kolónia)

The Dog Colony (Psia kolónia) was a vanished colony of the poor on the edge of Starý háj, founded in 1923. It owed its name to its appearance and to the stray dogs, of which more gathered here than anywhere else in Petržalka.

This was the underside of the interwar boom: through the 1920s the number of houses in Petržalka grew sixfold, the village became the largest in Czechoslovakia — and along its edges rose settlements of those for whom there was no room in the village itself. Hundreds of families built themselves dwellings out of wood and material carried from the rubbish tips, and researchers described the result as "labyrinths in miniature". The same belt held [[Elyzium|Elýzium]] by the Danube near the [[Kupalisko-Lido|Lido bathing place]], reckoned the worst of them all, and the shacks of [[Ovsiste|Ovsište]].

The colonies did not outlive the estate: the panel construction of the 1970s swept away both the old village and its fringes. The exact spot can only be given approximately today — by the description, along the edge of Starý háj.
