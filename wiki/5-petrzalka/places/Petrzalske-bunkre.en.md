# The Petržalka Bunkers B-S 1–15 (Petržalské predmostie)

Along the whole of Petržalka, from the Pečniansky forest to the Danube branches, runs a line of fifteen military bunkers of the First Czechoslovak Republic — the "Petržalka bridgehead" (Petržalské predmostie), built in 1933–1938 against post-Anschluss Austria. Bratislava is the only European capital with a fortress system so well preserved. The object cipher is simple: B for Bratislava, S for "zrub" (an infantry blockhouse), the number marking its place in the line; each also had a nickname from a local landmark. The open ground around was barred by an 11-metre belt of obstacles with more than eight thousand reinforced-concrete and steel "hedgehogs".

![Bunker B-S 4 "Lány" with anti-tank hedgehogs](/assets/bunker-bs4.jpg)

The marker stands on the main museum object — B-S 4 "Lány" (January 1937, walls up to 220 cm, garrison of 27): volunteers of the Múzeum petržalského opevnenia society have been restoring it since 2011 and have returned it to its 1938 state using original documentation and photographs; inside are a twin machine-gun mount and Slovakia's only original anti-tank casemate with its cannon, and each September the bunker comes alive with an open day, re-enactors and an evening film screening. Museums also operate in B-S 6 "Vrba" (1935, by the D2 exit onto Bratská) and B-S 8 "Hřbitov" (1937, by the old WWI military cemetery, the Zachráňme petržalské bunkre society since 2009). The largest object, B-S 10 "Tri hranice" at the former triple border point, was repaired by volunteers by 2023 and even offered for overnight stays. Part of the line is lost (B-S 11 and B-S 12 were demolished for the housing estate), part rusts in private hands — but it is this concrete chain that reminds most clearly that in 1938 the state border ran right through Petržalka.

"Private hands" is no figure of speech: bunkers surface on real-estate portals from time to time. In 2022 a bunker in the Pečniansky forest (accessed from the levee) was offered for €144,220 together with a 9,422 m² plot — the listing suggested solving water with a well, electricity with solar panels and sewage with a septic tank; on sale at the same time were a 26-metre bunker in the thickets by the Devínske Jazero station in Devínska Nová Ves (~€7,000) and a 10-metre 1930s object near Stupava (€5,000, "for unconventional weekend relaxation"). The backdrop of this trade is bleak: civil defence in Slovakia went untended for decades, and in case of war the working shelters would hold only a quarter of a million people.
