# Park of the Border's Victims (Park obetí Hraničiarov)

Námestie hraničiarov (Border Guards' Square) is panelák Petržalka's first square and a place of double memory. It was here, on 3 April 1973, that the foundation stone of the "urban sector Petržalka" was ceremonially laid — then still in the middle of a green meadow; the panel blocks rose around it only by the late 1970s. The square with its park was handed over on 11 July 1981 — for the 30th anniversary of the law on the protection of state borders, and on the wall of the transformer station the 1985 slogan that "protection of the state border is the duty of every citizen" can still be read. The square was named in honour of the Border Guard — the very force that watched over the Iron Curtain running close by.

![The park section of the former Border Guards' Square](/assets/park-obeti-hraniciarov.jpg)

After 1989 the name became a bitter irony: the guards protected not people but the regime, and dozens of ruined lives lie on their conscience — near Petržalka, Kopčany and Jarovce alone at least 19 people died trying to cross the [[iron-curtain|Iron Curtain]], from the Ehrenfeld family (December 1952) to the eighteen-year-old German Hartmut Tautz (1986). In 2024 the park section of the square received, by decision of the borough and the city, a new official name — **Park of the Border's Victims (Park obetí Hraničiarov)**, and on 16 November 2024, for the 35th anniversary of the Velvet Revolution, a memorial plaque was installed here with a post wrapped in genuine barbed wire of the Iron Curtain.
