# Au Café

Au Café on Viedenská cesta by Tyršovo nábrežie is the right bank's revived legend: a café opened here almost two centuries ago, lived through booms, decline and demolition — and a second birth. The present building of 2003 is a free replica of the original; its terrace offers one of the best views of the castle and the old town across the Danube.

![Au Café](/assets/au-cafe.jpg)

The story was set off by the Karolina Augusta pontoon bridge (1825), which replaced the "flying bridge" ferry and enlivened the promenade island of Mostná niva (the future [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]]). In December 1825 Pressburg's burgher café-keepers, led by Anton Szlubek and Ferdinand Blaß, asked the magistrate for permission to build by the bridge's landing; the Empire-style building with large semicircular windows and a columned porch with a tympanum cost 21,749 gold pieces and opened ceremonially on 3 May 1827 — announced in the Preßburger Zeitung, its owners promising to "spare no expense", offering among other things "all kinds of ever-fresh original mineral waters". In the days of the provincial diet's sessions even its deputies dropped in. The blow came, oddly, from progress: the permanent [[Stary-most|Franz Joseph Bridge]] (1890) drew the public away, and the café was saved only by the [[Propeler|propeller ferry]] established where the old pontoon bridge had been. In 1892 the establishment was bought by Karl Palugyay, owner of the [[Hotel-Carlton|Green Tree hotel]] from the famous [[Palugyayov-palac|wine dynasty]]: glazed terraces and verandas, electric arc lamps and Sunday military concerts again gathered Pressburg's choicest circles; Au Café's centenary was celebrated in style under the next owner, Otto Kölln.

After the Second World War the café was called Sad ("The Park") and still kept its standing, but in the 1960s it declined. Its fate was decided in the spirit of the age: a congress hall was planned for the site. On 28 June 1966 the Večerník ran a piece titled "'Suicide' in the SAD" — a supporting column of the terrace had collapsed by itself, "as if the building had decided to hasten the end of its life"; soon the café was demolished. The congress hall was never built — the permit was refused over flood risk. Foundations for a new building were laid in the 1990s, but the money ran out, and only in the early 2000s did Ľubo Roman, owner of the neighbouring [[Leberfinger|Leberfinger]], buy the plot: on 12 September 2003 Au Café opened again, carrying on the old name's tradition.
