# Church of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross (Kostol Povýšenia svätého Kríža)

The Church of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross (Kostol Povýšenia svätého Kríža) on Daliborovo námestie is "the first Baťa church in Slovakia": a functionalist church that interwar Petržalka owes to the shoe magnate Tomáš Baťa. After joining Czechoslovakia in August 1919 the village grew at speed — from 270 houses in 1920 to 1,600 in 1930, from three and a half thousand residents to over fourteen thousand — while its only church, built in 1672, had long ceased to hold the congregation.

![The Church of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross — "the first Baťa church in Slovakia"](/assets/kostol-povysenia-kriza.jpg)

The new church was championed by the Bratislava prelate Ľudovít Okánik, the city's chief parish priest from 1930 (until 1934 Petržalka remained a filial parish of [[Dom-svateho-Martina|St Martin's Cathedral]]). Every architect he approached quoted at least two million crowns — far beyond the means of Petržalka's Catholics — so Okánik turned to his long-time acquaintance Tomáš Baťa. Baťa's court architect Vladimír Karfík designed a church with a budget of just over three hundred thousand: it was assembled from the prefabricated elements used on Baťa factory sites. Work began on 12 July 1932 — by tragic coincidence, the day Baťa died in a plane crash, which gave the church its memorial title of "the first Baťa church" — on 21 August the foundation stone was consecrated before a crowd of up to ten thousand, with sermons in Slovak, German and Hungarian; by 18 September services were already held in the new building, and on 6 November 1932 Okánik ceremonially consecrated the church. Like the old Petržalka church, it was dedicated to St John of Nepomuk.

The press of the day marvelled at a building without a tower: "A church in a wholly modern style… There are no dark corners in it, no half-light. Everything bathes in light itself… Simplicity, light and taste!" The [[functionalism|functionalist]] church serves worshippers to this day, and still looks elegant.
