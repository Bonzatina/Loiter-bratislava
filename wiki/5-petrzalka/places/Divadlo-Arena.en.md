# Aréna Theatre (Divadlo Aréna)

The Aréna Theatre (Divadlo Aréna) on Tyršovo nábrežie is a historic theatre of 1828 on the right bank of the Danube, a national cultural monument and one of [[5-petrzalka|Petržalka]]'s main cultural venues, directly opposite the old town by [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]].

![The Aréna Theatre annex with its large windows](/assets/divadlo-arena.jpg)

The theatre's name is literal: in 1828, at the initiative of Stöger, then director of the Municipal Theatre, a summer theatre modelled on Italian amphitheatres was set up on the Petržalka bank — an open "arena" where comedies, operas, operettas, ballets and concerts ran from spring to autumn, on Sundays and holidays. A couple of years later it was replaced by a wooden amphitheatre for 3,000 with a café and restaurant, which served the townspeople for almost seventy years. In 1899 (by other accounts, 1898) the present building rose on its site — **the oldest functioning theatre building in Slovakia** — in the manner of a romantic little château with a pair of towers before the main entrance; the public, used to the old Aréna and its popular programmes, at first received it critically. A caretaker's house and a water tower were built together with the theatre. The building kept this look until about 1939. Under the Slovak State, when Petržalka went to the Reich, the theatre became a junk warehouse, and in the bombardment of the [[Stary-most|Old Bridge]] at the end of the war it was damaged and lost its tall saddle-roofed fly tower. After the war the building was closed to the public for almost half a century, serving as a warehouse for television scenery. The revival was begun by the mime [[Sladek-Milan|Milan Sládek]], who founded a foundation for the Aréna's renewal in 1994 — in 1995 the theatre played again, and since 2002, when it passed to the Bratislava Region, its permanent director has been the actor Juraj Kukura.

In February 2022 the building's owner — the Bratislava Self-Governing Region — began a comprehensive reconstruction costing nearly 9 million euros, with the declared aim of "returning the Aréna to its 1939 appearance", though the trip into the past turned out less than literal. The fly tower with the stage rigging, historically at the rear of the building, had to be placed in the centre — over the decades the theatre's service rooms had grown up on the stage's original spot, and the monument authorities approved the new position. The side "wings" existed in the past too, but the new annex is no copy of the historic one: instead of blind brick walls it has large aluminium windows with matte anthracite frames, visible from the embankment car park and from Viedenská cesta. The reconstruction concept rests on a visible split between eras: the old part keeps its wooden windows in the original dark red, the new part gets anthracite aluminium.

The annex expanded the backstage: on the Danube side came a common room and a foyer for the LOFT rehearsal space upstairs (open to the public during performances and events), on the Petržalka side four actors' dressing rooms and a room for technical staff; the previous three dressing rooms became seven. The renewal also covered the Water Tower (Vodná veža) on the theatre grounds, and the building gained a café with a view of the Danube and the city panorama; during the works the Aréna's performances ran on the stage of the [[Divadlo-POH|P. O. Hviezdoslav Theatre]]. After the reconstruction the historic theatre was to become one of the most modern in Slovakia; the reopening was planned for the 2023/2024 season at the latest.
