# Lido Bathing Place (Kúpalisko Lido)

Lido was the legendary natural bathing place on the right bank of the Danube, roughly between the [[Stary-most|Old Bridge]] and today's Apollo Bridge, on Klokočova street. It came into being in 1928, when Petržalka was not yet part of Bratislava, and for half a century remained the city's main beach; nothing survives of it today but the name of a city neighbourhood and old photographs.

![The Lido bathing place on the Danube bank (Kúpalisko Lido)](/assets/kupalisko-lido.jpg)

It began modestly: a tidied gravel shore of the Danube, a small wooden pool and moored pontoons. In 1934 a larger pool and canvas changing cabins were built; a year later fire damaged the place, but it was restored fairly quickly. Showers, a large sandpit for children, a slide, a volleyball court and table-tennis tables gradually appeared, and refreshments were sold from wooden pavilions.

Then politics intervened. In 1938 Petržalka was annexed to the German Reich — and as a replacement for the lost Lido, a pool was built for Bratislava's residents at Tehelné pole in 1943. In 1944 Lido was damaged by USAAF bombing. After the war the shore became a favourite place of recreation again and worked until the early 1980s. In the 1990s plans appeared to revive the bathing place as part of a larger recreational complex, but they never went beyond plans: the equipment was carted off, the iron structures dismantled, the pools filled in, and self-seeded growth took the ground over.
