# The Gallery-Access House on Röntgenova (Pavlačový dom)

The modest four-storey L-shaped house on Röntgenova street is one of the few surviving examples of municipal social housing of the First Czechoslovak Republic in Bratislava. It was designed in 1931 by architect [[Quastler-Desider|Desider Quastler]] as dignified housing for workers' families: a smooth white facade with unevenly spaced windows, and glazed pavlače (open access galleries) on the courtyard side in a constructivist spirit — architectural dignity on a minimal budget. The flats were mostly one-room units and studios; the roof held a laundry, the ground floor a grocery, later even a Mototechna branch.

![The gallery-access house on Röntgenova today](/assets/pavlacovy-dom.jpg)

Locals called it simply the "činžák" — among pre-war Petržalka's family houses an apartment block was a curiosity, and the galleries offered a view of Bratislava Castle. The house lived through 1968, the panel construction of the 1970s and years of decline; after the revolution it housed the military administration, and today it is residential again. In the shadow of the new Dvory quarters it recalls the interwar idea that affordable housing need not be poor.
