# The "Peace" Mural (Mier)

The "Peace" mural (Mier) by painter Jozef Porubčin (1985) is a monumental painting of roughly 12 × 36 metres on the blind gable of a twelve-storey panel block at Osuského 1. The street itself is a piece of history too: built in the late 1970s as Thorez street, since 1991 it has borne the name of diplomat [[Osusky-Stefan|Štefan Osuský]], to whom Petržalka owes its place in Slovakia. At the centre of the composition is a flower, surrounded by silhouettes of young people and a dove of peace: a socialist-realist theme rendered in almost ornamental graphics in a brown-and-ochre palette. Such gable-wall paintings were a distinctly Petržalka phenomenon — a whole open-air gallery with no parallel elsewhere in Slovakia.

![The "Peace" mural on Osuského: the design and the restored painting](/assets/mural-mier.jpg)

After 2000 the wave of facade insulation buried almost all of the district's gable paintings under polystyrene — "Peace" was the last one standing, and then it too disappeared. It was saved by photographer and documentarist Martin Kleibl: after more than two years of negotiations with the building manager and the apartment owners, the painting was re-applied onto the freshly insulated facade — the work was carried out in the autumn of 2015 under the supervision of Porubčin himself. Thus "Peace" became the only monumental mural of Petržalka to survive insulation; its motifs later decorated a new tram stop in the district as well.
