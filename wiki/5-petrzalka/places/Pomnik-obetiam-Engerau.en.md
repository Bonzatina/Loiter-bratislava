# Monument to the Victims of the Engerau Camp (Pomník obetiam tábora Engerau)

In the Petržalka cemetery stands a low stone mound overgrown with ivy, carrying two tablets: text on the left, a list of names on the right. This is the common grave of those who died in the [[engerau-camp|Engerau camp]] — the most substantial thing recalling it in Petržalka, and at the same time nearly invisible.

![Monument to the Victims of the Engerau Camp (Pomník obetiam tábora Engerau)](/assets/pamatnik-engerau-cintorin.jpg)

The graves were found in the spring of 1945: five mass burials were discovered on the outer side of the old Petržalka cemetery. A commission assembled by the Slovak government exhumed the dead — those who had died of hunger, cold and beatings, and those who had been shot — and reburied them here. The numbers differ between sources: the inscription on the monument speaks of 497 resting here, the [[Leberfinger|2017 plaque]] at the other end of Petržalka of 460 exhumed including more than a hundred victims of the evacuation march, and Austrian publications of more than five hundred. Only 51 people could be identified, and then from documents the gravedigger had saved; five were taken away by Hungarian relatives, thirteen are buried in individual graves nearby.

The monument was unveiled on 8 June 1958. The tablet carries the names of thirty-three identified martyrs, with three more added later at the bottom — which is to say that most of those lying here remained nameless. The inscription is direct and in its way exact: "Here rest 497 innocent victims whom, in the spring of 1945, on the threshold of our freedom, fascist murderers killed in the Petržalka cemetery. Let this grave be a lasting warning against the horrors of fascism. Eternal memory to the martyrs!" In 2021 the plot was renewed — the individual graves and the monument's surroundings were restored; the work cost €18,750, four fifths of it from the state.
