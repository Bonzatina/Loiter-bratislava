# Leberfinger Restaurant

Leberfinger is a historic building on Viedenská cesta at the western edge of [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]], home to a restaurant with 250 years of history — the oldest inn of the Danube's right bank. The long whitewashed house under a tall tiled roof with a row of dormer windows remembers the era when the road to Vienna passed here.

![Leberfinger Restaurant](/assets/leberfinger.jpg)

The house originally served as a coaching inn (zájazdný hostinec) on the Vienna road; Napoleon is named among its guests — French troops stormed the Pressburg bridgehead in 1809, defended by [[Bianchi-Vinzenz|General Bianchi]]. The establishment took its present name from Ludwig Leberfinger, who ran the restaurant from 1894. Under socialism the house held a chimney-sweeps' training school, and in 1992 the building burned out. In 1996–1998 it was restored by the actor and entrepreneur Ľubo Roman — the actor Pavol Mikulík became the first resident of the revived house; a few years later the same Roman brought the neighbouring [[Au-Cafe|Au Café]] back to life as well. Today's restaurant has a children's garden and an ice-cream bar, and across the road survives the historic [[Zastavka-Viedenskej-elektricky|Vienna tram stop]].

The house's heaviest chapter is the winter of 1944/45. In the building next to what was then the inn stood one of the six sub-camps of the [[engerau-camp|Engerau camp]] for Hungarian Jews, and of all six Leberfinger is the only one still standing today. On 29 March 1945, during the camp's evacuation by march towards Mauthausen, Viennese SA men shot at least thirteen prisoners on this spot. A memorial plaque was unveiled on the wall exactly seventy-two years later, on 29 March 2017, by the justice ministers of Slovakia, Austria and Hungary — Lucia Žitňanská, Wolfgang Brandstetter and László Trócsányi — on the initiative of the Vienna Research Centre for Post-War Justice. Its text runs in four languages, Slovak, Hebrew, Hungarian and German, and ends: "We shall never forget the suffering of the victims nor the crimes of the perpetrators. Never again!"
