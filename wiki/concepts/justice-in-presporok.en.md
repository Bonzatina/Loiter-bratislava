# Justice in Old Prešporok

Bratislavans walk past the execution sites of old Prešporok every day without telling them apart from any other stretch of paving: nothing survives anywhere. Gallows were built of wood and dismantled once the sentence had been carried out, so the city's medieval and early-modern executions exist only in the archives — and in a single stone [[Pamatna-tabula-Agaty|slab]] on Hurbanovo námestie.

There were several places where sentences were executed, and all of them are busy spots in the centre today: by Rybárska brána, on the Main Square in front of the [[Stara-radnica|Old Town Hall]] (where the city's first gallows stood), at the Vydrica Gate towards [[Vydrica|Vydrica]], and further out at Krížna street and Trnavské mýto. The gallows before [[Michalska-veza|St Michael's Gate]], on what is now the boundary of Župné and Hurbanovo squares, were deliberately conspicuous: everyone entering the town saw them.

An execution was a public event and, by today's standards, oddly festive: nobody worked on the day of the sentence, people came in from the surrounding villages and from far away, food and drink were sold, children were brought along, and a bell in the tower announced the beginning. Hanging and beheading were the most common; for the gravest offences the condemned was broken on the wheel, while burning and drowning were reserved above all for accusations of witchcraft. Water served as a "test of innocence" with inescapable logic: a woman who drowned was judged innocent, one who surfaced was believed to have had the devil's help, and death then followed anyway. The town executioner was a salaried figure of the magistrate, respected and feared at once; the city's other "unclean" business fell to him too — down to supervising the municipal brothel on today's [[Obchodna-ulica|Obchodná]].

The severity was balanced by peculiar safeguards: had an innocent nobleman been executed on its verdict, the town judge and his assessors answered with their own heads and property, and last-minute pardons did happen and were recorded. The one victim of this system the city names is Agatha Toott Borlobaschin, burnt at the moat before St Michael's Gate on 24 May 1602: the first documented "witch" of Pressburg.
