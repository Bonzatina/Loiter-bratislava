# Little Carpathian Wine

The Little Carpathian wine region (Malokarpatská vinohradnícka oblasť) runs along the south-western slopes of the Little Carpathians from Bratislava to Horné Orešany — and the city does not lie beside it but inside it: eight of its boroughs are entered in Slovakia's vineyard register as wine communities. Five of them — [[4-devin|Devín]], [[4-karlova-ves|Karlova Ves]], [[3-nove-mesto|Nové Mesto]], [[3-raca|Rača]] and [[3-vajnory|Vajnory]] — make up the Bratislava wine district in the top category B1; three more — [[4-devinska-nova-ves|Devínska Nová Ves]], [[4-lamac|Lamač]] and [[4-zahorska-bystrica|Záhorská Bystrica]] — belong to the Stupava district, category B2. The region as a whole has 5,359.2 hectares of registered vineyards, divided into twelve districts and 119 wine communities.

The south-western slopes of the Little Carpathians are considered the true treasury of Slovak viticulture, and for long centuries the Bratislava region was the country's most important. It was not only a matter of natural conditions: the markets lay close by — Bratislava itself, and beyond it the Czech lands, Germany, Austria and Silesia. The geological substrate is granite and the alluvial fans of Little Carpathian streams; the soils are sandy loam, moderately stony. From Devín to Častá these are above all white wines, Veltliners first among them; the reds are the speciality of the region's two edges — Frankovka from Rača and Blauer Portugieser from Orešany.

Rača's Frankovka modrá is the name that travels furthest. Maria Theresa made its reputation: in 1767, recovering from a grave illness, the empress credited her recovery to a barrel of Frankovka from the Rača parish priest Radohanyi, and the "miraculous" wine was never again absent from her cellars, while a donation charter for red wine sealed its fame. The gold-embroidered vestment with which the empress thanked the priest is still shown at the [[Racianska-pamatna-izba|Rača Memorial Room]].
