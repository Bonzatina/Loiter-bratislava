# Sandberg

Sandberg is a sand outcrop above Devínska Nová Ves, minutes from the panel-block housing, and one of Bratislava's most unusual natural sites: millions of years ago a sea splashed here. The yellow sand cliffs are sediments of a Tertiary sea, and their layers have yielded hundreds of fossil species — from snails and bivalve shells to shark teeth and turtle remains. Today it is a major palaeontological and geological locality within the Devínska Kobyla National Nature Reserve (Národná prírodná rezervácia Devínska Kobyla).

![The sand cliffs of Sandberg](/assets/sandberg.jpg)

The site's very value nearly destroyed it: while the slopes were freely accessible, visitors walked straight on the sand and illegally dug out fossils, damaging the fragile ecosystem. Most of the territory is therefore now fenced off — it can only be viewed from a safe distance, entering the enclosures is prohibited and fined, and an online camera keeps watch over the outcrop. None of this spoils the atmosphere: the sand cliffs cut into the urban fabric and are at their best towards evening, when the setting sun floods them, and the slopes open views of the Morava river, [[Hrad-Devin|Devín Castle]] and the Austrian countryside beyond.

The dry sandy conditions also sustain rare fauna: the most striking resident is the European bee-eater (včelárik zlatý), a vividly coloured bird that digs nesting burrows up to a metre deep into the sand walls. The walk is easily continued uphill — to the [[Vyhliadkova-veza-Devinska-Kobyla|lookout tower on Devínska Kobyla]] past an abandoned missile base; the terrain is easy, so families with children and photographers alike come here readily.
