# Malý Slavín Memorial (Pamätník Malý Slavín)

Malý Slavín — "Little Slavín" — is the grave of two Soviet soldiers about halfway along the forest road between Rača and Marianka, in the middle of the Little Carpathian woods. The name, a diminutive of the great [[Slavin|Slavín]] above the city, attached itself to the place, and today it is a popular stop for walkers and a Sunday destination for dozens of Bratislavans and people from the surrounding area.

![Malý Slavín Memorial (Pamätník Malý Slavín)](/assets/pamatnik-maly-slavin.jpg)

During the liberation of the city two Russian soldiers, Ivan Gorjonov and Nikolaj Finuškin, were badly wounded. They came from different units and died on 4 April 1945 while being transported; they were buried together in the forest above Rača, and from the end of the war a small memorial stood over the grave. A proper monument — the shared headstone of both men — was unveiled here in 1984, along with the landscaping of the whole area: in August of that year, for the 40th anniversary of the Slovak National Uprising, the entire site with its shelters and large fire pit was ceremonially opened. The monument is made of a roughly worked granite boulder, to which a black marble plaque with an inscription in Cyrillic was added. A spring became part of the memorial place too, named after the soldiers — Ivan's and Nikolaj's spring, laid out as early as 1981.

After 1989 the red star disappeared from the monument and the whole site fell derelict. In 1996 it was reconstructed and a new memorial plaque was put up; the restoration of the monument itself was carried out in 2003 by the restorers Andrej Baník and Ladislav Chamuti, and cost €1,760 — an almost symbolic sum against the city's other restorations.
