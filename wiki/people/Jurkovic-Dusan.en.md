# Dušan Jurkovič

Dušan Samuel Jurkovič (23 August 1868, Turá Lúka — 21 December 1947, Bratislava) was the "poet of timber", Slovakia's best-loved architect of the turn of the century, who fused folk building traditions with Art Nouveau. He studied at Vienna's state industrial school (1884–1889), where the urbanist Camillo Sitte taught, and trained in Michal Urbánek's atelier in Vsetín. He made his name with wooden buildings in the Moravian-Slovak folk spirit: the Libušín mountain lodge at Pustevny (1897–1899), the spa complex at Luhačovice (1902), his own villa in Brno (1906). During the First World War he designed some 35 military cemeteries in Western Galicia, weaving Lemko and Rusyn motifs into them.

![Dušan Jurkovič](/assets/jurkovic-dusan.jpg)

After 1918 Jurkovič moved to Bratislava and joined the building of the new capital. Among his Bratislava works are the [[Legionarske-domy|Legionnaires' Houses]] in Nové Mesto (with Jan Pacl, 1920s), a rare local example of **Rondocubism**, the "national style" of young Czechoslovakia, and the functionalist [[Sanatorium-Koch|Koch Sanatorium]] (1929–1930, with Jindřich Merganc and Otmar Klimeš), regarded as the most modern clinic in the country. A long friendship tied him to the clinic's founder [[Koch-Karel|Karel Koch]]: in the war, when the Germans put a hundred-thousand-mark bounty on the anti-fascist surgeon's head, it was Jurkovič who saved his life. He himself considered the **burial mound of Milan Rastislav Štefánik on Bradlo hill** near Brezová pod Bradlom (1924–1928) his life's principal work — a stepped composition of rough-cut stone with four pylons growing into the hill's silhouette; before it, he had arranged Štefánik's funeral on the same spot. A late work is the cable car station at Lomnický štít in the High Tatras. The [[Jurkovicova-teplaren|Jurkovič Heating Plant]] by the Danube also bears his name, though there is no documentary confirmation of his authorship.

In 1945 Jurkovič was the first to receive the title of National Artist of Slovakia; he was awarded the Order of T. G. Masaryk and the Pribina Cross. Slovakia's main architectural prize bears his name — among its laureates is [[Dedecek-Vladimir|Vladimír Dedeček]].
