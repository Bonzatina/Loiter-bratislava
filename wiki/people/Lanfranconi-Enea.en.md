# Enea Grazioso Lanfranconi

Enea Grazioso Lanfranconi (1850–1895) was the Italian engineer who "tamed the Danube": chief designer of one of the last great regulations of the river at Bratislava. The Milanese Lanfranconi family moved to Pressburg in 1870; twenty-year-old Enea, having finished his civil-engineering studies in Milan, joined the hydrographic department of the Ministry of Public Works and rose quickly. Fame came with his five-volume atlas of the Danube channel from Devín to Gönyű in Hungary, printed in a hundred copies — a single map survives today.

![Enea Grazioso Lanfranconi, lithograph](/assets/lanfranconi-enea.jpg)

Inherited and purchased quarries by the Danube gave him the freedom to pursue his passions: articles on navigation, a unique collection of books, maps and paintings about the Danube (now in Budapest), and a collection of views of Bratislava bequeathed to the city — it is kept in the city gallery's depository. He was also a keen archaeologist, one of the founders of the Hungarian Archaeological Society. He owned a palace on Ľudovít Štúr Square (now the Ministry of the Environment) and an Art Nouveau chateau in the Botanical Garden. The end was sad: on 9 March 1895 the 44-year-old Lanfranconi, worn out by sleepless months at the bedside of his gravely ill wife, shot himself in his own house; he is buried in Varese, in his native Lombardy. His name — with a lost "n", "Lafranconi" — is borne by a [[Internat-Lafranconi|student dormitory]] and a Danube motorway bridge.
