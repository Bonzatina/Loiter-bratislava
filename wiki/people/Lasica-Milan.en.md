# Milan Lasica

Milan Lasica (3 February 1940, Zvolen — 18 July 2021, Bratislava) was a humorist, playwright, actor, lyricist and singer, one half of the legendary Lasica–Satinský duo with which modern Slovak humour began. Almost his entire life passed in Bratislava — from the school bench to his death on the stage of his own theatre.

![Milan Lasica](/assets/lasica-milan.jpg)

The family moved to Bratislava soon after his birth — his father, a bank clerk, was given a post at Tatra banka (after February 1948 the bank was abolished and the father was "sent into production"). Lasica finished the local gymnasium (today's GAMČA) and in 1957, despite his cadre file, entered dramaturgy at the VŠMU — the school of rector Andrej Bagar gave a chance even to the "politically unreliable". While still a student he began performing at the Tatra Revue cabaret, and in 1958, aged eighteen, played the lead in the pantomime "The Old Suitor" — for [[Sladek-Milan|Milan Sládek]]. From 1959 the Lasica–Satinský duo lived on stage: authorial dialogues pushing banal situations into the absurd, modelled on Voskovec and Werich — intellectual satire instead of folk joviality, humour as "a manifestation of freedom".

The regime did not tolerate freedom for long. After television dramaturgy (1964–1967) the duo settled at the Divadlo na korze — in 1970 the theatre was closed for criticising the occupation order, and the pair were banned from performing. Their refuge became the Večerní Brno cabaret (1970–1972, under StB surveillance), then the operetta and drama of Bratislava's Nová scéna. Lasica chose not to emigrate, following Jan Werich's precept: "If you want to do this kind of humour, you must grow up in the language." From 1982 he headed Štúdio S — today's Štúdio L+S — which he later ran as director. He sang with the swing orchestra Bratislava Hot Serenaders, recording albums of old evergreens with them; for decades he hosted television shows and wrote feuilletons. His honours include the Crystal Wing for lifetime achievement (1997, with Satinský) and the Pribina Cross, 2nd class (2005).

Lasica died as he lived: on 18 July 2021, at the anniversary concert of the Bratislava Hot Serenaders in Štúdio L+S, having finished the song "Ja som optimista" ("I Am an Optimist"), he suddenly collapsed during the bow; he could not be revived — a sudden cardiac arrhythmia. He was married to the actress Zora Kolínska, then to the actress and diplomat Magda Vášáryová; daughters Hana and Žofia.
