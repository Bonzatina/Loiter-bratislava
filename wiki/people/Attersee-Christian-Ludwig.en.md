# Christian Ludwig Attersee

Christian Ludwig Attersee (real name Christian Ludwig; born 28 August 1940, Bratislava) is one of the best-known Austrian artists of our time — and a Bratislava native almost forgotten in his home city. He was born into the family of architect [[Ludwig-Christian|Christian Ludwig]], author of [[Manderlak|Manderlák]] and the [[Obchodny-dom-Dunaj|Dunaj department store]], and the Austrian-born Susanna (d. 2003). In 1945 the family left the city and settled in Linz; the artist never returned to Bratislava.

![Christian Ludwig Attersee](/assets/attersee-portrait.jpg)

He spent his childhood by Lake Attersee in Upper Austria, from which he later took his pseudonym. From an early age he showed talent in music, drawing and literature (and also became a titled yachtsman). He studied architecture at Vienna's Hochschule für angewandte Kunst, then painting in Vienna and Berlin. Since 1990 he has been a professor at Vienna's Academy of Applied Arts. His signature style is experimental figurative-symbolic painting with erotic motifs and a loud, saturated palette.

His most visible work in public space is the giant five-storey mosaic with a mythological Atlas on a hotel facade on Vienna's Mariahilferstrasse (nos. 78–80, 1996). In 2000 the Slovak National Gallery showed an Attersee exhibition — a rare case of Bratislava remembering its native son: the city has neither a memorial plaque nor a street in his name, which prompted the reproach that "Bratislava forgets its natives".
