# Ferdinand Milučký

Ferdinand Milučký (26 September 1929, Rajec — 26 July 2019, Bratislava) was one of the most significant Slovak architects of the second half of the 20th century, a poet of "maximal abstraction": flowing spaces demarcated by parallel wall planes. A pupil of [[Bellus-Emil|Emil Belluš]] and Jan Koula (whom he assisted in folk architecture research), from 1958 he was a leading designer at Bratislava's Stavoprojekt. His hand united Mies van der Rohe, brutalism and the Slovak folk tradition — and, as scholars note, he managed to stay untainted by socialist realism.

![Ferdinand Milučký](/assets/milucky-ferdinand.jpg)

His principal work is the [[Krematorium|Bratislava crematorium and urn grove]] (1967–1969), called one of the finest Slovak buildings of the 20th century. The wiki holds two more of his works: [[Park-Andreja-Hlinku|Andrej Hlinka Park]] in Ružinov (1978) — a peak of Slovak landscape architecture — and the [[Dom-Miluckeho|architect's own house]] on Révová (1975), a manifesto of modernism in dialogue with vernacular building. Off-stage remain the Trávniky housing estate (1968), the Bratislava restaurant at Expo '67 in Montreal, the Czechoslovak embassies in Moscow and Rome, the House of Arts in Piešťany, and the redesigns of the Main and Franciscan squares (1988).

A five-time laureate of the Dušan Jurkovič Prize, winner of the Herder Prize (1999) and holder of the Order of Ľudovít Štúr, 2nd class (2005). The Slovak National Museum showed a lifetime exhibition of his work while he was still alive (1993).
