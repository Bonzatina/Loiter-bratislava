# Matthias Corvinus (Matej Korvín)

Matthias Corvinus (Matej Korvín, 1443–1490) was the Hungarian king who left Bratislava two great gifts: a university and a Golden Bull. The educated and linguistically gifted fifteen-year-old Matthias Hunyadi was elected — but not crowned — in January 1458: the crown of St Stephen was in the hands of Emperor Frederick III, and only the efforts of the king's tutor Ján Vitéz brought it back. Matthias received his legitimate coronation only in March 1464, in the seventh year of his reign.

![Matthias Corvinus, a miniature from a Corvina codex](/assets/matej-korvin.jpg)

Two months after the coronation, on 27 May 1464, the king issued the city the **Golden Bull of Bratislava** — a parchment with a golden seal confirming the privileges of Pressburg's burghers: rights to their real property and free carriage of goods down the Danube all the way to Buda. The precious charter is kept in the Bratislava City Archive. And in 1465 Matthias founded here the **Academia Istropolitana** — the first university on the territory of today's Slovakia, which became a centre of learning of the Hungarian kingdom. In folk memory Matthias remained "the good and just king" ("King Matthias died — justice died"), though historians recall another side too: he executed and imprisoned his own supporters, slapped the aged Vitéz across the face, and ordered a tailor's nose cut off for some mistake. He died at 47 in Vienna in April 1490 — by tradition, of a stroke brought on by fury over unwashed figs. Since 2018 a square between Mýtna and Radlinského streets has borne his name.
