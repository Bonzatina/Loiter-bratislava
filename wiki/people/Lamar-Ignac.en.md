# Ignác Lamár — Schöne Náci

Ignác Lamár (11 August 1897, [[5-petrzalka|Petržalka]] — 23 October 1967, Lehnice) was the best-known figure of Bratislava's streets in the twentieth century, whom the whole city knew by his nickname Schöne Náci ("handsome Náci"). For nearly forty years, from the 1920s to the mid-1960s, he was an inseparable part of the street life, cafés and pastry shops of old Prešporok: a quiet, poor and always impeccably dressed man in a tailcoat, top hat and white scarf, with a walking stick inherited from his father.

![Ignác Lamár — Schöne Náci](/assets/lamar-ignac.jpg)

His life was far darker than the image. The family broke up early: his younger brother, who had cerebral palsy, was placed in an institution, his mother left the household, his father drank himself to death, and the debts left the boy homeless. He scraped by at whatever came along — pastry cook's and shoemaker's apprentice, coal carrier, carpet beater, stagehand, street singer. The theatre also gave him the great attachment of his life, the singer Žaneta, whose departure hit him hard.

Left with nothing, he chose the one thing nobody could take from him: manners. Lamár walked his unvarying route from [[Michalska-veza|St Michael's Gate]] across [[Stara-radnica|the Main Square]] and Zelená street to the Danube embankment, lifting his top hat to the ladies, bowing and offering compliments and good wishes in the three languages of a trilingual city. The pastry shops knew him: at Stürtzer's bakery on Sedlárska they would wrap cakes for him. His last years were spent in hospital with tuberculosis, and he died in Lehnice, where he was buried in an unmarked grave. In September 2007 his remains were moved to Bratislava, to the Ondrejský cemetery, fulfilling his wish to lie in his own city, near the grave of Július Satinský — partner of [[Lasica-Milan|Milan Lasica]] in their legendary double act.

His memory proved sturdier than his biography: the band Lojzo recorded a song about him, a fictionalised book called *Volali ho Schöne Náci* appeared, and in 1997 [[Socha-Schone-Naciho|an aluminium statue of him]] by Juraj Meliš was unveiled at Rybárska brána — today one of the most photographed spots in the city and something of a symbol of old Prešporok.
