# Ödön Lechner

Ödön Lechner (27 August 1845, Pest — 10 June 1914, Budapest) was the leading figure of Hungarian Art Nouveau and the author of two buildings that stand side by side in Bratislava: the [[Modry-kostolik|Blue Church]] and the [[Gymnazium-Grosslingova|gymnasium on Grösslingová]]. He studied at a technical school in Buda (1865) and in Berlin (1866–1868), and began his career in France under Clément Parent restoring monuments — a training that later let him handle historical forms freely without copying them.

![Ödön Lechner](/assets/lechner-odon.jpg)

Lechner built his style on the search for a "Hungarian national language" in architecture: a modern, rationally organised plan dressed in motifs read out of folk art and out of Oriental, above all Indian, models. Hence his signature devices — filigree ceramics on the roofs, coloured glazed tiles on the facades, a soft, almost flowing line in place of the right angle. His principal Hungarian works are the Museum of Applied Arts (1891–1896), the Geological Institute (1896–1899), the church of St Ladislaus in Kőbánya (1891–1896) and Szeged town hall (1882–1883).

In Prešporok he was commissioned for the school chapel of St Elizabeth and for the Catholic Royal Gymnasium next door; the gymnasium opened in autumn 1908, the church was begun in 1909 and consecrated in 1913. Both buildings use one palette and one set of devices, so that together they read as a single ensemble — and remain the summit of Art Nouveau in Slovakia. Countess Gabriela Szapáry financed the work. Lechner strongly influenced Hungarian architects of the early twentieth century and remains one of the best-known names in the country's architecture.
