# Arpád Tesár

Arpád Tesár (1 February 1919, Vrútky — 15 June 1989, Bratislava) was a civil engineer and professor, the man whose structures hold up both of the city's great bridges: he engineered the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]] and authored the post-war [[Cerveny-most|Red Bridge]]. His credo: "a statically perfect structure is aesthetically beautiful too."

The son of a railway worker, Tesár trained at the Slovak Technical University in Bratislava and, at the end of 1944, at the Technical University in Berlin-Charlottenburg, where he assisted in Ferdinand Schleicher's atelier alongside the luminaries of German bridge-building (Dischinger, Tolke, Agatz). On his return, until 1948 he repaired the war-torn railways at Bratislava's ministry of bridges and railways — to those years the city owes the [[Cerveny-most|Red Bridge]] (1946–1948) and the rebuilt railway part of the [[Stary-most|Old Bridge]]. He then headed the project department of the Bratislava branch of the Vítkovice ironworks and the Hutný projekt office, and from 1955 the design department of the Welding Research Institute, developing pressure-vessel welding techniques for the lower-grade Soviet ore that had replaced the Swedish supply.

Tesár's chief work is the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]] (1967–1972) in tandem with the architect Jozef Lacko: a cable-stayed structure without a single pier in the channel that put Bratislava into the world's engineering annals. His portfolio also holds the first tower on Kamzík (predecessor of today's [[Televizna-veza-Kamzik|TV tower]]), the prestressed cable pipeline bridge in Kralupy, the Bánov and Tisovec viaducts, industrial works from Istebné to Podbrezová, the Slovnaft flare towers and the roof of Bratislava's Ondrej Nepela Winter Stadium. Tesár was a member of the Slovak Academy of Sciences and a laureate of the Aurel Stodola Gold Medal.
