# Emil Belluš

Emil Belluš (19 September 1899, Slovenská Ľupča — 14 December 1979, Bratislava) is a classic of 20th-century Slovak architecture and the founder of architectural education in Slovakia. Born into the family of a successful cabinetmaker (his father decorated Budapest's New York Café), he began his studies in Budapest in 1918 and completed them at the Czech Technical University in Prague (1923) under the traditionalist professor Antonín Engel — a combination that defined his signature: functionalism softened by respect for history and detail. Belluš believed that "perfect works are made of many perfect details".

![Emil Belluš](/assets/bellus-emil.jpg)

He built in Bratislava throughout the interwar and post-war eras. An early work is the [[Propeler|ferry wharfs of the propeller boat]] (1930) on both banks of the Danube and the Slovak Rowing Club clubhouse in Petržalka (1931; Belluš was a passionate rower and a founding member of the club). On SNP Square rose his [[Druzstevne-domy|Coop Houses]] — the "three red houses" with ceramic cladding (1930s). The travertine-clad branch of the [[Narodna-banka-ceskoslovenska|Czechoslovak National Bank]] on Štúrova street (1936–1938) was conceived as an image of state solidity. In 1941 he delicately adapted the [[Letny-arcibiskupsky-palac|Archbishop's Summer Palace]] for government use, returning baroque splendour to the central pavilion. Post-war works include the Devín Hotel (1948), the Faculty of Architecture building of the Slovak University of Technology, the New Town Hall, and the [[Internat-Mlada-Garda|Mladá Garda dormitory]] (1953–1955) on Račianska — his one large work in the socialist realist key.

From 1940 Belluš was a professor: he effectively created the Slovak school of architecture and served as president of the Slovak Architects Society (1945–1953). A law forbidding professors to design in parallel forced him, at 53 — in his creative prime — to give up practice for teaching; under the communist regime he also endured expulsion from the Slovak Academy of Sciences and forced retirement. Today Slovakia's main architectural award bears his name — the Emil Belluš Prize.
