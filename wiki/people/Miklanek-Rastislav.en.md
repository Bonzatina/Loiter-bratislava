# Rastislav Miklánek

Rastislav Miklánek (born 5 February 1942) is an academic sculptor, a master of park sculpture and the "finishing" of public spaces, whose works are scattered all over Bratislava. A graduate of the Bratislava Academy of Fine Arts (1966, sculpture; in 1971 he completed postgraduate studies in sculpture restoration), while still a student he created the statuette of the Bratislava Lyre — the prize of the famous music festival — and the Intervision Golden Key.

![Rastislav Miklánek](/assets/miklanek-rastislav.jpg)

His principal Bratislava cycle is the **zodiac signs in [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]]** (turn of the 1970s–80s): twelve spheres of red andesite with reliefs, placed around the semicircular rest areas of garden architect Alfonz Torma, Miklánek's constant collaborator. The sculptor found the stone by chance — he spotted interesting masonry from the road near Galanta and traced it to a quarry by the village of Dobrá Niva; he did the rough shaping with a jackhammer, the reliefs with chisels. The spheres were conceived as landmarks — and still work as such: "meet you at the Fish". The park's entrance sculpture, its trunk symbolising the park's history, never received its planned text part, and the sculptor turned down the fountain himself, passing the commission to Ambróz Balážik — which is how the Drinking Doves came to the park.

With Torma, Miklánek also reshaped the shores of [[Strkovecke-jazero|Štrkovec Lake]] in Ružinov — there stands his famous concrete camel, a beloved climbing frame for generations (originally white, repainted yellow to the author's chagrin) — and the [[Medicka-zahrada|Medical Garden]], with its entrance sculpture in the form of a linden leaf and flower vases. The fate of his works saddens the master: letters have been stolen from the sculptures, graffiti covers them. In socialist times the zodiac's authors were branded "parasites of socialism" in the press for their "excessive earnings" — culture minister Miroslav Válek stood up for them. Around 2012 Miklánek, invoking copyright, stopped a revitalisation of the park that threatened to shift the spheres into a "circle of the seasons"; the current competition for the park's renewal promises to preserve and restore the sculptures.
