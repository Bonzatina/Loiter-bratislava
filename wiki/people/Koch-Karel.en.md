# Karel Koch

Karel Koch (1890–1981) was a physician, surgeon and philanthropist, creator of the [[Sanatorium-Koch|Koch Sanatorium]] and the Koch Garden — and a man whose name was struck from encyclopedias and textbooks for decades. Czech by birth, he came to Bratislava in 1919 together with other Czech doctors to build up the healthcare of young Czechoslovakia.

![Karel Koch (reproduction of a portrait)](/assets/koch-karel.jpg)

In the early 1930s Koch opened a private women's clinic on Partizánska street, built by his friend [[Jurkovic-Dusan|Dušan Jurkovič]], and with it a garden: of its more than 120 species of ornamental trees and plants, nearly half were brought by the professor himself. In the war the renowned surgeon joined the anti-fascist resistance — the Germans put a hundred-thousand-mark bounty on his head, and it was Jurkovič who saved his life. After the liberation Koch spent a few days as mayor of Bratislava — and gave up the office for medicine.

The "humanist and democrat", as the publicist Slavo Kalný calls him, could not get along with the communist regime: in a fabricated 1950s trial for "treason and espionage" Koch received a life sentence and spent twelve years in prisons. Amnestied in 1963, he emigrated with his family to Canada after the 1968 occupation and died there in 1981. The verdict was annulled only in March 1991 — ten years after his death; he never lived to see justice. Koch's story was returned to the city by Kalný's book "Forget Koch" (Zabudnite na Kocha) — and now by the reviving garden that bears his name.
