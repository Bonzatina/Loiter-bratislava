# Ivan Matušík

Ivan Matušík (12 July 1930, Bratislava — 20 February 2022) was the chief architect of commercial Bratislava in the post-war decades. A native of the city and a pupil of [[Bellus-Emil|Emil Belluš]], he graduated in architecture from the Slovak University of Technology in 1953, and from 1961 worked at the State Project Institute of Commerce (ŠPUO), which he directed from 1968 to 1989 — hence his signature typology: department stores, shopping centres, markets.

![Ivan Matušík](/assets/matusik-ivan.jpg)

Among his Bratislava works are the circular [[Obchodne-stredisko-Slimak|Slimák shopping centre]] (1960–1964), a "snail" with a round atrium; the largest post-war complex of the city centre — the [[Prior-a-hotel-Kyjev|Prior department store with the Kyjev Hotel]] on Kamenné Square (1964–1968, the hotel in 1973); and the glazed [[Trznica|Market Hall]] at Trnavské mýto (1983). Ideological critics called him "an imitator of Western architecture" and a formalist, and after the Soviet invasion of 1968 the architect fell from favour; reassessment came at the turn of the century.

In 1976–1980 Matušík taught at his alma mater and founded and edited the architecture journal *Projekt*. He won four Dušan Jurkovič Prizes, the Crystal Wing and the CE.ZA.AR award (2003). His wife was the architect Gabriela Churová.
