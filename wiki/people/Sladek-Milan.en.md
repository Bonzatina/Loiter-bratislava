# Milan Sládek

Milan Sládek (23 February 1938, Streženice — 4 December 2024, Cologne) was a world-renowned mime, choreographer, director and teacher, creator of the pantomime character Kefka — and the man who gave Bratislava back the [[Divadlo-Arena|Aréna Theatre]].

![Milan Sládek](/assets/milan-sladek.jpg)

Sládek began with woodcarving at Bratislava's school of applied arts (graduating in 1957), performed in the student theatre of Comenius University, studied acting at Bratislava's VŠMU and with E. F. Burian in Prague, where he formed his first pantomime ensemble. In 1958 he cast the eighteen-year-old [[Lasica-Milan|Milan Lasica]] in his pantomime "The Old Suitor", and on 10 March 1960 Kefka first stepped on stage — a piccolo from a garden restaurant in the comedy "Hrča" who became his signature character. Unlike the French school, Sládek was interested in ensemble work from the very start. In 1962 his troupe returned to Bratislava and became an autonomous body within the National Theatre, touring festivals regularly.

August 1968 broke the career in two: after the occupation Sládek left with part of the troupe via Sweden for West Germany and settled in Cologne. In 1974 he opened the Kefka Theatre there — at the time the only permanent pantomime theatre in Western Europe — and in 1976 founded the international pantomime festival GAUKLER, which for twelve years ranked among the world's best. With his troupe and the Goethe-Institut he toured more than fifty countries, and in 1987 became professor of pantomime at the Folkwang Hochschule in Essen.

After 1989 Sládek returned home: in 1994 he headed the newly founded International Institute of Movement Theatre (until 2002) and initiated the reconstruction of the historic [[Divadlo-Arena|Aréna]] building, which had stood as a warehouse for half a century — in 1995 the theatre played again. His late works are scattered across the world — "The Threepenny Opera" in Tokyo (2005), a Mozart pantomime for Vienna (2006), and in January 2016 his "Antigone" became the first pantomime performance ever staged at UNESCO in Paris. In 2000 President Schuster bestowed on him the Order of Ľudovít Štúr, 3rd class.
