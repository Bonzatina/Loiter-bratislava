# Milan Michal Harminc

Milan Michal Harminc (7 October 1869, Kulpín, now Serbia — 5 July 1964, Bratislava) was the "nestor of Slovak architecture" and one of the most prolific architects of his time: more than 300 buildings across the former Austro-Hungarian lands and their successor states — from Serbia to Ukraine — are attributed to him. A Slovak from Kulpín in Vojvodina, he learned the craft in practice — in the Budapest offices of János Bobula, Albert Schickedanz and Fülöp Ferenc Herzog, took part in Hungary's millennium construction boom, and opened his own firm in Budapest in 1897; after the First World War he moved his practice to Slovakia and settled in Bratislava.

![Milan Michal Harminc, archival photo](/assets/harminc.jpg)

Harminc was an eclectic in the best sense: he switched freely between historicism with secession notes and modern forms, adapting to the task and the client. In Bratislava his full range is on display: the classicist [[Palac-Tatra-banky|Tatra Bank palace]] (1920s) with statues by Ján Koniarek, the monumental [[Slovenske-narodne-muzeum|Slovak National Museum building]] with its Doric columns (1924–1928) — his "purest" work — the rebuilding of the [[Hotel-Carlton|Carlton hotel]] with its four added storeys (1925–1929), the modernist [[Dom-Slovenskej-ligy|House of the Slovak League]] with its arcade (1936), the brick-red [[Evanjelicky-kostol-Legionarska|Protestant church on Legionárska]] (1932) with its Constructivist massing, and — at the end of his life — the [[Kostol-svatej-Margity|Church of St Margaret]] in Lamač (1947–1951). Earlier still, in 1906–1907, he built the first Slovak National Museum building in Martin; among his interwar works outside Bratislava, the neo-classical YMCA building in Lučenec (1925) is characteristic.

Harminc lived to 94 and died in Bratislava, leaving the city buildings of four different eras and styles — a rare case of one author building one city for so long and in so many ways.
