# Vinzenz Ferrerius von Bianchi

Vinzenz Ferrerius Friedrich von Bianchi (1768–1855) was the Austrian general who held the Pressburg bridgehead against Napoleon's army. Born in Vienna, a graduate of the Engineering Academy, he distinguished himself at Aspern-Essling and on 29 May 1809 took command of the fortified bridgehead on the right bank of the Danube — its core was the territory of today's [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]], with the fortified forefield stretching beyond the village of Engerau and across the Danube islands of the future [[5-petrzalka|Petržalka]].

![Vinzenz von Bianchi, engraving](/assets/bianchi-vinzenz.jpg)

The fighting for the bridgehead lasted almost a month and a half, and, unable to break the defence, the Grande Armée turned its fire on the city itself: after the first shelling on 4 June, Bianchi reassured the magistrate ("the bombardment of the city testifies to the enemy's despair"), and at the peak of the barrages of 26–28 June, when some four thousand shells burned 123 houses, he answered the enemy with a famous letter: "You may burn more houses in Pressburg, but I will not cease to defend my position." The city's fate was decided by the defeats of the main forces at Wagram and Znaim — on 10 July Bianchi evacuated the bridgehead under orders, and on the 14th the French occupied Pressburg under the armistice. For the defence he was promoted to Feldmarschalleutnant; the grateful city gave him a ceremonial reception, honorary citizenship and a commemorative engraved goblet with a scene of the siege. Later the victory at Tolentino brought Bianchi the title of Duke of Casalanza; he visited Pressburg for the last time in 1853 and died in 1855 in today's Rogaška Slatina.
