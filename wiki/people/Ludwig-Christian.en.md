# Christian Ludwig

Christian Ludwig (26 March 1901, Bratislava — 9 February 1967, Linz) was an architect and one of the key yet unjustly forgotten figures of Slovak interwar modernism. A Bratislava native from a German-speaking Evangelical family (his grandfather Carl was a curator of the church community), he trained as an architect in Munich (graduating in 1925), returned to his home town and spent three years in the studio of Wimmer and Szőnyi (Franz Wimmer, Endre Szőnyi), before opening an independent practice in 1928 in close collaboration with Augustín Danielis.

In less than two decades Ludwig visibly changed the face of the city centre. His main Bratislava works: [[Manderlak|Manderlák]] (with Danielis and Emerich Spitzer) — Slovakia's first high-rise on SNP Square; the [[Obchodny-dom-Dunaj|Dunaj department store (Brouk a Babka)]] (1936) — one of the finest examples of Slovak [[functionalism]]; [[Lutherov-dom|Luther House]] on Palisády (1931–1932, with Danielis and reliefs by Alojz Rigele); and the multifunctional [[Ramharterov-dom|Ramharter house with Café Regina]] on Hurbanovo Square (1937) — medical offices, apartments, a café and shops under one roof. He also designed the organ of the Evangelical church on Panenská street.

Ludwig's signature was the ability to merge modernism with tradition: he was fluent in the language of functionalism (skeleton frame, free plan, ribbon glazing) yet never broke with context or the conservative tastes of his clients — which is why architectural historians long left him in the shadow of "purer" modernists.

In 1945 Ludwig emigrated with his family to Austria and opened a design office in Linz, working on hydroelectric and industrial projects. He never returned to Bratislava. His son, born in Bratislava in 1940, became a famous Austrian artist under the pseudonym [[Attersee-Christian-Ludwig|Christian Ludwig Attersee]].
