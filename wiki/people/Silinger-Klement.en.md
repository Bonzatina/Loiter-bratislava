# Klement Šilinger

Klement Šilinger (15 November 1887, Sazená, Czechia — 15 January 1951, Bratislava) was the chief conductor of [[rondocubism|Rondocubism]] in Bratislava. Trained at the Prague Academy of Fine Arts (1910–1914, under Jan Kotěra), he travelled Europe and the USA — where he met Frank Lloyd Wright — worked as an architect for the Ministry of Public Works, and from 1925 ran his own office on Dostojevského rad in Bratislava.

![Klement Šilinger](/assets/silinger-klement.jpg)

Early Šilinger means Rondocubism national in spirit: the apartment houses on [[Domy-Stetinova|Štetinová]] (1922) and [[Dom-Heydukova-23|Heydukova 23]] (1923), the huge [[Dom-postovych-zamestnancov|block for postal employees]] on Legionárska, and the style's summit — the [[Anatomicky-ustav|Institute of Anatomy]] on Sasinkova, called the best example of Rondocubism in Slovakia. By the late 1920s he had switched to the language of Le Corbusier: the [[Internat-Lafranconi|Lafranconi dormitory]] became Slovakia's first university dormitory and one of the country's first realisations of the modern movement; among his late works is the gallery-access "pavlač house" on Kalinčiakova (1931). Illness interrupted his practice in 1939–1946; after the war he joined the reconstruction effort and died in Bratislava in 1951.
