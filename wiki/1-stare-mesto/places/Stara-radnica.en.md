# The Old Town Hall (Stará radnica)

The Old Town Hall (Stará radnica) by the Main Square is the centuries-old heart of Pressburg's municipal self-government and one of the city's oldest ensembles, home today to the [[Muzeum-mesta-Bratislavy|Bratislava City Museum]]. Within its walls fitted the whole amplitude of old city life: from magistrate sessions and prison cellars to feasts that lasted for days.

![The Old Town Hall tower](/assets/stara-radnica.jpg)

They knew how to celebrate here: banquets were held for the election of the richtár, the installation of a new magistrate, monarchs' visits, and later for mayors' weddings and christenings. One feast in honour of a delegation of the Czech estates lasted several days: the guests were allowed only short rests towards morning — and the town councillors were already waking them with glasses in hand at freshly laid tables. The music was provided by the Turmmusik — the orchestra of the town-hall tower guards, who watched day and night for fires, floods, ice jams and approaching enemies, and gave the townsfolk "tower concerts" from the balustrade. In 1701 the commander of the watch smashed a musician's violin in fury (the city docked him six gulden), and in 1702 the council banned the private band of the competitor Küttl — the tower musicians' monopoly was guarded in earnest.

The town hall had its dark side too: its cellars held debtors, suspects under investigation and women accused of witchcraft, while by its walls on the Main Square stood the pillory (later a cage on Franciscan Square). Medieval Pressburg justice was swift but had counterweights: if an innocent nobleman were executed on its verdict, the richtár and the assessor-judges answered with their own heads and property. Pardons are recorded as well — in 1478 the counterfeiter Scheftenberger's pyre was commuted at the last moment to beheading, and the gallows that stood before Michael's Gate, on today's boundary of Župné and Hurbanovo squares, served as a "warning" to every newcomer: peace must reign in the town.
