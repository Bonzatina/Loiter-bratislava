# Carlton Hotel

The Carlton Hotel (Hotel Carlton, today Radisson Blu Carlton) at [[Hviezdoslavovo-namestie|Hviezdoslav Square]] 3 is an inseparable part of the old town's image and one of Bratislava's most luxurious hotels. Behind the 1920s facade hides an eight-century innkeeping biography: an inn on this spot is recorded as early as the 13th century.

![Carlton Hotel](/assets/hotel-carlton.jpg)

First stood here the inn At the Swan (U labute), from 1760 At the Three Green Trees (Zu den 3 grünen Bäumen). In 1838 it was bought by Johann Löwy, director of the first Hungarian Bratislava–Trnava railway, who in 1844–1846 had it rebuilt to a design by Ignác Feigler the Younger — the same builder who later raised the [[Palugyayov-palac|Palugyay Palace]] — into the three-storey Green Tree hotel. From 1849 the café in the hotel was run by the wine merchant Jakub Palugyay, who took over the whole hotel in 1857: the [[pressburg-families|dynasty]] ran it until 1912. In 1905 the building housed **Slovakia's first cinema** — the Electro Bioscop (in 1913 it moved across the square, where it lives to this day under the name Mladosť).

Next door, from 1860, competed the National hotel with its Savoy café. In 1908 it was bought by Henrich Prüger, manager of London's Savoy in 1903–1909; he added the Gervay house (1911) and, after Karol Palugyay's death, the Green Tree as well (1912), rebuilt all three buildings and in June 1913 opened the Savoy-Carlton complex. The name Carlton, the story goes, is assembled from the names of the former owners — Karol Palugyay (Carl) and his wife Antónia ("Tonka"). The hotel took its present shape in the great rebuilding of 1925–1929 to a design by [[Harminc-Milan-Michal|Milan Michal Harminc]]: the three buildings merged into one, grew by four storeys and received a unified facade with the characteristic tall roof. By the 1980s the Carlton had decayed so far that it was closed; after reconstruction the hotel reopened in October 2001.
