# Chatam Sofer Mausoleum (Mauzóleum Chatama Sofera)

The Chatam Sofer Mausoleum (Mauzóleum Chatama Sofera) at the tram tunnel portal on the General Svoboda embankment is Bratislava's foremost Jewish shrine and a monument unique in all of Slovakia. Chatam Sofer (born Moshe Schreiber) is revered in the Orthodox world to this day as the greatest rabbi of his age; in the first half of the 19th century he was the pride of the Pressburg community and headed the local yeshiva — one of Europe's most important centres of traditional Jewish learning, numbering 400 students at its height and considered the largest since Babylonian times.

![Chatam Sofer Mausoleum (Mauzóleum Chatama Sofera)](/assets/mauzoleum-chatama-sofera.jpg)

The rabbi died on 3 October 1839 at the age of 77 and was buried in the city's oldest Jewish cemetery under the castle hill. The cemetery perished during the Second World War with the construction of the tunnel, but the plot with Sofer's grave survived; in 1999 preparations for a reconstruction began — with the tram line diverted and the slope on [[Muzea-SNM-na-Zizkovej|Žižkova street]] reinforced — and in 2002 the mausoleum was ceremonially opened. The ensemble on the slope between the Danube and the castle was designed by architect Martin Kvasnica, reconciling the strict requirements of Jewish law with contemporary architecture: for the kohanim — descendants of the Temple priests, forbidden to step on cemetery ground — an elevated walkway was built above its surface. Inside are 23 graves in their original places and 41 gravestones; the memorial is part of the Slovak Route of Jewish Cultural Heritage. The view from the tunnel has since changed beyond recognition: where the Danube panorama once opened, the buildings of the River Park complex now stand opposite.
