# Palugyay Palace (Paluďaiho palác)

The Palugyay Palace (Paluďaiho palác, older spelling Palugyayov palác) at Pražská 1, near the main railway station, is a neo-renaissance palace with wine cellars — the headquarters of old Pressburg's largest wine empire and a national cultural monument. Since 2002, following a reconstruction, the building has housed the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Slovakia.

![Palugyay Palace (Paluďaiho palác)](/assets/palugyayov-palac.jpg)

The palace was built in 1873–1879 as the operational base of his firm by the wine merchant and entrepreneur Jakub von Palugyay; the design was by Ignác Feigler the Younger — a member of the same Pressburg builders' dynasty that later gave the city the building of the [[Kaviaren-Stefanka|Štefanka café]]. The complex, with its production facilities and cellars, kept expanding until 1918; this is where the prize-winning Château Palugyay wine was born — a name that in time passed to the building itself.

The Palugyays are one of the principal [[pressburg-families|Pressburg families]]: from hotels and the wine trade they built an empire holding the title of imperial-royal purveyors, their wine was drunk at monarchs' courts and aboard ocean liners, and Karl Palugyay, owner of the [[Hotel-Carlton|Green Tree hotel]] — today's Carlton — bought and restored to glory the right-bank [[Au-Cafe|Au Café]] in 1892. The family also commissioned the sculptor Ján Fadrusz (the [[Muzeum-hodin|Museum of Clocks]] preserves that connection). The collapse of the monarchy and the crisis of the 1930s ruined the empire — the palace on Pražská remains its most visible monument. The city has a second palace of the same name, on the Main Square.
