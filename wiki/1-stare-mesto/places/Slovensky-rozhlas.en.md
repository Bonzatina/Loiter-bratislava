# Slovak Radio Building (Slovenský rozhlas)

The Slovak Radio building (Slovenský rozhlas) on Mýtna street is an **upside-down steel pyramid** — one of Bratislava's most recognisable and most disputed silhouettes. The design by Štefan Svetko, Štefan Ďurkovič and Barnabáš Kissling was born as early as 1967, yet the house was only finished in 1983 — the marathon construction earned it the nickname "building of the century", and its look and owner the other one, "the iron fist of the regime". Architectural historians see the opposite in the pyramid: a work of the era when architecture began to free itself from ideological pressure, continuing the pre-war tradition.

![Slovak Radio Building (Slovenský rozhlas)](/assets/slovensky-rozhlas.jpg)

The upward-widening volume hangs from a steel frame — a construction exceedingly rare for its time. Experimental broadcasting began here in 1984, regular broadcasting in 1985. Inside there is more than studios: the building's concert hall holds one of Slovakia's largest organs, classical and alternative concerts run here to this day, and the ground floor houses a gallery open to the public — the notable exhibition "New Slovak Image" took place here in 1988. The pyramid stands a block from [[Namestie-slobody|Freedom Square]], forming with it the city's most concentrated "socialist" ensemble.

The author duo of Svetko and Ďurkovič left the city a very different building as well — Vrakuňa's [[Pentagon]]: two extremes of one era from the same hands.
