# The Park of Culture and Leisure — PKO (Park kultúry a oddychu)

The Park of Culture and Leisure (Park kultúry a oddychu, PKO) was a legendary cultural complex on the Danube embankment on the way to Karlova Ves, demolished in the 2010s; for several generations of Bratislavans it was the place of first dances, concerts and riverside walks. The site grew out of the International Danube Fair, which had drawn visitors from all over Europe since 1921: in 1943 a kilometre-long terrace was built up on the bank from rock excavated for the tunnel under the castle hill, and in 1951–1954 exhibition pavilions and the main building rose to the design of architects Pavol Andrík, Ján Štefanec and Kamil Gross. The Danube entrance was guarded by two statues in folk costume by Tibor Bártfay beneath sgraffito decoration; the vestibule had a floor of coloured marble and 28 stained-glass windows after sketches by Janko Alexy, and on the wall hung František Gajdoš's monumental Harvest Festival. The whole city helped finish PKO: by the end of 1954 over 35,000 volunteer brigade workers had earned "Builder of PKO" badges for their hours of labour.

![The PKO entrance from the Danube side](/assets/pko.jpg)

Opened in April 1955, the complex received almost 200,000 visitors in its first year: the chemical trade fair (until Incheba opened in 1973), the Coneco and Flóra exhibitions, the Bratislava Lyre festival, Slovakia's biggest jazz festival, the famous dance-hall parquet, regional compatriot balls, a summer stage and minigolf by the river. In 2005 the city hall sold the buildings and land to the developer Henbury Development; citizens protested, the city litigated and spoke of buying the land back, but in April 2009 demolition began, and seven years later the legendary halls were gone for good. Today the River Park development stands on the site; of PKO there remain photographs and the city's memory.
