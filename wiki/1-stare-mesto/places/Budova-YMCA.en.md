# YMCA Building (Budova YMCA)

The YMCA Building (Budova YMCA) at the corner of Karpatská and Šancová streets is **the first house of the worldwide YMCA youth movement built in continental Europe** — and at the same time the cradle of Slovak sport: it was here that basketball, volleyball, table tennis and fencing were first played in Slovakia. A national cultural monument (since 2005) — and one of the most underfunded shrines of the city's memory.

![YMCA Building (Budova YMCA)](/assets/budova-ymca.jpg)

The plot was donated after the First World War by the old bachelor Baron Georg von Schulpe, and the construction was paid for by the American YMCA; the design came from Czech architects working in Bratislava, [[Grossmann-Jiri|Jiří Grossmann]] and [[Balan-Alois|Alois Balán]]. The house with lodgings, offices, a gymnasium and a spacious hall was completed in 1921 (ceremonially opened, by other accounts, in 1923); its unplastered red-brick facades echoed the Zehnhaus workers' colony opposite and the nearby Menzel asphalt factory. Community life thrived: in the winter months around 1930 the library received up to 600 readers a day, and the ground floor held Slovakia's first self-service canteen.

After 1948 the building was taken away — the socialist youth union occupied it, in 1951 the house was nationalised, and the great hall became the Dukla cinema: in 1964 **Czechoslovakia's first panoramic cinema** opened here (after 1989 — the YMCA cinema). Restitution dragged on for decades — the court case between the administering foundation and YMCA na Slovensku ended only in October 2014, in the YMCA's favour. The century-old building is decaying: a comprehensive reconstruction needs about 4 million euros, and the grants received sufficed only for the collapsing sidewalk; yet the house lives — it holds the A4 contemporary culture space, the Majestic and Randal music clubs, the Dezorz puppet theatre, the Stoka theatre and a dozen associations.
