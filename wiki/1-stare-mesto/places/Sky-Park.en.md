# Sky Park

Sky Park is a multifunctional high-rise quarter between Čulenova, Bottova and Továrenská streets, the most recognizable piece of Bratislava's new downtown and Zaha Hadid's posthumous work in Slovakia. Four identical 31-storey residential towers by Zaha Hadid Architects, with flowing concrete "skirts", wear facades of a unique matte bronze colour mixed specially for the project; the first three were completed in 2020. They are joined by the 18-storey Sky Park Offices tower (Vietzke & Borstelmann Architekten, 2021; LEED Gold and WELL Gold certificates) and the rising 119-metre Sky Park Tower, while between the towers lies a park of more than 35 thousand m² designed by Zaha Hadid Architects, Townshend Landscape Architects and architect Igor Marko.

![Sky Park — the Zaha Hadid towers](/assets/sky-park.jpg)

The quarter stands on industrial land with a dramatic history: the Apollo refinery — "Bratislava's Apolka" — worked here until American bombers struck it on 16 June 1944 (the Winter Harbour and the [[Stary-most|Danube Bridge]] were bombed the same day; the [[Most-Apollo|Apollo Bridge]] also keeps the plant's name). After the war production was partly restored, in 1949 the company split into Slovnaft and Benzinol, and the site served the growing Slovnaft until 1963, when it was demolished. The only surviving building is the [[Jurkovicova-teplaren|Jurkovič Heating Plant]] (a national monument since 2008): during the construction of Sky Park it was restored as a public space, preserving the roof structure, the coal hoppers, a restored crane and 31,736 bricks of the historic facades.
