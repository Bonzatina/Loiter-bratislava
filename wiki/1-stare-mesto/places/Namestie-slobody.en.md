# Freedom Square (Námestie slobody)

Freedom Square (Námestie slobody) is one of Bratislava's largest squares, a concrete quadrangle of roughly 200 by 200 metres north of the old town and the city's chief monument to the square-building aesthetics of socialism. At its centre is the city's largest fountain, Družba ("Friendship"): a basin 45 metres across with a nine-metre, twelve-ton steel linden flower — the symbol of the Slavs.

![Freedom Square (Námestie slobody)](/assets/namestie-slobody.jpg)

In the Middle Ages vineyards grew here; the square probably arose in the 17th century together with the [[Letny-arcibiskupsky-palac|Archbishop's Summer Palace]] (today the Government Office of the Slovak Republic). The lawn was split in two by a princely alley — the Fürstenallee, from which the square's folk name Firšnál was born. Until 1948 this was the city's largest open space, until the buildings of the 1940s–50s — the Postal Palace and the blocks of the Technical University — closed it in on all sides. Under socialism the square was called Gottwald Square ("Gotko"): a late-1970s project by the Design Institute of Culture rolled it in concrete with flowers in concrete planters (budgeted at 25 million crowns), and on 13 November 1980, with Gustáv Husák in attendance, the square was opened together with a six-metre sculptural group of Klement Gottwald surrounded by workers.

After the revolution the square was among the first to be renamed (officially from 1 April 1991), and on 25 November 1990 pyrotechnicians removed the Gottwald group with a single controlled blast; its place in the square's memory was taken over time by monuments to Karel Kryl (after 1994) and Gisi Fleischmann (2017). The main memorial is on its way: the Memorial to the Democratic Revolution of 1989 — a monumental pedestal set into a replica of the "anti-crowd" raised planter opposite today's Government Office, with castings of shoe soles (the traces of protesters who trampled such planters of Gottwald Square in November 1989) and the inscription November; the competition-winning design is by Lackovič, Zatlukaj and Trajter, with the unveiling set for 17 November. The historian Radoslav Ragač calls placing the memorial on the site of the Gottwald group a symbolic gesture of democracy's victory over totalitarian power. The Družba fountain stood dry from 2007 to 2023 — the reconstruction begun in 2021 brought it back to life: the broken paving was replaced by exterior terrazzo made of the recycled old slabs and the fused-basalt tiles of the basin itself, the green islets became places to linger, and a space for events and demonstrations appeared in front of the faculty building. The barrier-free access right into the fountain's basin did not please everyone, though ("a lido does not belong in front of the Government Office"). Today the square lives with concerts, rallies and protests — and next door rises another symbol of the era, the [[Slovensky-rozhlas|upside-down pyramid of the Slovak Radio]].
