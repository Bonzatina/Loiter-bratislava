# Franciscan Church (Františkánsky kostol)

The Church of the Annunciation with the friary of the Friars Minor (commonly the Franciscan Church) is **the oldest surviving sacred building in Bratislava**. It stands on Františkánske námestie, two steps from the [[Hlavne-namestie|Main Square]], and is of interest not only for its architecture but for the several major historical episodes tied to the place.

![Franciscan Church (Františkánsky kostol)](/assets/frantiskansky-kostol.jpg)

The friary came first: around 1220 the Franciscans began building it on the northern edge of the town then standing, and the work dragged on to the end of the 13th century. The church itself was begun only in the second half of that century. Its founder is held to be the Hungarian king Ladislaus IV the Cuman, who ordered it built in memory of the victory over the Bohemian king Přemysl Otakar II at the Battle on the Marchfeld in 1278. It was raised in a Romanesque-Gothic manner and consecrated with great ceremony on 24 March 1297, in the presence of Ladislaus's successor Andrew III, the Archbishop of Esztergom and a great crowd of Pressburgers.

The building then lived through era after era. The earthquakes of 1580 and 1586 brought down the vault of the nave; a new, by then renaissance, vault took more than thirty years, and in the following century the present baroque one replaced it. Of the original Gothic church only the presbytery and part of the outer walls of the main nave survive today. The facade was rebuilt in the baroque style in 1745–1746 by the builder Lucca de Schramm, and the stone Immaculate Virgin with two angels above the portal is by the Pressburg sculptor Jozef Sartory — the same who freely reworked the [[Morovy-stlp-Rybne-namestie|plague column on Rybné námestie]]. The inscription above the entrance reads VIRGINI DEI PARAE SACRUM.

The most valuable thing inside is the chapel of St John the Evangelist, a two-storey Gothic burial chapel of 1360–1380 against the northern wall, counted among the finest sacred structures in all Slovakia for its artistic treatment. The tower has a story of its own: the six-sided Gothic tower was added to the south side of the nave in 1410–1420 and stood in its original form until 1897. Structural failure was discovered then, its upper part was dismantled — and it has served ever since as a garden pavilion in Petržalka's [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Garden]], while a faithful neo-Gothic copy stands on the church. There is also a baroque chapel of Our Lady of Loreto (the Black Madonna) of 1708, a simplified repetition of Bramante's "Holy House" in Loreto in Italy: it was founded by the Hungarian palatine Prince Pavol Esterházy.

The church's historical role went well beyond the parish. In 1578 townsmen outraged by the magistrate's injustices gathered here; in 1601 the Hungarian diet sat in the friary. And after every coronation in [[Dom-svateho-Martina|St Martin's Cathedral]], the newly crowned king came here to dub selected Hungarian nobles Knights of the Golden Spur.
