# Agáta Memorial Plaque (Pamätná tabuľa Agáte)

Set into the paving of Hurbanovo námestie, where it meets Župné námestie and a few steps from [[Michalska-veza|St Michael's Gate]], lies a pale stone slab with a hand-drawn inscription: "On this spot, on 24 May 1602, … Toott Borlobaschin was the first person in Bratislava burnt for witchcraft." It is the city's only memorial to the witch trials — and, in effect, the only marker standing on one of [[justice-in-presporok|Prešporok's places of execution]].

![Agáta Memorial Plaque (Pamätná tabuľa Agáte)](/assets/tabula-agaty.jpg)

Agatha Toott Borlobaschin came from [[2-podunajske-biskupice|Podunajské Biskupice]] and is considered the first documented victim of the witch hunts in Pressburg. She was denounced, according to the surviving accounts, by a butcher's wife who called her neighbour a witch and a murderer; under torture Agáta "confessed" to intercourse with the devil, to flying on a broomstick and to striking a woman blind. She was burnt at the moat before St Michael's Gate on 24 May 1602.

A bronze relief in her memory was commissioned by the Uroboros society; the work of the sculptor Ľudmila Cvengrošová was unveiled on 24 May 2002, on the four-hundredth anniversary of the execution. In the first half of 2014 the plaque vanished and its fate was never established. Five years later, on 12 July 2019, a stone replica modelled on the original returned to the spot: it was cut by the stonemason Roman Kovrižnych with the support of the SLARS association and on the initiative of the city guide Zuzana Slavena Matei. The same theme is carried on in the city by the "Bosorka" sculpture on the castle hill.
