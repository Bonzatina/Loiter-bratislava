# Ján Kuciak & Martina Kušnírová Memorial

The Ján Kuciak & Martina Kušnírová Memorial (Pamätník Jána Kuciaka a Martiny Kušnírovej) on SNP Square commemorates the double murder that changed Slovakia. On 21 February 2018 the investigative journalist Ján Kuciak and his fiancée Martina Kušnírová, both 27, were shot dead in their home; there was no sign of struggle or robbery.

![Ján Kuciak & Martina Kušnírová Memorial](/assets/pamatnik-kuciaka.jpg)

Kuciak had made his name investigating the tax frauds of a businessman close to the ruling SMER-SD party, and at the time of the murder he was working on a report about Slovak politicians' ties to the 'Ndrangheta, one of the most feared Italian crime organisations. News of the killing brought people into the streets: the "For a Decent Slovakia" rallies became the largest protests since the Velvet Revolution and led to the government's resignation. The gunmen and go-betweens — Marček, Szabó, Andruskó, Zsuzsová — received 15 to 25 years; the businessman Marian Kočner was accused of ordering the murder and acquitted in that case, but convicted of forging 75 million dollars' worth of promissory notes, while Zsuzsová was also charged with the 2010 murder of a southern Slovak mayor.

The memorial on SNP Square — beside the Uprising monument where the protests gathered — keeps the couple's names and faces; the metal quill growing from a crack in the pavement became a symbol of unbowed journalism.
