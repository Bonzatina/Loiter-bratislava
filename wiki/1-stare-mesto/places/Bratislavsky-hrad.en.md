# Bratislava Castle (Bratislavský hrad)

Bratislava Castle (Bratislavský hrad) is the castle on the hill above the Danube, the city's defining silhouette and a national cultural monument. The most dramatic chapter of its biography is the fire: before dawn on 28 May 1811 flames broke out in the Winter Riding Hall, a strong wind drove the fire across the shingle roofs, and soon, as the Pressburger Zeitung wrote, "the colossal building stood all at once entirely in flames". Feeble pumps, short hoses and the chaos of the firefighting left no chance for the Royal Palace or its surroundings — the fire spread to the Podhradie as well, where it claimed victims and wrought enormous destruction. For almost a century and a half the castle became a ruin.

![Bratislava Castle from the Danube](/assets/bratislavsky-hrad.jpg)

For the ruin's rescue the city is indebted to the preservationist architect Alfred Piffl (1907–1972), a native of Kerhartice in Bohemia, who together with the painter Janko Alexy tirelessly fought the advocates of demolishing the "reprehensible" wreck. Piffl recorded the approval of the reconstruction in his diary on 28 May 1953 — the very anniversary of the fire; gratitude never came: the regime sent the castle's saviour to prison. The latest reconstruction of 2008–2011 returned the castle to its appearance from the reign of Maria Theresa: the white four-towered palace shines above the river, the queen's state rooms serve the National Council of the Slovak Republic (opened for guided tours on the fire's anniversaries), and the other wings house the [[Historicke-muzeum-SNM|SNM Museum of History]].
