# Ferry Wharfs of the Propeler

The "Propeler" is a Bratislava institution: a little ferry that shuttled between the banks of the Danube for decades. For it, in 1930, the young [[Bellus-Emil|Emil Belluš]] built two one-storey wharf buildings — on the left bank by Ľudovít Štúr Square (Námestie Ľudovíta Štúra) and on the right, Petržalka bank by the Vienna road (Viedenská cesta). Each held a waiting room and a ticket booth; above the flat roof of the left-bank pavilion stood the sign PROPELLER with a clock.

![Ferry wharf of the Propeler, archival photo](/assets/propeler.jpg)

This is pure early [[functionalism]]: a light frame, glass walls, nothing superfluous — the form dictated entirely by the operational logic of the crossing. Both buildings survive; the left-bank one was later reimagined by architect Ján Bahna, with a restaurant inside. The pavilions remain perhaps Belluš's most intimate work in the city — and a pleasant find for anyone strolling the embankment.
