# Obchodná Street (Obchodná ulica)

Obchodná ("Trade Street") is one of Bratislava's oldest streets, a bustling shopping-and-tram artery between Hurbanovo námestie and Kollárovo námestie. It was once called Uhorská ("Hungarian") and Schöndorfská — after the suburban village of Schöndorf that stood on this very spot. Its residents were once mostly winegrowers who sold their own wine right in the wide gateway passages of their houses; in 1772 Pressburg's first pawnshop opened on the street, and by 1899 a tram was already running along it.

![Obchodná Street (Obchodná ulica)](/assets/obchodna-ulica.jpg)

The street's beginning at Hurbanovo námestie was caught by the era of the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]]: the dilapidated house at Obchodná 3 — in January 1974 it still held a barber's, a watch-repair shop and a tobacconist's — soon vanished; the city explained it thus: "the construction of the new thoroughfare across Bratislava leading to the SNP Bridge requires the removal of several parts of old Bratislava." In 1981 a new tram line was opened along the street — the first stage of the city's second west–east link, meant to relieve the overloaded Špitálska. A singular monument to the street was created by photographer Ľubo Stacho: for thirty years (1984–2014) he photographed its shop windows, which reflected not goods but the era — from mourning for Soviet general secretaries to the changes after 1990, when it was no longer the displays that changed but the shops themselves: houses gained new storeys, courtyards opened to the city.
