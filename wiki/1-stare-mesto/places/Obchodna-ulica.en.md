# Obchodná Street (Obchodná ulica)

Obchodná ("Trade Street") is one of Bratislava's oldest streets, a bustling shopping-and-tram artery between Hurbanovo námestie and Kollárovo námestie. It was once called Uhorská ("Hungarian") and Schöndorfská — after the suburban village of Schöndorf that stood on this very spot. Its residents were once mostly winegrowers who sold their own wine right in the wide gateway passages of their houses; in 1772 Pressburg's first pawnshop opened on the street, and by 1899 a tram was already running along it. The street has a spicy medieval chapter too: somewhere hereabouts stood the White House (Biely dom) — the town's famous brothel, owned by the magistrate itself (its proceeds funded church institutions and hospitals, among other things) and overseen by the town executioner, the "cleanser of the town from evil". The idea was purely practical: crowds of soldiers and journeymen passed through Pressburg, and the townsmen preferred to keep them away from their wives.

![Obchodná Street (Obchodná ulica)](/assets/obchodna-ulica.jpg)

At the crossing with Poštová the street lost one of the townspeople's best-loved spots — the "jama" (the Pit): an ornamental mini-park in a deep hollow opposite the then Hotel Palace, where Bratislavans came to rest, children to play, and in winter to sled down its steep slopes; old-timers also recall the excellent ice cream nearby. In August 1954 this very "jama" made the photo archives: a warm-up by Chinese basketball players staying at the Palace drew a crowd of onlookers on Obchodná — then, incidentally, named after Molotov. In the 1970s the pit was filled in: first for a car park, then for the construction site of the Hotel Forum (1987–1989, today's Crowne Plaza), behind which only a small square remains toward Obchodná. The Palace itself on the corner of Poštová and Obchodná — built in 1930 as the Karas department store (Karasov obchodný dom), with the legendary Čas cinema and Bratislava's first fast food, the late-1980s Gurmán — served as a hotel until 1992, then became a bank, and today holds offices.

![The "jama" at the corner of Obchodná and Poštová, 1954](/assets/jama-obchodna.jpg)

The street's beginning at Hurbanovo námestie was caught by the era of the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]]: the dilapidated house at Obchodná 3 — in January 1974 it still held a barber's, a watch-repair shop and a tobacconist's — soon vanished; the city explained it thus: "the construction of the new thoroughfare across Bratislava leading to the SNP Bridge requires the removal of several parts of old Bratislava." In 1981 a new tram line was opened along the street — the first stage of the city's second west–east link, meant to relieve the overloaded Špitálska. A singular monument to the street was created by photographer Ľubo Stacho: for thirty years (1984–2014) he photographed its shop windows, which reflected not goods but the era — from mourning for Soviet general secretaries to the changes after 1990, when it was no longer the displays that changed but the shops themselves: houses gained new storeys, courtyards opened to the city.
