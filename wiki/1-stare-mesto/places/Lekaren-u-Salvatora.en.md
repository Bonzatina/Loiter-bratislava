# Salvator Pharmacy (Lekáreň u Salvatora)

The Salvator Pharmacy (Lekáreň u Salvatora) at Panská 35 is a neo-Renaissance house of 1904 built by the apothecary Rudolf Adler, with a stone statue of Christ the Saviour (Latin Salvator) by [[Rigele-Alojz|Alojz Rigele]] on its facade; a protected monument since 1963. But the house's chief treasure is not the facade — it is what stood inside: **the Baroque furnishings of a Jesuit pharmacy from around 1727**, whose oldest parts go back to 1658, when archbishop Juraj Lippay gave the Jesuits a pharmacy.

![Salvator Pharmacy (Lekáreň u Salvatora)](/assets/lekaren-u-salvatora.jpg)

The fate of this furniture is a ready-made novel. After the order's dissolution it was sold at auction (1775, apothecary Karol Sessel), in 1833 it moved to Count Csáky's palace, and from 1884 it belonged to Adler, who in 1904 moved the pharmacy to the ground floor of his new house — recreating the officina on the model of the Jesuit residence, with a stucco ceiling and four allegorical paintings (one painted by the apothecary's daughter). The pharmacy worked here until 1995 — and then the furnishings passed into private hands, were dismantled and taken out of Bratislava. A sale to Switzerland was thwarted at the last moment by the culture ministry, which declared the furniture a national monument; for fifteen years it lay in a warehouse in Trenčianske Teplice, until in 2010 it was bought by the pharmacist-collector Erik Kovács. He fully restored the ensemble and built a house for it in Nové Mesto nad Váhom with an exact copy of the officina — down to the stucco and floor tiles — opening a Museum of Pharmacy there in 2018.

The return began when in May 2020 the city obtained the house on Panská in full ownership: on 15 October 2021 the magistrate signed a contract to buy the furnishings back for 990 thousand euros before VAT. "We saved the pharmacy, restored it and are returning it to Bratislava, where more visitors will see it," said Kovács, who by his own account turned down higher offers. Among the old officina's inscriptions there is one that fits this story: "Whatever you do, do it wisely and remember the end."
