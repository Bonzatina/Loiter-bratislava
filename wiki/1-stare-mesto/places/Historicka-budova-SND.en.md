# Historic SND Building (Historická budova SND)

The historic building of the Slovak National Theatre (Historická budova SND) closes [[Hviezdoslavovo-namestie|Hviezdoslav Square]] from the east — a neo-renaissance theatre by the Viennese architects Ferdinand Fellner and Hermann Helmer, a national cultural monument and the ceremonial facade of theatrical Bratislava. The Slovak National Theatre has lived here since 1920 (today its opera and ballet).

![Historic SND Building (Historická budova SND)](/assets/historicka-budova-snd.jpg)

A theatre has stood on this spot since 1776, when Count Juraj Csáky built the city stage to a design by Matej Walch. The present building is the work of the famous pair Fellner and Helmer, who built an incredible 45 theatres across Europe in their signature Central European baroque-renaissance blend "with a French flavour"; the Bratislava theatre is one of them. Despite the opulent complexity (monumental twin staircases in a single space, offset by half a storey), the builder Ignác Feigler the Younger — the same who raised the [[Hotel-Carlton|Carlton]] and the [[Palugyayov-palac|Palugyay Palace]] — finished in a record two years: from August 1884 to September 1886. The opening took place on 22 September 1886; the interior was lit by 800 gas lamps. At first the Municipal Theatre served visiting German and Hungarian companies; in 1920 it became the home of the SND.

The interior was adorned with K. Spányi's allegories in the foyer and paintings by Baron Lüttgendorf-Leinburg in the auditorium; above the portal stands the stone muse Thalia by the Viennese sculptor T. Friedl and putti with the symbols of tragedy and comedy from Viktor Tilgner's workshop, and in front of the building — Tilgner's Ganymede fountain. The facade busts have a drama of their own: the five by W. Marhenke (Liszt, Shakespeare, Goethe, Katona, Vörösmarty) were taken down in 1936 to make way for Slovak-Czech classics — the new ones were never made, the old ones lay in the basement until 1972 (Vörösmarty's went missing), and only in 2003 did copies return to the facade, the lost bust replaced by Ivan Herényi's Mozart. The building was remodelled for the stage's growing needs in 1935 and, after war damage, in 1949; the major reconstruction of 1969–1972 added a modern annex on the Komenského square side. The auditorium seats 609.
