# Slavín — Memorial and War Cemetery

Slavín is the memorial and war cemetery of Soviet soldiers who fell liberating Bratislava and Western Slovakia, on the hill of the same name above the Old Town's villa quarter. The ensemble, designed by architect Ján Svetlík, was built in 1957–1960 and unveiled on 4 April 1960, the fifteenth anniversary of the city's liberation; since 1961 it has been a national cultural monument. Above the ceremonial hall rises a pylon of about 40 metres, crowned by an 11-metre statue of a flag-bearing soldier by Alexander Trizuljak. A persistent legend holds that the sculptor hid a motif in the figure: the flagstaff and the lowered submachine gun form the silhouette of a cross — a quiet gesture in an atheist era; the same play with a cross appears in the relief on the [[Najvyssi-sud-SR|Supreme Court]] building.

![Trizuljak's flag-bearer atop the Slavín pylon](/assets/slavin.jpg)

It is not just a monument but a real cemetery: about seven thousand Red Army soldiers rest in six mass graves and 317 individual plots. By no means all of them died in Bratislava itself — the city was liberated on 4 April 1945, and the fallen were brought here from all over Western Slovakia, among them some 120 soldiers from the battle for Trnava on 1 April 1945. The memorial's terrace is one of the city's best lookouts: the whole centre, the Danube and the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]] spread out below. The statue has suffered lightning strikes more than once, and the gilded star and flagstaff have been restored.
