# Bratislava Calvary (Bratislavská kalvária)

The Bratislava Calvary (Bratislavská kalvária) above Hlboká cesta, by the Church of Our Lady of the Snows and Horský park, is a pilgrimage way with more than three centuries of history. It was conceived in 1694 by the Jesuit rector Albert Mechtl as a monument to the victory over the Ottomans at Vienna — the cross was raised where a Turkish camp had once stood. Over thirty-odd years, by 1725, a baroque complex of nine chapel-stations had grown, leading the pilgrim from the Mount of Olives to Golgotha.

![The cross on the Bratislava Calvary](/assets/kalvaria.jpg)

The 20th century all but destroyed the Calvary: after the war, especially in the 1950s, part of the hillside with its chapels was literally dug away for the construction of Pražská street, and vandalism and neglect finished off the rest. Revival came from the citizens: the Bratislava Calvary association and the architects — the Hantabal couple — built minimalist modern stations along the historic route, respecting the proportions of the old chapels, and returned crosses and lighting to Golgotha. The work won the Dušan Jurkovič Prize (2024) and a CE.ZA.AR nomination, and at Easter 2026 the renewed Calvary celebrated for the first time after its consecration.

The Calvary also has a detective story — the fate of the statue of St Veronica. When artworks were rescued from the ravaged Calvary in the 1970s, only a badly damaged torso reached the [[Muzeum-mesta-Bratislavy|City Museum]] — missing its head, limbs and lower half. Half a century later, in 2023, the missing lower part was found under a layer of earth by the original station — someone had probably hidden it there deliberately decades before. Restorer Samuel Horváth joined the finds together, and from historical photographs a copy of the statue was made, which returned to its original place above Pražská street for the Calvary's 330th anniversary.
