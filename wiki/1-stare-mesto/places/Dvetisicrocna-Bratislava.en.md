# Two-Thousand-Year Bratislava (Dvetisícročná Bratislava)

Two-Thousand-Year Bratislava (Dvetisícročná Bratislava) is a set of five monumental concrete reliefs by sculptor Juraj Meliš (1979–1980) on the retaining wall along the descent from the main station to the centre (Štefanovičova 2). For newcomers it is a symbolic gate into the city's history and identity, for locals — a digest of their own roots right on a busy street. The wall, curved in a half-arc, follows the slope; between the five panels, planes of raw concrete are left as rhythmic pauses.

![Two-Thousand-Year Bratislava — the reliefs on the retaining wall (Dvetisícročná Bratislava)](/assets/dvetisicrocna-bratislava.jpg)

Meliš, who worked in Bratislava from the start of his studies at the Academy of Fine Arts in 1960, usually carved raw wood — and here, in the white concrete of the sunken relief, a close look reveals the imprints of formwork boards. He verified the content with historians Blanka Puškárová and Pavol Dvořák and architect Imrich Puškár. The panels run chronologically: the first from the station — Celtic, Slavic and Roman settlement; the second — the Middle Ages; the third and fourth — architectural monuments of later eras (the Water Barracks, the [[Stara-radnica|Town Hall]], the Reduta, the Primate's Palace, the [[Bratislavsky-hrad|Castle]], Academia Istropolitana); the fifth, the most ascetic, carries the names of the oldest communes in a geometric grid of varied typefaces, with historic inscriptions — Meliš's signature device. The ensemble is signed "Meliš 80" (panels up to 360 × 378 cm); its condition is good, though the concrete begs to be cleaned of graffiti. Curiously, in 1985 Meliš made a similar "historical" series for his native Nové Zámky — this time in his own wood and with paints, inside the Mier cinema.
