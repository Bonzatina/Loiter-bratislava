# TETA Department Store (Obchodný dom TETA)

The TETA Department Store (Obchodný dom TETA) on Dunajská street is regarded as **the city's first department store** (1929): designed by architect Alexander Skutezky, its builder Armin Hecht also put up the house at Grösslingová 9 — the two blocks, in clean functionalist forms, were linked by an open passage. The name TE-TA is composed of the surnames of its owners — the merchants Teweles and Taussig, who kept such "unit-price" stores across Czechoslovakia; the brand was deliberately fitted to Czech and Slovak ("teta" means aunt; the Balkan branches traded as TATA — "father"). It sold clothing, footwear, leather goods and housewares; the Kamzík by the Praha cinema (today's BILLA) later opened on the same model.

![TETA: the 1962 queue and the Teta sign](/assets/obchodny-dom-teta.jpg)

The house's second claim to fame came in 1962: a Veľkopredajňa potravín — a large self-service food store — opened on the ground floor, with a crowd stretching to Námestie SNP (the Prior department store did not yet exist). The "Teta" was loved for its metropolitan polish — espresso bars by the entrances, branded Vinea and Pepsi instead of generic pop — and for a unique trait of the "grocery passage": you could walk through the shop from Dunajská to Grösslingová (then Red Army street). In 1994 a scene of the cult film "On the Beautiful Blue Danube" was shot in the Teta. Groceries under the Teta name lived here for half a century, until 2012; now these walls hold a LIDL — the through-passage is closed and nothing of the original interior remains, which even mayor Matúš Vallo publicly lamented: "new grocery chains steamroll the past, with no regard for what creates a sense of place."
