# Metropol Residential Building and Cinema (Obytný dom a kino Metropol)

*Metropolka*, as the city calls it, is the corner house between Špitálska and Mickiewiczova streets by Americké námestie. People walk past it daily and few know its story: when it was built it counted among Czechoslovakia's architectural gems and drew attention abroad, and its café was where the Slovak cultural milieu of the interwar years took shape.

![Metropol Residential Building and Cinema (Obytný dom a kino Metropol)](/assets/metropol.jpg)

[[Tvarozek-Juraj|Juraj Tvarožek]] drew the project in 1927 and the house was built in 1928, financed by his brother Tomáš. It is conservatively minded modernism: a corner building of two wings with flat roofs meeting at a bevelled, rounded corner, with a clearly stated base, body and cornice, and a regular grid of near-square windows in thin stone frames. Inside, the plan was busier than the façade: two basement levels held the cinema, the ground floor the Metropol café, the upper floors flats of two to four rooms, and the cinema interiors were done in Art Deco. Descriptions differ on whether to read it as five storeys per wing or six with two basements, on a closed three-wing layout with a corner stairwell. It was declared a national cultural monument in 1985.

Café Metropol quickly became a hub of social life, where artists, writers, architects and public figures of the First Republic met. After the Slovak State was proclaimed, the café's free-thinking character made it inconvenient to the ľudák regime, and the Tvarožek family joined the democratic resistance — first against that regime, then against Nazism. The post-war respite was brief: under the communists several family members went to prison, and one of the best-known trials bore the name "Tvarožek a spol." and ended in exceptionally harsh sentences. The house meanwhile lost its milieu — the spaces changed purpose, the cinema closed in the early 1990s, and the 1997 reconstruction (Ján Miloslav Bahna and Ľubomír Závodný; the café interior by Závodný and Matej Siebert) adapted part of the floor area for a bank, moving the café up to the first floor.

In 2024 the BEEF architekti studio (Rado Buzinkay, Andrej Ferenčík, Peter Baroš) converted 559 m² of the building into co-living — a deliberate nod to its former social role. The result is 19 flats of 16 to 35 m², with a shared kitchen, chill-out zone and laundry on the first floor. Authenticity is left in view: the exposed ribbed concrete ceilings and the building's distinctive blue windows are preserved, and glass blocks in the bathrooms bring in secondary light. A memorial plaque on the façade recalls both the architecture and the fates of those who built it.
