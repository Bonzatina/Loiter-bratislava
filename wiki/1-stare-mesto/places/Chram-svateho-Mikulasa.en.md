# Church of St Nicholas (Chrám sv. Mikuláša)

The Orthodox Church of St Nicholas (Chrám sv. Mikuláša) on Mikulášska street is an early Baroque church of 1661 on the tourist path under the castle — and at the same time one of the city's most neglected treasures: a national cultural monument on the special priority-protection list that has looked forgotten for decades. The church was built in 1661 by Františka Khuen, widow of palatine Pálffy — hence the relief with the Pálffy family arms above the entrance portal.

![Church of St Nicholas (Chrám sv. Mikuláša)](/assets/chram-svateho-mikulasa.jpg)

Until 1953 the church was used by the Greek Catholics (Uniates); after the Uniate church was abolished, it was sold for a symbolic crown to the Orthodox community. Inside, a rare iconostasis painted around 1930 by the Ždanovský family — Russian émigrés — is slowly decaying; the crypts under the church were looted back in the 19th and 20th centuries, and their entrance is now bricked up — nothing of value remains there, the Orthodox Eparchy of Prešov assures. Money for repairs was found before, but kept "getting lost": timbers bought to shore up the church lay on an upper floor for decades, and the marble slabs donated by a Bulgarian businessman were eventually taken back by their owner; thirty years ago a general reconstruction was estimated at nearly 20 million crowns.

Still, there is good news: services have resumed — at least four a week, and on Thursdays the church is open all day to ordinary visitors too (on other days as circumstances allow, in the afternoons). The eparchy is preparing the project documentation for a reconstruction based on the architectural-historical and art-historical research already carried out; hopes rest on the European Recovery Plan and culture-ministry grants. The war in Ukraine has added urgency: hundreds of Orthodox refugees in Bratislava need a church of the rite they know — the little Church of St Rastislav on Tomášikova cannot hold them all.
