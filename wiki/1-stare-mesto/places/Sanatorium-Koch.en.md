# Koch Sanatorium (Kochovo sanatórium)

The Koch Sanatorium (Kochovo sanatórium) at Partizánska 27 was built in 1929–1930 to a design by [[Jurkovic-Dusan|Dušan Jurkovič]] with Jindřich Merganc and Otmar Klimeš. For Jurkovič, the "poet of timber", this was a rare venture into pure [[functionalism]] — and an exemplary one at once: on completion the clinic was regarded as the most modern in all of Czechoslovakia.

![Koch Sanatorium (Kochovo sanatórium)](/assets/sanatorium-koch.jpg)

The four-storey building with an extended V-shaped plan — three sections along a central corridor — turns its rear balconies towards the garden. The concrete skeleton with brick infill is clad in ceramic tiles with a stressed horizontal joint; large windows and a central ribbon window flood the wards with light. The private women's clinic was founded by Professor Karol Koch: 36 beds, an operating theatre, a consulting room and a small chapel. Behind the house the gardener Jozef Mišík planted a garden with a rich collection of tree species — valuable in its own right.

The building still serves medicine — today it houses a maternity ward.
