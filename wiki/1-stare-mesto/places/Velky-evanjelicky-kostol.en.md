# Great Lutheran Church (Veľký evanjelický kostol)

The Great Lutheran Church (Veľký evanjelický kostol) on Panenská street is Bratislava's principal Lutheran church and the most eloquent monument of an age when a Protestant church in the Habsburg monarchy was permitted to exist but not to be seen. It was built in 1774–1776 to a design by the builder Matej Walch in an austere baroque classicism, on the site of an earlier wooden church that had long since become too small for the city's German Lutheran congregation. Permission is credited to Joseph II, then co-regent with Maria Theresa, and it came with conditions: the church was to have no tower and was not to be ornate.

![Great Lutheran Church (Veľký evanjelický kostol)](/assets/velky-evanjelicky-kostol.jpg)

Hence its whole appearance: from the street it is a large white house under an enormous hipped roof, with no belfry, no declamatory portal and almost no ornament — and that enforced restraint became its character. Inside, the roof conceals a monumental central hall with two tiers of galleries, covered by five vaulted bays that give the space excellent acoustics; the roof truss itself, cut by a Hamburg carpenter whose name is not recorded, is considered unique on a European scale. The church received Slovakia's first pulpit altar (the pulpit set directly above the altar — a typically Lutheran solution, in which the sermon outranks the image), and the altar painting was donated by a native of the city, the painter Adam Friedrich Oeser.

The present organ is the third instrument here: it was built in 1923 by the Gebrüder Rieger–Jägerndorf firm, its design entrusted to the organist G. Rhode and the architect [[Ludwig-Christian|Christian Ludwig]] — if the dating holds, one of Ludwig's earliest works in the city. The hall's acoustics have made the church a standing concert venue, and it has been protected as a national cultural monument since 23 October 1963. Alongside it, in the same Lutheran precinct between Panenská and Konventná, stand the Small Lutheran Church and the lyceum building — the quarter to which [[Lutherov-dom|Luther House]], built by the congregation in the 1930s, also belongs.
