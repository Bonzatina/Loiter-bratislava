# Johann Nepomuk Hummel Monument (Pomník Jána Nepomuka Hummela)

Monuments are usually counted as immovable cultural property — Bratislava has proved that the same monument can be moved five times in a single century. The one in question honours perhaps the world's best-known native of Pressburg, the composer Johann Nepomuk Hummel (1778–1837), and is the work of another local son, Viktor Tilgner (1844–1896) — the same whose putti and Ganymede fountain adorn the [[Historicka-budova-SND|historic SND building]].

![Johann Nepomuk Hummel Monument (Pomník Jána Nepomuka Hummela)](/assets/pomnik-hummela.jpg)

The composer's stature was so well understood in his home city in the 19th century that it resolved to give him a worthy monument. It was to stand in front of the Municipal Theatre, but the old theatre had to be pulled down and a fountain was planned in front of the new one — so the monument was placed before the theatre, facing the fountain, on a low grassy mound that added to its monumentality. It was unveiled on 16 October 1887. It is made of Carrara marble, with a larger-than-life bronze bust; two putti complete the pedestal. The monument was raised by the masonic lodge Silence, which charged its member, the Pressburg archivist Johann Nepomuk Batka, with raising the money — he solved the task by organising benefit concerts and art exhibitions.

The moves began in 1904: the monument was shifted to a nearby small park in front of the church of the Notre Dame convent, so that the [[Pomnik-Sandora-Petofiho|Petőfi monument]] could take its former place. After 1945 it was precisely in front of that church that a monument to the liberators had to be put up — St Joseph and Hummel had to disappear from there. The monument was moved to nearby Rybné námestie, to a thoroughly unsuitable spot in front of the Nesztor Palace (today the Embassy of the Federal Republic of Germany); the place was not as busy then as it is now, but unsuitable it remained, and the monument was later carried off to the garden behind [[Grasalkovicov-palac|Grassalkovich Palace]].

There the precious monument gradually turned into a ruin playing in the rainbow colours of spray cans: the marble putti lost not only their fingers but their wings, the monument was disfigured with inscriptions, partly doused in red oil paint, the bronze oxidised and the column cracked. After a thorough reconstruction it was placed once again in front of the German embassy and ceremonially unveiled on 9 May 2002 — the German ambassador Frank Lambach, the mayor of Staré Mesto Andrej Ďurkovský and the heads of diplomatic missions in Slovakia attended. The restoration was carried out by the ROPO Rybárik agency, at a total cost of €46,140.
