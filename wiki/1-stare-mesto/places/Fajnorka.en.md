# Fajnorka — the Mechanical Engineering School

Fajnorka — the Secondary School of Mechanical Engineering (Stredná priemyselná škola strojnícka) at Fajnorovo nábrežie 5 — is the city's oldest technical school and its most recognisable school building: a brick mass with romantic turrets that reads from the [[Stary-most|Old Bridge]] and the Petržalka bank, and looks from the water more like a town hall than a school. Its first twenty pupils arrived in autumn 1903, but the school had no walls of its own yet — the first academic year was spent in the [[Primacialny-palac|Primate's Palace]].

![Fajnorka — the Mechanical Engineering School](/assets/skola-fajnorka.jpg)

The school was founded at the initiative of the Hungarian minister of trade, to keep in Prešporok the technical talent that was draining to Budapest and Vienna. A site was found on a filled-in Danube branch; the foundations were laid on 17 September 1903, the building designed by the Budapest architect Július Kolbenheyer was finished in 1904, and the well-known firm Hugó és Kurz built it. The result is a symmetrical block around an inner courtyard, with a facade of bare brick set off by pronounced ceramic details and folk motifs — the same language of late historicism with an Art Nouveau graft as Budapest's own industrial schools. Inside: high ceilings, wide vaulted corridors, mosaic tile floors and wooden windows. At first the pupils used only the ground and first floors: the second was taken by the Bratislava Fine Arts Society (Bratislavský výtvarný spolok) for its exhibitions. The eastern and western wings were added in 1926–1928.

During the First World War the school's rooms housed workshops for disabled soldiers; in July 1918 the Austro-Hungarian emperor Charles I inspected them in person, and a year later the last invalids left and the building returned to its pupils. The best known of them is Ján Golian, later a general and one of the leaders of the Slovak National Uprising, who studied here in 1921–1925; a memorial plaque on the facade recalls it. The entrance hall also carries a plaque to József Kiss, the most famous Hungarian flying ace of the First World War. Fajnorka also produced the ice-hockey players Vladimír Dzurilla, Jozef Golonka and Marián Šťastný and the writer Dobroslav Chrobák; in a hundred and twenty years more than twelve thousand people have graduated from it. The building still serves its purpose — mechatronics, mechanical engineering and a technical lyceum are taught here.

## Practical Information

- Address: Fajnorovo nábrežie 5
- Access: a working school, no public entry; the facade is best seen from the embankment, the Old Bridge and the Petržalka bank
