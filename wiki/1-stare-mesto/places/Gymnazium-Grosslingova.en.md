# Grösslingová Gymnasium — "Gamča"

The gymnasium on Grösslingová (Gymnázium Grösslingová), known across the city as Gamča, is a rare case of Art Nouveau — the style of grand palaces and villas — being spent on a school. The nickname abbreviates the former name: Gymnázium Alexandra Markuša na ulici Červenej armády, the Alexander Markuš Gymnasium on Red Army Street. The school traces its line to a Jesuit college founded in 1626, which changed both names and addresses more than once.

![Grösslingová Gymnasium — "Gamča"](/assets/gymnazium-grosslingova.jpg)

Since autumn 1908 the gymnasium has occupied a building designed by [[Lechner-Odon|Ödön Lechner]]. Three storeys above ground, an L-shaped plan and a generous scatter of ornament: turrets, plant motifs, glazed tiles, mosaics; the richly decorated brick fence with its wrought-iron infill deserves a look of its own, the metal repeating the same stems and leaves. On the adjoining plot on Bezručova street Lechner built the school chapel of St Elizabeth — today known as the [[Modry-kostolik|Blue Church]]; together the gymnasium and the church form one ensemble and the summit of Art Nouveau in Slovakia, as the palette alone shows — the same soft pastels on both buildings.

The building has never changed its purpose: the only interruption came with the two world wars, when a military hospital was installed in it — the rest of the time pupils have been taught here without a break. Among them the composer Gejza Dusík, [[Lasica-Milan|Milan Lasica]] and the musician Vašo Patejdl.
