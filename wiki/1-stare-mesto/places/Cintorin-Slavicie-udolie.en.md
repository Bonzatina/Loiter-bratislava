# Slávičie údolie Cemetery (Cintorín Slávičie údolie)

Slávičie údolie Cemetery (Cintorín Slávičie údolie) is Bratislava's largest cemetery: 18.5 hectares and more than 16,000 graves in the "Nightingale Valley" on the city's western edge, not far from the university dormitories. A good half of the Slovak pantheon of the 20th century lies here — from Alexander Dubček to [[Lasica-Milan|Milan Lasica]].

![Slávičie údolie Cemetery (Cintorín Slávičie údolie)](/assets/cintorin-slavicie-udolie.jpg)

The cemetery was founded in 1912 on the site of the old "cemetery of the poor", where until then the destitute, suicides and soldiers had been buried. At first the graves clustered around the gravedigger's house — the new cemetery had no ceremonial hall yet; in the First World War a common soldiers' grave appeared here. Slávičie údolie became the city's central cemetery in the 1950s; the grounds were expanded several times — first in 1969–1970, most recently in 1978. The cemetery has its material history too: old tombstones were made mostly of artificial stone with cut and applied lettering, while today's are ordered only in granite.

The cemetery has dedicated sections for notable figures and for children, and an urn wall stands by the entrance. The most visited grave is the monument to Alexander Dubček (†1992), who by tradition chose the place of his last rest himself. Nearby lie the writers Margita Figuli, Emil Boleslav Lukáč, Janko Alexy, Ján Kostra, Fraňo Kráľ, Ján Ondruš and Milo Urban, the hockey goaltender Vladimír Dzurilla, the actors [[Lasica-Milan|Milan Lasica]] and Jozef Kroner, the director Janko Borodáč, the opera star Lucia Popp and the composer Marek Brezovský.
