# The Old Bridge (Starý most)

The Old Bridge (Starý most) is Bratislava's first permanent bridge across the Danube and the city's record-holder for the number of names. For a thousand years before it, the city made do with fords and ferries: a ford on the site of today's Water Tower is documented in writing in 1252; wooden bridges were built under Sigismund of Luxembourg (1407 and 1430) and under Matthias Corvinus — a pile bridge with a lifting middle section (1468–1473, swept away by the flood of 1486). In 1676–1825 a "pendulum" bridge operated — an anchored chain of boats with a ferry swinging between the banks — and for the coronations of Hungarian kings (1563–1830) pontoon bridges of tied-up boats were laid across the Danube. The last predecessor was the pontoon Caroline Bridge, laid in 1825 for the coronation of Caroline Augusta of Bavaria and serving — dismantled each winter — until 1889.

Construction of the steel bridge began on 12 August 1889, and already on 30 December 1890 the finished bridge was ceremonially opened by Emperor Franz Joseph I himself, whose name it received (as bronze plaques at the approaches announced). The 460-metre structure on three stone piers cost almost two million gold coins; in 1891 the railway section was completed alongside, and from 1914 the famous "Vienna tram" ran across it — its [[Zastavka-Viedenskej-elektricky|surviving stop]] with the original paving can be seen at the edge of [[Sad-Janka-Krala|Janko Kráľ Park]].

![The Old Bridge before reconstruction](/assets/stary-most.jpg)

After 1918 the bridge was called the Danube Bridge, and from 28 October 1934 the Štefánik Bridge. In April 1945 the retreating German troops blew it up, cutting Petržalka off from the city; the Red Army first laid two temporary crossings (a pontoon one in April and a wooden pile bridge by the SNM building in August 1945), and then, together with German prisoners of war, assembled on the surviving piers a new bridge from the standardized demountable Roth-Waagner truss system in 5 months and 17 days — the Red Army Bridge, opened on 3 February 1946 as a "provisional solution". In 1950 the railway section was restored on the shared piers; until the opening of the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]] in 1972 this pair remained the only permanent Danube crossing, and trains ran across it until the launch of [[Pristavny-most|Prístavný Bridge]] in 1985. The "provisional solution" served until 2 December 2013 (from 1990 already under the name Old Bridge). In the complete reconstruction, designed by Miroslav Maťaščík, the old load-bearing structure was replaced by a new six-span truss with a 137-metre main span, the two river piers were merged into one, and the walkways were widened with viewing terraces; the works officially ended on 15 December 2015, on 19 May 2016 the bridge opened to pedestrians and cyclists, and on 8 July 2016 the first tram crossed it — to Petržalka.
