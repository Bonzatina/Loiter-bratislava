# Grassalkovich Palace (Grasalkovičov palác)

The Grassalkovich Palace (Grasalkovičov palác), also known as the Presidential Palace, is an axially symmetric rococo building on [[Hodzovo-namestie|Hodžovo Square]], since 1996 the residence of the President of the Slovak Republic. It was built in 1760 by Count Anton Grassalkovich (Grasalkovič, Grassalkovich de Gyarak), a lawyer who rose to preside over the Hungarian Royal Chamber; the design is attributed to Andrej Mayerhofer, though some sources name F. A. Hillebrandt. At the time the building stood on open ground outside the still-fortified town. Joseph Haydn gave concerts in the palace — a fact once commemorated by Jozef Kostka's 1959 plaque on the west wall, now kept in the depository of the Bratislava City Museum. Inside survives the originally free-standing Chapel of St Barbara; since 1963 the palace with its park, fence and gates has been a national cultural monument.

![Grassalkovich Palace (Grasalkovičov palác)](/assets/grasalkovicov-palac.jpg)

The palace became a presidential residence twice. In the early 1940s [[Bellus-Emil|Emil Belluš]] adapted it as the seat of the president of the wartime Slovak state — an experience that likely led to his later rebuilding of the neighbouring [[Letny-arcibiskupsky-palac|Summer Archbishop's Palace]]. From the 1950s the building housed the Klement Gottwald Central House of Pioneers and Youth, and only after a three-year reconstruction costing nearly 280 million crowns did President Michal Kováč accept the symbolic key to the palace on 30 September 1996. Every year on 15 June, the anniversary of the inauguration, the palace opens its doors to the public.

Behind the palace stretches a French-style garden adjoining that of the [[Letny-arcibiskupsky-palac|Summer Archbishop's Palace]], open to the public as a park with an entrance from [[Namestie-slobody|Freedom Square]]. A reconstruction in the late 1990s largely restored its historic appearance. The garden holds the fountain Joy of Life (Radosť zo života, 1978) by Tibor Bártfay and co-authors — the same sculptor whose [[Fontana-Planeta-mieru|Planet of Peace]] sphere greets visitors before the main façade — and an equestrian statue of Maria Theresa, moved here from Nitra in the early 1990s. The monument to the city's native composer Johann Nepomuk Hummel, which also stood here, returned in 2002 to its historic place on [[Hviezdoslavovo-namestie|Hviezdoslav Square]].
