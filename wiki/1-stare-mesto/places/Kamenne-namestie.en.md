# Kamenné námestie (Stone Square)

Kamenné námestie is a place Bratislava has been arguing about for decades: formally the city centre, in practice a torn-up space that has earned the nickname "Bratislava's black hole". It got its name back in the 18th century, when stonemasons worked here; later it held small markets with fruit, vegetables and fresh produce, ringed by typical burgher houses with ornate architecture. Before the First World War the square was Stein Platz in German and Kő tér in Hungarian, under socialism it was Kyiv Square (Kyjevské námestie), and only afterwards did the stone name come back.

![Kamenné námestie (Stone Square)](/assets/kamenne-namestie.jpg)

The break came in the second half of the 20th century. The historic buildings were demolished and [[Matusik-Ivan|Ivan Matušík]]'s work rose in their place — the [[Prior-a-hotel-Kyjev|Prior department store]], Czechoslovakia's first full-service department store, and Hotel Kyjev. By the architectural standards of their day these were ambitious things, but the urban fabric of the old centre went down with the houses, and the square lost its shape: what was meant as a modern city node ended up a set of unrelated volumes with no legible connection between them.

Years of underfunding and careless upkeep have since been added: parking, through traffic, scattered functions with no common idea. Even the paulownia planted as a green accent withered and had to be removed. Since 2016 the city has been running the "Living Square" (Živé námestie) project — greenery, places to sit, a space for performances — but delivery is stuck on underground garages that need heavy investment and on tangled ownership; on current estimates nothing will move before the late 2030s. Specialists warn that repaving alone will not do it: the problem here is urbanistic — how the buildings are placed and how much space sits unused.

- Address: Kamenné námestie, Bratislava
