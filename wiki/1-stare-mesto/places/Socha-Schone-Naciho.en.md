# Statue of Schöne Náci (Socha Schöne Náciho)

The statue of Schöne Náci stands on Rybárska brána where it opens onto the Main Square, next to Café Mayer — and it is probably the most photographed sculpture in the city. A smiling man in a tailcoat with a fluttering scarf leans his right hand on a stone post and raises his top hat with the left, greeting passers-by: this is how [[Lamar-Ignac|Ignác Lamár]], the street legend of old Prešporok, greeted the city for decades.

![Statue of Schöne Náci (Socha Schöne Náciho)](/assets/socha-schone-naciho.jpg)

The figure, cast in an aluminium alloy and standing about 194 centimetres tall including the hat on its grey stone base, was made by the academic sculptor Juraj Meliš, author of the [[Dvetisicrocna-Bratislava|"Two-Thousand-Year-Old Bratislava"]] reliefs by the main station. It was unveiled on 26 July 1997 at the "Korzo" celebration marking the completion of the fourth stage of the old town's pedestrian-zone renewal. The sculpture is deliberately a little caricatured: not a portrait but an image — courtesy as a genre of city life.

The statue belongs to the city heritage reserve and is owned by the Staré Mesto borough. Vandals have damaged it repeatedly, so the figure has been repaired and shifted along Rybárska brána several times; the top hat is regularly plastered with stickers and marked with felt-tip. Together with the [[Dunajska-promenada|Danube promenade]] and [[Cumil|Čumil]], it defines the informal genre of Bratislava street sculpture — not heroes on pedestals but townspeople at eye level with the passer-by.
