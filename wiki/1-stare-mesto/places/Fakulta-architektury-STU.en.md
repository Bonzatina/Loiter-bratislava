# Faculty of Architecture and Design STU (Fakulta architektúry a dizajnu STU)

The house where future architects are taught was built by the country's leading architect: the **Pavilion of Theoretical Institutes** (Pavilón teoretických ústavov) of the Slovak University of Technology went up in 1950–1952 to a design by [[Bellus-Emil|Emil Belluš]], and has held the Faculty of Architecture and Design ever since. The inscription "PAVILÓN TEORETICKÝCH ÚSTAVOV" above the street entrance is still there.

![Faculty of Architecture and Design STU (Fakulta architektúry a dizajnu STU)](/assets/fakulta-architektury-stu.jpg)

The building is composed symmetrically across the full width of [[Namestie-slobody|Námestie slobody]]: the middle section is set back, a wide flight of steps opens in front of it, and wings with deep loggias frame it on either side. The monumentality nods to the palaces nearby, but the facades were denied historical finery — Belluš worked with clean planes, large glazing and simple geometric elements. The one concession to the era is twenty reliefs in the socialist realist manner showing various trades: a teacher with a sapling and a schoolboy, a vet at a cow, a peasant woman with a sheaf. The interior turns on a large entrance hall along the central axis, joined by an auditorium running the whole width of the building.

The school's graduates include the architects who shaped Bratislava itself: Dušan Kuzma (the Academy of Fine Arts buildings on Drotárska), [[Milucky-Ferdinand|Ferdinand Milučký]] (the [[Krematorium|crematorium]]), [[Matusik-Ivan|Ivan Matušík]] (the [[Prior-a-hotel-Kyjev|Prior store and Hotel Kyjev]]), and Martin Kusý, Pavol Paňák and Peter Bauer, authors of the [[Nova-budova-SND|new Slovak National Theatre building]].
