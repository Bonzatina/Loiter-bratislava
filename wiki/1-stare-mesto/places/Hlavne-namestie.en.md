# Main Square (Hlavné námestie)

Hlavné námestie is the central square of Staré Mesto and the point the whole historic centre converges on: an almost square plan, the [[Stara-radnica|Old Town Hall]] to the north-east, palaces on the remaining sides. It borders Františkánske námestie to the north, and Kostolná, Radničná, Rybárska brána, Zelená and Sedlárska streets run into it.

![Main Square (Hlavné námestie)](/assets/hlavne-namestie.jpg)

One theory holds that in the 12th century Bratislava had a single large central square which later building split in two — into today's Františkánske and Hlavné. The first record of a name is from 1373: Forum, the Market; in various forms (Marcht, Ring) the name held until the 18th century, and the square really was the place for markets, entertainments, public executions and coronation processions. In 1572 the Maximilian Fountain was built here, solving the shortage of drinking water. The present name first appears in a charter of 1783, in the German form Hauptplatz.

After that the name changed with every regime, and the list is a short history of the 20th century in itself: in 1850 the square was named for Emperor Franz Joseph I, after the birth of the First Czechoslovak Republic it became Masaryk Square, and under the Slovak State, Hitler Square. The pre-war name returned briefly after the war, until in 1953 it was renamed 4 April Square, after the date of the city's liberation by the Red Army. The historic name came back after the Velvet Revolution.

There have been two reconstructions since. In the nineties the greenery was removed and the square paved in stone, in an attempt to give it back a medieval character — two figures of soldiers appeared then too, and tourists like to be photographed with them. The paving was replaced in 2005–2006, and the square has stood almost unchanged since. It is unusually rich in monuments and palaces: besides the town hall there is the Discount Bank building, the [[Palugyayov-palac|Palugyay Palace]], the Apponyi Palace (part of the town hall complex), the Governor's Palace and the Kutscherfeld Palace; the embassies of Japan, Greece and France occupy individual houses. Since the end of the 20th century the square has hosted New Year concerts, Christmas and Easter markets every year.
