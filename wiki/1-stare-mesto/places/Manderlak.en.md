# Manderlák

The Manderla apartment and commercial block (Obchodný a obytný dom Manderla), known around town simply as "Manderlák", is **Slovakia's first true high-rise**: an eleven-storey tower on the south-eastern edge of SNP Square (Námestie SNP 23). It was commissioned by the wholesale meat dealer Rudolf Manderla, who was inspired by American skyscrapers; the design is by architects [[Ludwig-Christian|Christian Ludwig]], Augustín Danielis and Emerich Spitzer, and it was built by the Viennese firm Pittel & Brausewetter. The building was completed in 1935 (1937 by other accounts) and was considered the tallest residential building in the country at the time.

![Manderlák](/assets/manderlak.jpg)

Structurally it is a reinforced-concrete skeleton: up to the fifth floor it runs on eight axes, narrowing to four above — hence the characteristic stepped silhouette of a tower growing out of a lower block. The austere [[functionalism|functionalist]] facade is animated by vertical strips that grow into the rooftop structure. The house pointedly stepped out of the street line of the freshly modernised square, and its height was a deliberate provocation to contemporaries.

The ground floor held a vaulted shopping passage with a curved arcade; the second floor housed offices and the Grand Café with a view of the city — a favoured meeting place of Bratislava's artistic community. The building still stands, used as housing with retail below.
