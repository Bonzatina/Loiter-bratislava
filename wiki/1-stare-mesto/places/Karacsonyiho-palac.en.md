# Karáčoni Palace (Karáčoniho palác)

The Karáčoni Palace (Karáčoniho palác, older spelling Karácsonyiho palác) at Štefánikova 2 is a historicist palace of 1883–1884 and a national cultural monument, number 101-596/0. In the summer of 2026 it came out of a major reconstruction paid for largely from the European Recovery and Resilience Plan; the building houses the Office of the President of the Slovak Republic and the administration of the Government Office.

![Karáčoni Palace (Karáčoniho palác)](/assets/karacsonyiho-palac.jpg)

Among Bratislava's palaces this one is young: not eighteenth-century baroque but the late nineteenth century. It was built on the site of a utility building belonging to the [[Grasalkovicov-palac|Grassalkovich Palace]] complex, and its appearance is directly tied to the transformation of Štefánikova into a grand urban artery running from the centre to the newly opened [[Hlavna-stanica|main railway station]] — the same wave that gave the street the [[Kaviaren-Stefanka|Štefanka café]] and the whole quarter its present character. The client was Count Quido Karáčoni (1817–1885), a deputy, landowner, notable figure at court and founder of charitable institutions. The massive, plastically articulated façade facing the street survives in its original state and counted among the principal objects of the new street when it was built; what changed over the decades were mainly the interiors and the garden wing.

The building lost its lustre gradually and bureaucratically. In 1957 administrative offices were installed and the former courtyard was roofed over with sheet metal; a further remodelling in 1998 failed to improve the overall condition. By the 2020s the main problems were damp, damaged structures and heavy heat loss — which is to say the question was no longer about appearances.

Work began in spring 2025: first demolition inside and the removal of an unsuitable extension at the rear, then structural securing of the retaining wall and treatment of the damp. The building then received new heating and electrical distribution, modern data networks, efficient lighting and new lifts; the roof and its timber frame were repaired, and the façade insulated and put in order. The changes were most visible from the rear, garden side. The tender was announced in November 2024; of four bidders Metrostav Slovakia won, signing the contract with the Government Office and the Office of the President. The contract came to more than €8.6 million with VAT, of which €5.7 million was the European Union's contribution through the Recovery and Resilience Plan. One idea dropped out of the original plans: the nursery for the children of staff at the quarter's state institutions never materialised, and the spaces stayed administrative.

Visitors are not admitted — this is a working building, and what there is to see is the façade from the pavement of Štefánikova.

## Practical Information

- Address: Štefánikova 2, Bratislava
- Access: interiors closed; the building is occupied by state institutions
