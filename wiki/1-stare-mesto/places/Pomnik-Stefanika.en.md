# Štefánik Monument (pomník M. R. Štefánika)

The monument to Milan Rastislav Štefánik (pomník M. R. Štefánika) stands on the square bearing his name (Námestie M. R. Štefánika) by the Danube front of the Eurovea complex: a bronze general in a flying suit, 7.4 metres tall and six tons in weight, with a tall pylon behind him carrying a single-tailed lion — the symbol of the Czechoslovak legions (not to be confused with the heraldic Czech lion). On the pylon below the lion is a plaque commemorating the Pittsburgh Agreement: on 30 May 1918 representatives of Czech and Slovak organizations in the USA signed the accord on a common state of Czechs and Slovaks, and Štefánik himself — one of the founders of Czechoslovakia — became its first minister of war.

![Štefánik Monument by Eurovea (pomník M. R. Štefánika)](/assets/pomnik-stefanika-eurovea.jpg)

The monument has a long and dramatic backstory. The first statue of Štefánik in a flying suit, by sculptor Bohumil Kafka, was erected in 1938 on the site of the dismantled equestrian monument of Maria Theresa — today's Štúr Square (a reduced copy of Fadrusz's statue on the [[Dunajska-promenada|Danube Promenade]] recalls that story). Behind the general rose a pylon of about 27 metres with a lion; since Hitler was already master of Austria, the lion was turned to face east. After Germany occupied Petržalka, the Führer — so the story goes — pronounced his famous line by the Danube: "That cat must go" ("Tá mačka musí preč"). In 1939 the lion was taken off the pylon, in 1944 the pylon itself was demolished, and in the 1950s the new regime removed and probably melted down the statue of Štefánik — only in 2012 was the face section of the sculpture discovered in the Slovak National Gallery depository in Zvolen.

The lion survived it all: on 28 October 1988, for the 70th anniversary of Czechoslovakia, it was unveiled anew on a shortened pylon in front of the [[Slovenske-narodne-muzeum|Slovak National Museum]] on Vajanského nábrežie as a symbol of Czechoslovak statehood — the missing parts of Kafka's lion were recreated by restorer Jozef Porubovič. On 23 February 2009 the lion was taken down again — this time for the new Štefánik monument by the rising Eurovea. The new bronze statue was cast in Moravian Blansko after Kafka's smaller sculpture erected in 1994 in front of the Petřín observatory in Prague; the pylon with the lion moved here from Vajanského nábrežie, and the pedestal gained a plaque with the donors' names.
