# Hviezdoslav Square (Hviezdoslavovo námestie)

Hviezdoslav Square (Hviezdoslavovo námestie) is Bratislava's main promenade: a long pedestrian square along the southern edge of the old town, stretching between the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]] and the [[Historicka-budova-SND|historic building of the Slovak National Theatre]]. It has borne the name of [[Hviezdoslav-Pavol-Orszagh|Pavol Országh Hviezdoslav]] since the early 1930s — and since 1937 the bronze poet himself has been sitting at the theatre end of the square, in the form of [[Pomnik-Hviezdoslava|his monument]].

![Hviezdoslav Square (Hviezdoslavovo námestie)](/assets/hviezdoslavovo-namestie.jpg)

The history of the square's names is a short course in the city's history: from the late 18th century it was called Promenade, in 1840–1852 Theaterplatz, then for a decade it bore the name of Field Marshal Radetzky, from 1899 to 1920 that of Lajos Kossuth, and through the first Czechoslovak decade it was Palackého sady. Its appearance changed too: until recently the square resembled a small city park, but a reconstruction at the end of the 20th century turned it into an urban corso — with a longitudinal fountain along its axis, a stage for concerts and dense rows of restaurant terraces along the northern side.

The eastern end of the square is anchored by the historic building of the National Theatre, next to which stands the revived historic [[Hotel-Carlton|Carlton hotel]], inseparable from the image of the old town. The southern side is occupied by the embassies of the United States and Germany; the security barriers of the American mission are a constant subject of civic dispute, marring the promenade. The square also holds the fountain "Girl with a Deer" by [[Rigele-Alojz|Alojz Rigele]].
