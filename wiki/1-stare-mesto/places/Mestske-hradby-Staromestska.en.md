# City Walls on Staromestská (Mestské hradby na Staromestskej ulici)

The longest surviving stretch of Bratislava's medieval fortifications runs along Staromestská street — north from [[Dom-svateho-Martina|St Martin's Cathedral]], with the Bird Tower, the Bax Tower and the Little Bastion. The noisy arterial road for which [[Vydrica|Podhradie]] was demolished passes directly beneath the wall; above it, between the wall and the parkan wall, quite a different city opens up — a quiet narrow lane from which you can see what is invisible from below.

![City Walls on Staromestská (Mestské hradby na Staromestskej ulici)](/assets/mestske-hradby-staromestska.jpg)

The General Investor of Bratislava opened this stretch to the public on 15 April 2016, daily from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m.; a year later, from 1 April 2017, access was repeated on the same terms. The route leads from the cathedral up the western flight of steps through the so-called Narrow Lane behind the walls to the Bird Tower — through it, without entering the interior, visitors reach the space between the wall and the parkan wall and go on to the bridge over Staromestská. The lane between the two walls is open too, with a self-service library in the Little Bastion, and an exhibition of period weapons is set up in the lower part of the Bird Tower; panels along the way tell the history of the fortifications. The whole area of the Bax Tower, with its viewing platform over Staromestská, was opened on 6 June 2016 — at the same time the brickwork of the Bird Tower was cleaned, the paving and steps by the Bax Tower repaired and litter bins installed.

Walking along the walls all the way to Kapitulská street, as intended, did not work out, and the reason stands right beside it. A structural survey by the engineer Holub, made in 2015 for the Esterházy Palace at Kapitulská 6–10 — a building directly adjoining the parkan wall — showed an extremely high risk that its outer wall would collapse onto the wall and parkan of Staromestská: rot is destroying the tie beam, which will fall and pull the roof structure down with it, after which the gable masonry will collapse partly into the building and partly onto the neighbouring plot. On the GIB's request the state building supervision authority inspected the site, confirmed that this section of pavement must stay closed and again ordered the owner to carry out structural securing without delay. So the open part ends where somebody else's dangerous roof begins.
