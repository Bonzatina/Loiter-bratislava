# Umelka Gallery (Galéria Umelka)

Umelka (Galéria Umelka) at Dostojevského rad 2 is the oldest functionalist building in Slovakia and one of the country's oldest cultural institutions: an exhibition pavilion built in 1925–1926 by the Umelecká beseda slovenská artists' society to a design by Alois Balán and Jiří Grossmann, it has served artists without interruption for a century. The national cultural monument is considered the symbolic building of modern architecture's arrival in Slovakia: traces of arched decoration are still visible at the entrance along with the heavy coffered ceiling of the vestibule, yet the large exhibition hall is already assembled from simple reinforced-concrete frames under a factory-style glazed roof, and the unplastered brick facades recall early Dutch modernism.

![Umelka Gallery (Galéria Umelka)](/assets/galeria-umelka.jpg)

The house is written into political history too: in front of it, in August 1968, Ladislav Bielik took his famous photograph of a man baring his chest before a Soviet tank — the world's best photo of that year — and in November 1989 it was here that the first protest assemblies took place from which the Public Against Violence movement (Verejnosť proti násiliu) was born. Since 1990 the building has belonged to the Slovak Union of Visual Arts (Slovenská výtvarná únia), which carried out the first reconstructions since 1927 (2004–2006, 2010, 2013–2014) and holds dozens of exhibitions a year here — without public funding.

## Practical Information

Dostojevského rad 2 — opposite Šafárikovo námestie, between the historic centre and Eurovea. The gallery is open Tuesday to Sunday from 12:00 to 18:00, admission free.
