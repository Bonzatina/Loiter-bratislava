# Stein Brewery (Pivovar Stein)

The Stein Brewery (Pivovar Stein) was Bratislava's principal brewery for 135 years, occupying an entire block of Blumentál between Blumentálska, Krížna, Legionárska and Bernolákova streets. The Stein brothers brewed their first beer a few days before Christmas 1872 and opened the gates in early 1873; the enterprise immediately ranked among the largest in Upper Hungary, turning out 60–80 thousand hectolitres a year and supplying not only Bratislava but — according to surviving documents — even Germany and Switzerland. The site was chosen deliberately: next door stood the [[Stanica-konskej-zeleznice|horse-drawn railway station]], converted to steam traction in the very year the brewery was founded, and the rails carried the beer to the Slovak regions, to Moravia and Bohemia, and to the Danube port for shipment further into Europe.

![Stein Brewery on Legionárska street, 2009 (Pivovar Stein)](/assets/pivovar-stein.jpg)

The complex grew in stages. The first phase (1876–1896) produced the brewhouse and fermentation house along Legionárska; the oldest buildings were the bottling plant and the spent-grain drying house, with the boiler house, barrel-washing shop and a bottling hall over extensive lagering cellars facing the courtyard, and an administration building with staff apartments on Blumentálska — traditional structures by the Feigler family, Bratislava's dynasty of builders. The interwar cold store with its water tower was already a modern reinforced-concrete work by the Pittel & Brausewetter firm, which also built the wartime silo and a new brewhouse. In 1944 permission was granted for a new fermentation hall — the spilka, with a monumental shell-concrete dome designed by architect Herbert Zrnovský; construction dragged into the mid-1950s and was finished only after nationalization. The final rebuilding of 1967–1973 arrived just in time for the company's centenary. Together with the Stollwerck chocolate factory (later Figaro), Stein was the city's largest food-industry enterprise of the 20th century.

The brand's history broke twice. The world crisis of the late 1920s and early 1930s halved production, but the brewery recovered; under the wartime Slovak state the Steins sold their majority stake to Slovenská banka, the war left the plant badly damaged — and yet output resumed as early as September 1945, which contemporaries likened to a small miracle. Nationalization in 1948 proved the real catastrophe: the beer was deliberately turned into a cheap "workers'" drink, the enterprise kept changing names (West Slovak Breweries, then Bratislavské pivovary a sódovkárne), and the beer sold as Bratislavský ležiak, Jubilejné, Starosladovnícke or Danubius Stein. The production record came in 1982 — 733 thousand hectolitres. After 1989 the Stein name returned; the brewery even tried to teach Slovaks to drink fruit beers with lemon and peach flavours — radlers a decade before their fashion — but never came close to the numbers of its best years. As late as the 1990s the characteristic yeasty smell of brewing still hung around Krížna street and Račianske mýto.

The end came quickly. In 2007 the brewery was bought by the Luxembourg development group Orco, which cared nothing for beer — the prize was land in the city centre; production stopped in 2008, and the Stein brand has since been brewed by the Steiger brewery in Vyhne. The financial crisis forced Orco to sell the site at auction, while scrap collectors stripped the empty buildings of everything of value, down to the copper roofing. In September 2014 the brewery was demolished within a few weeks. The Monuments Board had proposed protected status for the whole ensemble back in 2013, but the Ministry of Culture listed only the spilka: the domed hall has been converted into offices, with the Spilka beer restaurant open since late 2020 on its lower level (Legionárska 8) — a dining hall set among the columns of the former fermentation house — and the Stein residential complex has risen around it. Beer bearing the name is still loyally poured in the city's old pubs — for instance at U poštárov on Šancová street.
