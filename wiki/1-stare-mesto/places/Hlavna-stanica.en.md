# Main Railway Station (Bratislava hlavná stanica)

The main railway station (Bratislava hlavná stanica) on Franz Liszt Square is the city's front door: some two hundred trains and up to sixty thousand passengers a day, six platforms and ten platform edges, connections to Vienna, Prague and Budapest. Few people call it beautiful today — yet beauty is exactly what it was once known for.

![Main Railway Station (Bratislava hlavná stanica)](/assets/hlavna-stanica.jpg)

The city got its first station building in 1848, for the lines to Vienna via Gänserndorf and to Břeclav; it still stands on Šancová street and now houses the Ministry of the Interior. The present building went up in 1871, a few hundred metres further north, after the line through Štúrovo linked Bratislava to Budapest. Its design is credited to Ignác Feigler the Younger, the same notable Pressburg architect who built the [[Palugyayov-palac|Palugyay Palace]]. The station of the early twentieth century looked nothing like today's: a richly worked façade, stucco, decorative gables and small roof turrets made it one of the most striking buildings in town. The 1904 rebuild added space and heightened that representative character.

The looks were lost in 1939–1941, when a modernisation in the spirit of the times stripped the historic ornament and left the façade plain and austere — roughly as it is known today. In 1883 a single-track link to [[3-raca|Rača]] was added, later feeding the Žilina direction, and in 1962 another to Nové Mesto. The last major addition was the glass extension of 1989, the "skleník": built as a temporary fix pending a new station complex in the east of the city, the temporary outlived the plan and is still in place. The long-promised full reconstruction has been deferred for decades; only parts have been renewed — the traction system in 2012, some interiors. On the forecourt stand the five concrete reliefs of [[Dvetisicrocna-Bratislava|"Two-Thousand-Year-Old Bratislava"]], and the old goods yard nearby houses the [[Muzeum-dopravy|Transport Museum]].

## Practical Information

- Address: Námestie Franza Liszta 1, Bratislava I
- Hours: open around the clock; ticket offices follow the operators' schedules
- Website: [zssk.sk](https://www.zssk.sk)
- Access: trams and buses to the centre from the station; about 15 minutes on foot to Staré Mesto
