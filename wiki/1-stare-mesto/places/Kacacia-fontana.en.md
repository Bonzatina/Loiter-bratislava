# Duck Fountain (Kačacia fontána)

The Duck Fountain (Kačacia fontána) hides in the dense greenery of a small park at the edge of Šafárikovo námestie by the tram stop — one of the city's most curious and most easily missed fountains, a national cultural monument since 1963. It was created in 1914 by Bratislava sculptor Robert Kühmayer in neo-baroque spirit: three bronze boys in a dynamic scramble are catching four ducks, water spouting from the birds' beaks. The pedestal is piled from granite and sandstone blocks depicting the Danube bank; an arc-shaped basin and a handsome decorative fence frame the composition, and the author's name and the year can be found on the stone.

![Duck Fountain (Kačacia fontána)](/assets/kacacia-fontana.jpg)

The square itself was born together with the bridge: after the permanent Danube crossing opened, a new quarter with a rectangular street grid grew on its northern bridgehead, and a square formed before the approach to the [[Stary-most|bridge]]; in 1914 it was adorned with the fountain. Back then it was a quiet, almost romantic corner by the river — today the little park lives to the rhythm of waiting for the tram.

Kühmayer is believed to have been inspired by an old Danube legend about a water sprite and petrified boys — there is no documentary proof, but the story has grown onto the fountain for good. As the tale goes, the water sprite showed young duck-herders the way to the Danube golden treasure, unlocked by the magic words "Seksament, open up!", for which he was punished by the treasure's guardian — an old catfish. The black toad, keeper of the Danube banks, laid a curse: anyone who speaks and hears the secret words will turn to stone. A boy shouted them out while the friends were herding their ducks — and all three petrified together with the birds. The legend was written down by Mária Ďuríčková in the famous collection Bratislavské povesti (Bratislava Legends).
