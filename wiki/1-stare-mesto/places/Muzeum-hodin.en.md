# The Museum of Clocks in the House of the Good Shepherd (Múzeum hodín)

The Museum of Clocks (Múzeum hodín) of the [[Muzeum-mesta-Bratislavy|Bratislava City Museum]] occupies the House of the Good Shepherd (Dom U dobrého pastiera) — a narrow rococo house on Židovská street, one of the few surviving fragments of the Podhradie, miraculously spared both by the great fire of 1913 and by the demolition for the [[Most-SNP|SNP Bridge]]. The exhibition tells of a craft in which Pressburg was the first city of the Hungarian kingdom until the mid-19th century: nearly half of all clockmaking masters in Slovakia worked here, the oldest record names the locksmith Hans in 1440 (clocks were then made by smiths and locksmiths), and in 1776 Pressburg's clockmakers founded their own guild — the only one in Hungary.

![The House of the Good Shepherd on Židovská street](/assets/muzeum-hodin.jpg)

The stars of the collection are the works of Jacob Guldan (1726–1790), a native of the Domažlice region in Bohemia who began in the Podhradie: the museum holds seven of his pieces, including figural clocks with carved wooden decoration on the transience of life (a paradox: the Guldan brand sells pocket watches to this day, though the master himself never made any). Unique are the "Ages of Man" clocks — their case was carved from a single piece of wood by the sculptor Ján Fadrusz, author of the Maria Theresa coronation monument, on commission from the [[pressburg-families|Palugyay]] wine merchants; the rarest exhibit is the double-sided tin shop sign of the 18th-century clockmaker Benjamin Schmidt, a "catalogue" of his wares. There is also a tower-clock mechanism from the Devín church — all gears and transmissions of the locksmith's school. Open Thursday to Sunday, 11:00–18:00.
