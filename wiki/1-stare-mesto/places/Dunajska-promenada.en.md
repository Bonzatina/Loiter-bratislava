# Danube Promenade (Dunajská promenáda)

The Danube Promenade (Dunajská promenáda) is the old strolling embankment of the left bank, from the passenger port to River Park: on Sundays all of Pressburg once flowed here — to watch the river, sit on benches by the flowerbeds and make merry in the now-vanished [[Park-kultury-a-oddychu|PKO]]. Today the public more often picks the promenade by Eurovea with its cafés, but the old embankment keeps its particular atmosphere — and a whole open-air gallery of statues and memorials.

![Danube Promenade (Dunajská promenáda)](/assets/dunajska-promenada.jpg)

The most unusual exhibit is the **Pressburg meridian** (Meridianus Posoniensis): in 1733 the cartographer Samuel Mikovíni, father of Slovak cartography, set the zero meridian of his maps along a line oriented on the north-east tower of the [[Bratislavsky-hrad|castle]] — common practice among cartographers until the world agreed on Greenwich in 1854. The meridian is traced in the promenade's paving, beside it a plaque of 1997 for the 250th anniversary of the scholar's death, and by the new [[Zuckermandel]] stands a statue of Mikovíni himself by František Gibala (1970). Further along the embankment: the Pressburg-born physicist Ján Andrej Segner, "forefather of the water turbine", whose Segner wheel inspired his townsman Wolfgang Kempelen to history's first steam-turbine patent; a pensive bronze [[Jurkovic-Dusan|Dušan Jurkovič]] by River Park; the monument to composer Mikuláš Moyzes by Karol Lacko — a wanderer that moved over from the [[Sad-Janka-Krala|right bank]] after vandalism and twice lost its stolen bust; and the faceted pillar of the poet and politician Martin Rázus (2013, Dzurek and Bartoš) of thirteen book-shaped slabs by the Devín hotel.

Among the newest stars is the "Mythical Boat" by the Czech sculptor Jaroslav Róna (2010) at River Park: a man steering the boat through dangerous currents, a queen-mother carrying the germ of new life, and a guardian dog — "a tribute to the great river and the city on its bank where cultures succeeded one another". Nearby stands a reduced bronze copy of Ján Fadrusz's legendary equestrian monument to Maria Theresa, which stood on today's Štúrovo námestie in 1897–1921: the Bratislava Beautification Society first presented it before the [[Hotel-Carlton|Carlton]], but the city council declined the siting, and the copy moved to River Park. There too, in the River Gallery, are a lion of old tyres by the Korean artist Ji Yong Mo and a metal bird by Ján Kondáč.
