# Michael's Tower (Michalská veža)

Michael's Tower (Michalská veža) above the gate of the same name is the only survivor of the city's four medieval gates (alongside the Laurinc, Fishermen's and Vydrica gates) and one of the Old Town's great landmarks. Construction began in the second half of the 13th century; after 1531 a fourth storey was added, the octagonal superstructure rose at the end of the 16th century, and the tower received its present silhouette with the onion helmet in the mid-18th century. Then too came the statue of Archangel Michael (installed 20 June 1758) and the clock face (1757), visible "from every corner of the town"; the tower also served as a fire lookout. Since 1962 it has been managed by the [[Muzeum-mesta-Bratislavy|Bratislava City Museum]].

![Michael's Tower from Michalská street](/assets/michalska-veza.jpg)

In 2021–2023 the tower underwent a general restoration costing €2.55 million: the facades, stone and relief elements, clock faces and bells were restored (one bell, shot through in the Second World War, was recast anew), the windows were replaced with replicas of the baroque ones, and in the head of the archangel's statue a time capsule of 1758 was found. The renewed tower opened with a new exhibition whose main exhibit is the tower itself: the climb through the floors is arranged as a journey through the centuries, from medieval embrasures to the museum era. Tickets and the information centre are at the Red Crayfish pharmacy (Lekáreň u červeného raka, Michalská 28), preserved in a house built on the foundations of the medieval barbican.

Before the gate, Michael's Bridge crosses the former moat, guarded by two pendant stone saints of the 1720s (the literature gives 1720, 1725 and 1727) — John of Nepomuk and the Archangel Michael. Century after century their story was kept by the Bratislava Beautification Society: restorations of 1877 and 1897, new sandstone pedestals of 1898 with the society's Hungarian dedication (covered since the 1980s by a Slovak plaque). Since the restoration of 1984 the bridge has carried artificial-stone casts by Ladislav Chamuti Sr.; in 2006 the copies were renewed by Chamuti Jr. and Andrej Baník, and when in June 2008 a night storm dropped a broken branch on the archangel and shattered it to pieces, the same pair of restorers cast the saint anew (installed in March 2009). The pharmacy itself dates from the first half of the 14th century and is among the oldest in Slovakia: from 1953 it housed the country's only Pharmaceutical Museum, closed in 2001 when the house was handed to a leaseholder, revived first as the reduced exhibition "Vessels for Storing Medicines" (2006) and then, in the summer of 2023, together with the renewed tower.
