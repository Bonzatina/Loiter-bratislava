# Čumil

Čumil is a bronze man peering out of a half-open manhole at the crossing of Rybárska brána, Panská and Laurinská — probably the most photographed sculpture in Bratislava and one of the chief attractions of the historic centre. The figure is small: 82 centimetres wide and 36 high — and nobody walks past it.

![Čumil](/assets/cumil.jpg)

The author is the sculptor, academic painter and graphic artist Viktor Hulík. The sculpture was unveiled on 26 July 1997 at the Korzo Party celebrating the completion of the pedestrian zone's reconstruction; thirty thousand people came, along with the then president of Slovakia Michal Kováč, and two works by Juraj Meliš — [[Socha-Schone-Naciho|Schöne Náci]] and the Napoleonic soldier — were installed the same day. Hulík explained the idea without any symbolism: "Čumil is an absolutely anonymous figure I invented, because as a teenager I often walked the corso and wanted this figure to express the good mood, the smile and the cheerfulness that once prevailed here." In Slovak čumil means a gawker, a curious onlooker; the author called him "the Bratislava rubbernecker".

Foreign media regularly take him for a soldier, because of what looks like a helmet. In fact it is a rolled cap with a pompom — a rounded hat with turned-up edges, a budajka, not a helmet. One American newspaper (the Times Union of Albany) printed a photograph captioned as if it showed a member of one of the occupying armies of the country's past, which forced the artist to explain himself.

The traffic sign above the manhole is a story of its own. Inattentive drivers have hit the sculpture more than once, most often delivery vans reversing carelessly; after the damage of 1999 a warning sign was put up above it, with a black silhouette of Čumil himself and a plate reading "MAN AT WORK" — a sign about 330 centimetres tall, itself damaged and restored in 2011. The stickers thickly covering it are considered part of the work's tradition rather than damage. The materials are bronze, cast iron, concrete and galvanised sheet; the owner is the Staré Mesto borough; interventions and repairs are recorded for 1999, 2011, 2013 and 2018. Copies of Čumil stand in other cities too, several of them in Russia and Belarus.
