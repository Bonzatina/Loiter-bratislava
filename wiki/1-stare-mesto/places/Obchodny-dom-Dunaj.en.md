# Dunaj Department Store (Obchodný dom Dunaj)

The Dunaj department store (Obchodný dom Dunaj) at Námestie SNP 30 was built in 1936 for the Czech retail chain Brouk a Babka to a 1935 design by architect [[Ludwig-Christian|Christian Ludwig]]. The building is considered **one of the greatest works of Slovak [[functionalism]]**: a ferroconcrete skeleton with a free floor plan yielded seven above-ground and two underground floors of open retail halls — and the whole thing went up in five months: work began on 1 April 1936, finished on 15 August, and on 1 September Slovakia's first department store was already opening to enormous crowds. It was originally named "Bohuslav Brouk" — after the co-owner's younger son (who, however, became a respected psychoanalyst rather than a merchant), later "Brouk a Babka".

![Dunaj Department Store (Obchodný dom Dunaj)](/assets/obchodny-dom-dunaj.jpg)

A communications core with a dual staircase and paired elevators freed up the facade: ribbon windows framed in stainless steel run across its entire width, the walls are clad in ceramic tile, and the perimeter originally carried neon illumination. Three large vitrines on the ground floor form a striking entrance zone. The building consistently employs industrially prefabricated elements — cutting-edge construction technology for 1930s Slovakia.

Jaroslav Brouk, who had founded the firm with Josef Babka back in 1908, brought back from his study trips to the USA and Western Europe novelties unseen in Czechoslovakia: self-service, mail order, sales on credit, pneumatic post, escalators and express lifts. The visionary's fate was bitter: after February 1948 the chain was nationalized, and Brouk himself was sent for "re-education" to the Jáchymov uranium mines, where the camp-broken 69-year-old entrepreneur died in 1953. The store, meanwhile, was renamed "Dunaj" (Danube) — the name the city knows it by; the neon advertising on the facade (hastily finished for the 1958 European figure-skating championships) was for decades the square's nighttime landmark. Since 2000 the building has been a national cultural monument; since 2015 it has been owned by CTP Invest, which prepared a reconstruction designed by Bogle Architects.
