# Paracelsus Memorial Plaque (Pamätná tabuľa Paracelsa)

On the side façade of the [[Primacialny-palac|Primatial Palace]], the one facing Uršulínska street, a bronze plaque hangs between two windows carrying a Latin line: *"In hac platea habitavit A.D. 1537 D.D. Paracelsus von Hohenheim"* — "on this street lived, in the year 1537…". It recalls the few days of September 1537 that Paracelsus spent in Pressburg.

![Paracelsus Memorial Plaque (Pamätná tabuľa Paracelsa)](/assets/tabula-paracelsa.jpg)

Philippus Aureolus Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim (1493–1541), the Swiss physician known as Paracelsus, broke the medical authorities of his day: he treated with minerals and chemical preparations, drew on the experience of military surgery, and held that health binds body, mind and nature together. He is counted among the founders of modern European medicine — and among the fathers of iatrochemistry. In 1537 Pressburg was a notable political and cultural centre of Hungary, and the city received its guest as a recognised scholar, with a formal welcome and a banquet in his honour.

The plaque was commissioned by the city archivist Ovídius Faust and put up in 1937, for the 400th anniversary of the visit. Faust wanted it on the house of the burgher Blasius Beheim, who is said to have hosted Paracelsus, but the owner refused, so it went onto the palace instead — a symbolic tie between the name and the city, since the actual house is unknown. The author was the academic sculptor Ľudovít (Ludwig) Mack, Bratislava-born and active at the turn of the 19th and 20th centuries; his signature "L. Mack" sits in the lower right corner of the inscription panel. The outer frame is cut from natural stone, the inner tablet cast in bronze. The upper part carries a low relief of a physician against the silhouette of Bratislava; the lower part, beneath angel wings, the city's coat of arms, an hourglass and the physicians' emblem. The Secession taste of the period shows in both the composition and the lettering.

## Practical Information

- Address: Primaciálne námestie 2, side façade towards Uršulínska street
- Access: free, from the street
