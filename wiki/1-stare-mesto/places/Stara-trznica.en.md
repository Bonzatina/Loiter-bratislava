# The Old Market Hall and the Bread Market (Stará tržnica)

The Old Market Hall (Stará tržnica) by SNP Square stands where the Bread Market (Brodplatz, Kenyérpiac) bustled for centuries — an irregular pentagon just outside the medieval walls. The historian Tivadar Ortvay described it meticulously at the turn of the 20th century: twelve butchers' stalls, moved here as early as the reign of Louis I, with the bakers' stands behind them; along the western edge ran the town walls and moat, and part of the market lay over the cemetery of St Lawrence's church (sv. Laurinca), used until 1774, where the "national martyr" Ferenc Bónis was buried after being beheaded on the Main Square in 1671 for his part in an anti-Habsburg conspiracy. The press of the day was less lyrical, calling the market a "stinking den of bacilli".

![The Old Market Hall (Stará tržnica)](/assets/stara-trznica.jpg)

The covered market — today's Stará tržnica — was opened by the city in October 1910, set exactly on the site of the demolished walls by the straightened Klobučnícka street. The open-air market only grew after that: in the 1930s an underground sales hall was dug beneath it — precisely on the site of the three-nave medieval church of St Lawrence. After the war the private traders were driven out and the greengrocer's Zelovoc moved underground (in 1950 the newspapers praised the "model service of our nationalized trade"), while a Czechoslovak Television studio moved into the hall itself; then the vegetables moved to kiosks on Kamenné Square, the underground entrances were covered with concrete slabs, and trees and a fountain took the market's place. The spot where housewives had bought fresh goods for centuries was gradually forgotten — until the market hall became a market and civic venue once more.
