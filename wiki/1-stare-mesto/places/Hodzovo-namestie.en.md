# Hodža Square (Hodžovo námestie)

Hodža Square (Hodžovo námestie) is one of the city's best-known squares, a transport hub on the northern edge of the Old Town where trolleybus and bus routes fan out in front of the [[Grasalkovicov-palac|Grassalkovich Palace]] — the residence of the President of Slovakia. In the socialist era it was called Peace Square (Mierové námestie), and older Bratislavans still say "Mierko"; on 1 April 1991 the square was renamed after the national awakener Michal Miloslav Hodža (1811–1870). Besides the palace it is framed by the Crowne Plaza hotel, Tatracentrum and the new Astoria Palace.

![Hodža Square: the Planet of Peace fountain and Tatracentrum (Hodžovo námestie)](/assets/hodzovo-namestie.jpg)

In front of the palace stands the [[Fontana-Planeta-mieru|Planet of Peace Fountain (Fontána Planéta mieru)]] by Tibor Bártfay — the three-metre steel sphere that the [[solar-system-model|"Strolls through the Solar System"]] project adopted as its Sun: all the model's planets are measured from here.

Beneath the square runs a pedestrian underpass — dating, by different accounts, from 1978 or 1981 — reconstructed in 2008; since 2021 it bears the name "Underpass of the Students of 16 November 1989" (Podchod študentov 16. novembra 1989), commemorating the student march on the eve of the Velvet Revolution. For a geologist the underpass is a small stone museum: the entrance from the fountain side is clad in coarse-grained pinkish Liberec granite — a porphyritic biotite granite of the Krkonoše-Jizera pluton over 300 million years old; one of the lower stair flights was dressed during the reconstruction in the finer-grained Sardinian granite Rosa Beta, and the dark floor slabs are "Bubovice syenite", petrographically a biotite-amphibole gabbrodiorite of the Central Bohemian pluton from near Březnice (~345 million years). Before the reconstruction the walls were covered with creamy Bulgarian Vračan limestone and light Silesian granite — now replaced by ceramic tiles.
