# The P. O. Hviezdoslav Theatre — DPOH (Divadlo P. O. Hviezdoslava)

The Pavol Országh Hviezdoslav Theatre (Divadlo P. O. Hviezdoslava, DPOH) on Laurinská street is a rare case of a drama stage born inside a bank. The Slovak National Theatre had lacked venues since its founding in 1920, and in 1941 the Ministry of Education asked the Slovak National Bank to include a theatre hall in the design of its new headquarters. The competition was won by architect Eugen Kramár, who invited Štefan Lukačovič as co-author; construction began at the end of 1942, but the war — including a bomb hit — halted the works. After the 1948 coup the design was "corrected" in the spirit of the age: Janko Alexy's stained glass depicted the building of socialism, and František Gajdoš's monumental frescoes drew folk traditions closer to the revolutionary ones.

![The P. O. Hviezdoslav Theatre on Laurinská](/assets/dpoh.jpg)

The first performance was played on 28 May 1955. In 1969 the hall's socialist-realist frescoes were covered by an acoustic panel wall, and in 1981–1983 the interiors were comprehensively rebuilt in a postmodern spirit. In 2007 the SND drama company moved to the new building by the Danube, and the city bought the house on Laurinská — it now hosts the P. O. Hviezdoslav City Theatre, whose repertoire includes plays drawn from Bratislava life. The building attracts not only theatre-goers but lovers of architecture — a layer cake of interwar design, socialist realism and postmodernism.
