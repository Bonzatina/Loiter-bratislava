# The Pentagon

The Pentagon is the entrenched nickname of the residential block at Stavbárska 34–42 in [[2-vrakuna|Vrakuňa]]: 450 flats built in 1970–1972 to a design by the well-known architects Štefan Svetko and Štefan Ďurkovič. Bratislava's grimmest address — and at the same time a curious specimen of late-modernist housing: unlike its Washington namesake, the name here comes not from the shape but from the **five blocks** that make up the complex.

![The Pentagon](/assets/pentagon-vrakuna.jpg)

The building is laid out as an open semi-arc, enclosing an atrium with a park, separated from the main road by a gentle slope. It began as a dormitory for the builders of the neighbouring Medzi jarkami estate — hence the name of Stavbárska ("Builders'") street; the one- and two-room studio flats were later handed out as social housing. That is how the environment formed which made the house notorious as a refuge of criminals and drug dealers, and media fame only cemented the image of the Pentagon and its surroundings as the city's bogeyman — an image that rubs off on the nearby [[UFO-socha|UFO sculpture]] as well.

The healing is slow, but it is happening. Since June 2004 the entrance at Stavbárska 38 has housed MIXklub, a low-threshold centre for children and teenagers — a safe space for those aged 6–18 growing up amid elevated crime. In 2013 an urban study for the revitalisation of the "Pentagon residential complex" was published. And in 2019 the two most troubled entrances — Stavbárska 40 and 42 — underwent a complete renovation: insulation, new lifts, "anti-vandal" doors, chip access and cameras cut off intruders; the remaining blocks are expected to follow.

By the mid-2020s the turnaround became visible: Vrakuňa's mayor Martin Kuruc speaks of a place that "is becoming safer, cleaner and more dignified to live in" — the result of years of joint work by the state and city police, the city hall and the councillors — and residents answer with dozens of grateful comments about the transformed parks and playgrounds. Neither the authorities nor the neighbours consider the Pentagon a symbol of fear any more, though some of the problems, locals admit, have merely moved to nearby streets: drug crime remains with the police, homelessness with the social services.
