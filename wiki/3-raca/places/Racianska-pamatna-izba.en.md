# Rača Memorial Room (Račianska pamätná izba)

The Rača Memorial Room (Račianska pamätná izba) is [[3-raca|Rača]]'s local-history museum in the Community House on Alstrova, the village's oldest street near the square: one half of the exhibition documents the winegrowing tradition, the other the ethnography and daily life of past generations. The chief relic is a **gold-embroidered liturgical vestment given by Maria Theresa to the Rača priest Radochányi** in thanks for a barrel of Rača Frankovka, to which the empress credited her recovery in 1767.

The place itself is half a millennium older than the museum: a chapel of a count's curia stood here as early as the 15th century. At the end of the 16th century the Kolonich curia was famed all the way to Pressburg — Count Siegfried Kolonich invited the evangelical preacher Ondrej Reis, whose sermons drew Lutherans from the whole region; the curia later belonged to the Keglevič and Erdődy families, and in 1756 Countess Terézia Erdődy donated the chapel with two vineyards to the Catholic parish. In 1935 the decayed chapel was bought by the village, and the modern Community House rose in its place; from 1956 it housed the district maternity ward (which older visitors still recall), then a health centre and a police station.

The Room's own biography has its drama: the first "Račianska izba" was opened by the local branch of Matica slovenská on 13 April 1975 in an old winegrower's house at Alstrova 86 — with plans to grow it into a whole "Rača skanzen" — but in 1979 the normalisation regime dissolved the branch, the Room perished, and its exhibits went to the City Museum's depository (a new family house now stands on the old site). After the Velvet Revolution the revived Matica spent years fighting for the Room's return, and in 1998 received a wing of the reconstructed Community House — together with the winegrowers' society. The Room lives by festivals: most guests come during Rača's hody and vinobranie, and year-round it serves schoolchildren as a living lesson in local history.
