# Statue of St Andrew (Socha sv. Ondreja)

The statue of St Andrew (Socha sv. Ondreja) in the park at the corner of Miletičova and Záhradnícka is a baroque migrant from the old town: for almost two centuries the saint stood in a niche on the corner of a large city building at the crossing of Štúrova and Gorkého streets, until the house was slated for demolition to make way for the planned seat of the Slovak National Bank. The statue was taken down already in the late 1930s, ahead of the coming demolition; the house itself was pulled down in 1941. In 1942 the statue was restored at the city's expense and set on a new pedestal in the Ružinov park.

![Statue of St Andrew (Socha sv. Ondreja)](/assets/socha-svateho-ondreja.jpg)

The figure is carved from sandstone: an old man in a draped robe holds with his right hand a diagonal cross in the shape of the letter "X" — his attribute (by tradition, the apostle Andrew was crucified in Patras on just such a cross, tied to it rather than nailed). The prismatic pedestal carries a cartouche with a stylized coat of arms of Bratislava at the top, and below it a plaque with a Latin inscription: "Hac statva sancti apostoli Andreae olim in platea eivsdem nominis A.D. 1712 imposita expensis civitatis A.D. 1942 erat restavrata".

The inscription dates the statue to 1712, while the literature gives 1742 — the truth, by different accounts, lies between them. The same inscription keeps the memory of the original address: in the 18th century the crossing where the saint stood was called St Andrew's street — today's Gorkého. The statue was last restored in 2009 and returned to the park; it is listed in Bratislava's city register of memorable objects (category 1A, no. RUZ-1A-24).
