# Cvernovka

Cvernovka (from *cverna*, "thread") is the popular name of Bratislava's thread mill, whose second works occupied the block between Svätoplukova, Páričkova and Košická streets in Mlynské Nivy. For a decade and a half after production stopped these halls were the city's chief unofficial cultural quarter — and then all but vanished from the map.

![Cvernovka spinning mill (Pradiareň)](/assets/cvernovka-pradiaren.jpg)

The Viennese entrepreneurs Salcher and Richter opened a thread and textile shop in the city in 1901, and on 17 January 1902, now backed by capital from the English thread house Coats, started production — the first thread factory in Hungary. It made cotton threads, later woollen ones on cardboard spools. After Austria-Hungary broke up the mill traded as Bratislavská cvernová továreň, exported to at least six countries, added embroidery, crochet and darning yarns, and in 1934 became one of the first firms in Central Europe to put a multi-coloured range of sewing thread on the market. It stayed in English hands until the nationalisation of 1948–1949, then went through several names — the International Women's Day Works (Závody Medzinárodného dňa žien, 1957–1990), BCT again from 1991. Bankruptcy in 2002 and the last machines stopping in 2004 closed the story. The halls had been built by the Viennese-Bratislavan contractor Pittel & Brausewetter, whose work is scattered across the city — from the Dynamitka chemical works to the Stollwerck factory.

The new owner began renting the empty halls out, and in 2006–2007 artists, designers, architects and video-makers gathered in the main spinning-mill building, the Pradiareň — no other house in Bratislava held so many of them. What began as a closed circle turned into an open centre: the Oskár Čepan Prize and the National Design Prize were awarded here, art academy students defended their diplomas, city markets and May Day celebrations filled the yard, and the Alexis bookshop — whose interior won the CE.ZA.AR award and made international "most beautiful bookshops" lists — traded here. In May 2012 the Ružinov building office banned all public events: the premises were registered as a warehouse and had never been approved for any other use. That same year the site was bought by the French developer JF Hamilton, which started demolition on day one without a permit; the work was stopped, but two buildings — the 1903 dye house and the 1912 boiler house — had been destabilised badly enough that they had to come down.

At the end of 2015 the Finnish developer YIT acquired the site. By 30 April 2016 the tenants of the "storey building", a 1964 annex to the Pradiareň, were served notice; in response four artists from the spinning mill next door — Viliam Csino, Boris Meluš, Branislav Čavoj and Šymon Kliman — founded the Cvernovka Foundation. The farewell concert Derniéra on 4 March 2016 drew over five hundred people, a collective objection to the zoning plan demanding that the Pradiareň keep its cultural function gathered more than two thousand signatures, and buying the building was even discussed — none of it helped: from 1 July 2016 the Pradiareň tenants were evicted too. With the foundation and the Bratislava Self-Governing Region behind them, the community moved to Račianska street and founded [[Nova-Cvernovka|Nová Cvernovka]].

YIT then ran an internal architectural competition (won by the Compass studio) and began building the Zwirn quarter. Three structures survive from the whole complex: the 1903 spinning mill, a national cultural monument; the 1904 power house; and the 1961 hostel, converted into the Pari residential building. The spinning mill and power house were reconstructed into an industrial-office complex under the marketing name Pradiareň 1900.

## Practical Information

- Address: Svätoplukova 2A, Bratislava
- Access: Pradiareň 1900 is an office building with a publicly accessible ground floor; the Zwirn quarter is going up around it
