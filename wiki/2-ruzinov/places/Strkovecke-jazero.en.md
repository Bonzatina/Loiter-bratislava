# Štrkovec Lake (Štrkovecké jazero)

Štrkovec Lake (Štrkovecké jazero) is the main body of water of [[2-ruzinov|Ružinov]] and a valued element of the district's urban fabric: a lake of 56,000 m² in the middle of the panel-built Štrkovec quarter, around which all local life revolves — from morning runs to fishing. It is a typical Bratislava "štrkovisko" — a lake left behind by gravel extraction.

![Štrkovec Lake (Štrkovecké jazero)](/assets/strkovecke-jazero.jpg)

As late as the 1950s the Mlynské rameno arm of the Little Danube reached here through Vrakuňa; when the arm gave way to the housing estate, gravel and sand for the construction sites began to be dredged from the remaining water. A trace of that extraction is the paradox of the depths: the lake (2.5–8 m) is shallowest in the centre, where the dredger stood, digging around itself. Until 1989 the water was clean and the lake served Štrkovec residents as a natural swimming spot; by the early 1990s it had fallen into such neglect — dirty water, unkempt banks — that filling it in entirely was seriously discussed. Today it is the opposite: a cinder running track loops the lake, there are playgrounds by the water, and the lake itself is a carp-water fishing ground (carp, pike, zander, eel). The lake lies on the main migration route of waterfowl along the Danube and serves them as a resting and feeding stop.

In the 1970s–1980s the banks of Štrkovec were reshaped by the sculptor [[Miklanek-Rastislav|Rastislav Miklánek]] with the landscape architect Alfonz Torma — this is where his famous concrete camel comes from, a beloved climbing frame for generations of Ružinov children. Štrkovec is also home to the [[Sportova-hala-Pasienky|Pasienky Sports Hall]] with its 1962 cable roof.
