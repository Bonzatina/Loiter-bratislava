# Miletičova Market (Trhovisko Miletičova)

Miletičova Market (Trhovisko Miletičova), for locals simply "Miletička", is Bratislava's best-known open-air market, its name familiar even beyond the city. Everything is sold here: flowers, vegetables and fruit from local growers, homemade products, crafts and cheap clothing — the latter has even entered the language: the mocking "dresses like from Miletička" (oblieka sa ako z Miletičky) became a city idiom. The market is famous above all for its atmosphere — the colours, the smells of cooked food and the people on both sides of the stall; in recent years the main entrance got a restored sign, renewed stalls and a stage hosting food festivals, fairs and seasonal celebrations.

![Miletičova Market (Trhovisko Miletičova)](/assets/trhovisko-mileticova.jpg)

The market lives on the grounds of the former city slaughterhouse (bitúnok) — a notable chapter of Bratislava's food industry. Meat-processing facilities stood on Miletičova street as early as 1884, and in 1924 a new modern complex was built here: several halls, each with its own function — slaughter and processing, refrigeration rooms, spaces for sick animals and rendering, rooms for the workers. Despite some historicizing details, the slaughterhouse architecture — especially the interiors — looks decidedly modernist; the construction is attributed to the firm Pittel a Brausewetter. The complex's landmarks, the tower and the chimney, still stand and are visible right from the market rows. In the 1970s the market moved onto the slaughterhouse grounds — and that is where the story of "Miletička" begins.

The street that gave the market its name honours Svetozár Miletić — a Serbian writer and politician from Vojvodina, a student of Ľudovít Štúr: around 1868 he defended the rights of Slovaks and their language in the Hungarian Parliament. The irony is that the serious politician's name became colloquial shorthand for cheap fashion. On the same Miletičova street operates the [[Saleziani-Mileticova|Salesian house]] — one of the three Bratislava houses of the Salesians of Don Bosco (Saleziáni Don Bosca), the order founded in 1859 by Giovanni Bosco and devoted to the education of youth: the Salesians have worked in Slovakia since 1924, their Ružinov house appeared as early as 1933, survived the 1950 liquidation of religious orders ("Action K") and was revived after 1989.
