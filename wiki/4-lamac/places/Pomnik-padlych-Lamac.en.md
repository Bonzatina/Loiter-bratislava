# Monument to the Fallen in Lamač (Pomník padlých v Lamači)

The monument to those who fell in the First and Second World Wars stands in the grounds of the [[Kostol-svatej-Margity|Church of St Margaret]] on Vrančovičova street — a work by [[Rigele-Alojz|Alojz Rigele]], unveiled on 2 September 1928 and listed among Bratislava's immovable cultural monuments under number 675/0.

![Monument to the Fallen in Lamač (Pomník padlých v Lamači)](/assets/pomnik-padlych-lamac.jpg)

The monument is architecturally composed, as a free-standing object: a figural composition sits on top of a prismatic pillar, memorial tablets with the names and portraits of the war's victims run down its sides, and on the front are a relief of the Slovak coat of arms and a likeness of Milan Rastislav Štefánik, with a relief of a sword and a lime branch below his tablet. The back carries a tablet about the monument's making and a later-added tablet of the Second World War's victims. The lower part is a stepped square pedestal with trough-shaped flower containers projecting from its sides — sculpted as an inseparable part of the whole.

The figural scene itself is carved in the round: a standing Christ supports a dying soldier. Christ wears a long cloak falling to his feet, and the sculptor's emphasis falls above all on the expression of face and hands. The soldier in Christ's arms kneels on one knee, in a First World War uniform, his head in a helmet, his left hand holding a rifle and his right holding Christ's hand: the pain and suffering caused by war. At the soldier's feet lies a felled lime tree with young shoots and leaves — the lime as a symbol of the Slavs and of patriotism, and the cut tree sprouting again as a metaphor for the Slovak nation after the First World War.

By the restoration of 2008 the monument was covered with an extensive growth of microflora that had practically changed the natural stone's original colour, and with a fine carbon deposit; the sandstone had degraded in places — losses, crusts, washed-out material — the marble tablets were cracked, and worst of all was the pedestal: two cracks two millimetres wide had tilted the sculpture. The restorer Pavol Ševčík cleaned the sculpture and the pedestal, made good the losses with Hořice sandstone, desalinated it (with compresses of cellulose and demineralised water), consolidated and hydrophobised the stone, restored the soldier's missing depiction, filled and retouched the cracks and secured the pedestal structurally, facing it with 6 cm slabs of Hořice sandstone. The work cost €19,700.
