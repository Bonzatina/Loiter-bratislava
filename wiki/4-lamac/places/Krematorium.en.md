# Crematorium and Urn Grove (Krematórium a urnový háj)

The Bratislava crematorium and urn grove (Krematórium a urnový háj) on the Hodonín road at the foot of the Little Carpathians is the peak of Slovak post-war architecture and the principal work of [[Milucky-Ferdinand|Ferdinand Milučký]] (designed 1962–1963, built 1967–1969; landscape by Vladimír Fašang). The idea is a Scandinavian-spirited modernism dissolved into the forest: long lengthwise walls demarcate the spaces, the transverse walls are entirely of glass, and from the funeral hall mourners look not at a wall but into the crowns of mature trees. A long ascending arc of a pathway leads to the hall through sparse woodland; the technical facilities are hidden underground.

![Milučký's crematorium in the Little Carpathians forest](/assets/krematorium.jpg)

Sculpture completes the architecture: Vladimír Kompánek's totem on the main meadow, Rudolf Uher's travertine piece, Tibor Tóth's figure *Sorrow* in the urn grove. Many of Slovakia's notable personalities are buried in the grove — including, by his own wish, Milučký himself. Since 2003 the complex has been a national cultural monument; it is regularly named one of the finest Slovak buildings of the 20th century.
