# The Well of the False Monks (Studňa falošných mníchov)

One of Lamač's most curious stories is that of the well of the false monks (studňa falošných mníchov). In June 1866 two mysterious monks appeared in the village, claiming to be from Prague: they bought provisions from the locals, kept to themselves, and spent their time measuring and taking notes on the hills and paths. Villagers who followed them discovered, in a copse below the Podhájie grove where the hills run down to the Záhorie plain, a neat well beautifully lined with stone.

In mid-July the monks vanished — and a week later, on 21–22 July 1866, the last battle of the Austro-Prussian War thundered at Lamač. Only then did the villagers understand who the "brothers" had been: Prussian officers of General Julius von Bose, scouting the terrain in habits (and securing water for the advancing troops). Furious at the deception, the people of Lamač tore down the strangers' hut, filled in the well and ploughed the spot over so that no one would ever find it again — which is why its exact location is unknown to this day, and this story has no marker on the map.
